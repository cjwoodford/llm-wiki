# Computation vs. Nature & The Observer-Relative Fallacy

## 1. Thesis & Definition

**Computation vs. Nature and the Observer-Relative Fallacy** identifies the fundamental metaphysical and linguistic confusion at the heart of the [[Machine Metaphor|Computational Theory of Mind]]: the false belief that **computation is an intrinsic physical feature discovered in nature**, rather than an **observer-relative mathematical description projected onto physical systems by conscious human minds**.

While historical science compared the brain to a steam engine or telegraph as an analogy, modern computationalism erased its own metaphorical status, claiming the brain literally *is* a computer.

### 1. John Searle: "Physics Has No Syntax"
- **Intrinsic vs. Observer-Relative Properties**:
  - *Intrinsic*: Mass, charge, voltage, gravitational attraction. (These exist regardless of whether humans exist).
  - *Observer-Relative*: Money, borders, marriage, chess games, and **computation**.
- **The Transistor Illusion**: A silicon gate does not "know" it is running an algorithm or optimizing loss; it is merely undergoing electromagnetic state transitions. It only becomes a computation when a conscious human designer interprets 5V as "1" and 0V as "0". To say the brain is intrinsically a computer is a category error.

### 2. Roger Penrose: The Gödelian Blind Spot
- In *The Emperor's New Mind* (1989) and *Shadows of the Mind* (1994), Penrose applied **Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem**:
  1. Any consistent formal computational algorithm contains mathematical truths ($G$) that the algorithm itself cannot prove.
  2. A conscious human mathematician, viewing the system from outside, can recognize that $G$ is true through conscious insight.
  3. Therefore, **human understanding is not an algorithm** and cannot be simulated by a deterministic or probabilistic Turing machine.

### 3. The Circularity of Information Theories of Mind (Searle on IIT & Koch)

A major contemporary extension of the computational fallacy occurs in **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)** (Giulio Tononi, Christof Koch), which asserts that consciousness is identical to integrated, differentiated information (quantified as $\Phi$). Under IIT, any physical mechanism with non-zero integrated states—such as a smartphone, an electrical circuit, or a photodiode—is minimally conscious (informational panpsychism).

In his critique (*Can Information Theory Explain Consciousness?*, 2013), [[John Searle|John Searle]] exposes the fatal circularity of this thesis:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             SEARLE'S CIRCULARITY CRITIQUE OF INFORMATION THEORIES      │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Consciousness is **Observer-Independent**: It exists in a subject   │
│    regardless of what any external observer thinks or interprets.      │
│ 2. Information (Shannon bits, syntax, tree rings, photodiodes) is      │
│    **Observer-Relative**: It only exists when interpreted by a mind.   │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Conclusion: You cannot explain consciousness by appealing to           │
│             information, because observer-relative information         │
│             presupposes the existence of consciousness already.        │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **The Photodiode and the Mercury Thermometer**: A photodiode that turns on in light and off in darkness has no subjective awareness of light; it merely undergoes a physical reaction to photons. It only becomes "information" when an external conscious observer uses it as a measurement tool.
- **The Tree-Ring Fallacy**: The annual growth rings of a tree stump correlate with seasonal cycles, but they only constitute "information about age" when a conscious forester interprets them. The tree ring in itself is just physical wood.
- **The Unit Problem of Panpsychism**: Consciousness is experienced as a unified, bounded qualitative field. Informational panpsychism cannot define where consciousness begins or ends: if an iPhone is conscious, is each transistor conscious? Each atom? Or the entire global internet?

### 4. Strategic Ambiguity & The Crosswired Vocabulary

A major historical and linguistic mechanism perpetuating the computational fallacy is what Carnegie Mellon historians Christopher Phillips and Alison Langmead (2026) term **"Strategic Ambiguity"**:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE DUAL REGISTERS OF AI RHETORIC                    │
├──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Register                 │ Operational Meaning                         │
├──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Narrow Technical Sense│ • "Learning" = Parameter loss optimization. │
│    (For Computer         │ • "Attention" = Weighted matrix vectors.    │
│     Scientists)          │ • "Thinking" = Executing instruction sets.  │
├──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Rich Anthropomorphic  │ • Evokes human consciousness, lived         │
│    Sense (For the Public │   experience, intentionality, and moral     │
│    & Media)              │   understanding.                            │
└──────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **The Historical Precedent (1950s–1960s)**: From Norbert Wiener onward, cyberneticians and AI pioneers deliberately adopted psychological terms (*"learning"*, *"memory"*, *"decision"*) to describe deterministic feedback loops. This strategic ambiguity allowed complex mathematics to be communicated easily across disciplines, but created the false impression that computers were beginning to duplicate human cognition.
### 5. Simulation Is Not Instantiation (Anil Seth & Tim van Gelder)

In *"The Mythology of Conscious AI"* (2026), neuroscientist [[Anil Seth]] identifies a foundational non sequitur underpinning contemporary AI computationalism: **confusing computational simulation with physical instantiation**.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│              SETH'S CRITIQUE: SIMULATION VS. INSTANTIATION             │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • A computational simulation of a rainstorm does not make anything wet.│
│ • A computational simulation of digestion does not digest food.        │
│ • A computational simulation of a black hole produces no gravity.      │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Conclusion: Simulating the informational routing of the human brain    │
│             does not instantiate consciousness unless one already      │
│             dogmatically assumes that consciousness is purely abstract │
│             computation.                                               │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **The Category Error of the Watt Governor (Tim van Gelder)**:
  - Cognitive scientist Tim van Gelder (*What Might Cognition Be, If Not Computation?*, 1995) pointed to the centrifugal **Watt Governor** of a steam engine. As the engine speeds up, cantilevered metal balls swing outwards, mechanically closing a valve to reduce steam flow.
  - The governor regulates the engine through continuous physical dynamics, gravity, and mechanics. Describing the governor as a "computer" that senses speed, computes difference metrics, and executes actuation commands is a total misunderstanding of physical reality.
  - Biological nervous systems are like Watt Governors—deeply embodied, continuous dynamical and thermodynamic engines—not Turing machines manipulating discrete symbol strings.
- **Refutation of the Bostrom Simulation Argument**: Nick Bostrom’s influential simulation hypothesis takes as an unexamined premise that *"a computer running a suitable program would be conscious."* If computational functionalism is false—because simulation cannot instantiate the biological physics of consciousness—the undeniable reality of human conscious experience proves that base reality is not a digital simulation.

## 6. Why the Computational Metaphor is Uniquely Pernicious

1. **The Speed & Scale Illusion**: High-speed switching across billions of nanometer transistors triggers human **Hyperactive Agency Detection**, mistaking rapid statistical retrieval for an inner subjective presence.
2. **The "Crosswired" Vocabulary (Floridi & Nobre)**: Computer science borrowed human psychological terms (*Memory, Attention, Learning, Global Workspace*), while cognitive science borrowed computational terms (*Buffers, Processing, Encoding*), rendering the circular projection invisible.
3. **The Gnostic Longing**: Flatters modern technocracy by treating the biological body as disposable "meatware" and death as a downloadable code problem ([[Techgnosticism|Techgnosticism]]).

# The 1956 Foundations of AI and Cognitive Science

## 1. Thesis & Definition

In the late summer and early fall of 1956, two back-to-back gatherings in New England—the **[[The 1956 Foundations of AI and Cognitive Science#The Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence (June–August 1956)|Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence]]** and the **[[The 1956 Foundations of AI and Cognitive Science#The MIT Symposium on Information Theory (September 10–12, 1956)|MIT Symposium on Information Theory]]**—laid the conceptual, technical, and institutional foundations for both **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** and **Cognitive Science**.

Together, these twin events ignited the **"Cognitive Revolution"** by establishing computation, formal symbol manipulation, and information processing as the primary paradigm for understanding intelligence in both physical machines and biological minds.

│               THE 1956 DUAL CONVERGENCE IN NEW ENGLAND                 │
├────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┤

│                                    │   - Miller (7 ± 2 Chunks / Memory)│

## 2. The Two Landmark Events of 1956

### A. The Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence (June–August 1956)
- **Location & Duration**: Dartmouth College, Hanover, New Hampshire; an eight-week workshop conceived in 1955 and executed over the summer of 1956.
- **Founding Organizers**: [[John McCarthy|John McCarthy]] (Dartmouth), [[Marvin Minsky|Marvin Minsky]] (Harvard/MIT), [[Claude Shannon|Claude Shannon]] (Bell Labs), and [[Nathaniel Rochester|Nathaniel Rochester]] (IBM).
- **Core Purpose & Christening**: Conceived to explore how machines could simulate aspects of human learning, reasoning, abstraction, and problem-solving. In the original 1955 funding proposal to the Rockefeller Foundation, [[John McCarthy|McCarthy]] deliberately coined the phrase **"Artificial Intelligence"** to differentiate the new symbolic computational paradigm from [[Norbert Wiener|Norbert Wiener's]] [[Cybernetics and the Macy Conferences|cybernetics]] and secure independent institutional footing.
- **Historical Significance**: Served as the official birthplace and christening event of Artificial Intelligence as an autonomous academic discipline.

### B. The MIT Symposium on Information Theory (September 10–12, 1956)
- **Location & Scope**: Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Cambridge, Massachusetts; sponsored by the IRE (Institute of Radio Engineers) Professional Group on Information Theory.
- **Interdisciplinary Cross-Pollination**: Gathered mathematicians, computer engineers, neurophysiologists, linguists, and experimental psychologists in the same lecture hall.
- **The "Birthday of Cognitive Science"**: Cognitive psychologist [[George Miller|George A. Miller]] famously designated **September 11, 1956**, as the exact birthdate of Cognitive Science due to three groundbreaking papers delivered consecutively on that single day, each applying formal information-processing models to dismantle behaviorism.

## 3. The Three Breakthrough Papers at MIT (September 11, 1956)

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             THE HISTORIC TRIO OF PAPERS (SEPTEMBER 11, 1956)           │
├──────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Paper & Authors              │ Core Breakthrough & Epistemic Shift     │
├──────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **The Logic Theory Machine** │ • First running symbolic AI program     │
│ [[Allen Newell]] &           │ • Proved 38 of 52 *Principia* theorems  │
│ [[Herbert Simon]] (w/ Shaw)  │ • Heuristic search trees replace brute- │
│                              │   force calculation                     │
├──────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Three Models for the       │ • Introduced formal generative grammars │
│ Description of Language**    │ • Refuted Markovian sequence-matching   │
│ [[Noam Chomsky]]             │ • Theoretical basis for innate syntax   │
├──────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **The Magical Number Seven,  │ • Human STM framed as limited channel   │
│ Plus or Minus Two**          │ • $7 \pm 2$ chunks of internal storage   │
│ [[George Miller]]            │ • Quantified internal mental capacity   │
└──────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Allen Newell & Herbert A. Simon — *The Logic Theory Machine*
- **The Demonstration**: Demonstrated the **Logic Theorist** (developed in collaboration with RAND programmer J.C. Shaw), widely recognized as the first running symbolic artificial intelligence program.
- **Whitehead & Russell Feat**: The program proved 38 of the first 52 theorems in Alfred North Whitehead and Bertrand Russell's *Principia Mathematica*. For Theorem 2.85, it derived a shorter, more elegant proof than Whitehead and Russell had produced by hand.
- **Heuristic Search Trees**: Introduced **heuristic search** to prune combinatorial explosion, demonstrating that computers could manipulate non-numeric, abstract conceptual symbols rather than merely crunch arithmetic.
- **Technical Innovation**: Led directly to the creation of Information Processing Language (IPL), inventing dynamic memory allocation and linked lists (the precursor to [[John McCarthy|McCarthy]]'s Lisp).

### 2. Noam Chomsky — *Three Models for the Description of Language*
- **Formal Generative Grammars**: Introduced mathematical and formal generative models of syntax (the foundation of the Chomsky Hierarchy), classifying languages by algorithmic complexity.
- **The Defeat of Behaviorist Linguistics**: Proved that human natural language syntax cannot be modeled as a finite-state Markov chain or stimulus-response conditioning. Sentences possess recursive, hierarchical phrase-structure rules ($S \rightarrow NP + VP$).
- **Innate Cognitive Architecture**: Laid the theoretical foundation for Universal Grammar and innate computational structures in the human mind, culminating in his devastating 1959 critique of B.F. Skinner's *Verbal Behavior*.

### 3. George A. Miller — *The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two*
- **Information Channel Capacity**: Applied [[Claude Shannon|Claude Shannon]]'s Information Theory to human psychophysics, modeling human short-term working memory as a discrete communication channel with a strict capacity bottleneck.
- **Chunking & Internal Representations**: Demonstrated that human immediate recall is constrained not by raw information bits, but by roughly **$7 \pm 2$ discrete cognitive "chunks"** (meaningful symbol units).
- **Direct Challenge to Behaviorism**: Provided measurable, quantifiable proof of internal mental storage and recoding operations, disproving the behaviorist claim that internal mental states are unscientific "black boxes."

## 4. Key Players & Their Dual Roles

The architects of 1956 were unique polymaths who refused to recognize an institutional barrier between computer engineering and human psychology:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE POLYMATHIC FOUNDERS OF THE PARADIGM                │
├────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Pioneer            │ Foundational Dual Role / Legacy                   │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [[John McCarthy]]  │ Coined "Artificial Intelligence" (1955/56);       │
│                    │ created Lisp; pioneer of time-sharing systems.    │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [[Marvin Minsky]]  │ Co-organizer of Dartmouth; MIT AI Lab founder;    │
│                    │ framed AI as synthetic mental architecture.       │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [[Claude Shannon]] │ Father of Information Theory; bits, bandwidth,    │
│                    │ channel capacity as universal language of mind.   │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [[Allen Newell]] & │ Bridged CS (Turing Award) & Psychology (APA);     │
│ [[Herbert Simon]]  │ formulated the Physical Symbol System Hypothesis. │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [[Nathaniel        │ Co-organizer of Dartmouth; chief architect of     │
│ Rochester]]        │ IBM 701; anchored AI to digital hardware.         │
└────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **[[John McCarthy|John McCarthy]]**: Conceived the Dartmouth workshop proposal, coined "Artificial Intelligence" to break from Wiener's cybernetics, and later invented **Lisp** (1958), the foundational programming language of symbolic AI.
- **[[Marvin Minsky|Marvin Minsky]]**: Co-founder of the MIT AI Laboratory, author of *The Society of Mind*, and pioneer who treated AI as the engineering of synthetic mental architectures and human psychology as biological software design.
- **[[Claude Shannon|Claude Shannon]]**: Developed mathematical Information Theory (1948); his definitions of entropy, channel capacity, bandwidth, and bits provided the shared quantitative language unifying digital computer engineering, telecommunications, and neural rate coding.
- **[[Allen Newell|Allen Newell]] & [[Herbert Simon|Herbert A. Simon]]**: Polymaths who won both the ACM Turing Award (1975) and the APA Distinguished Scientific Contribution Award. They formulated the **Physical Symbol System Hypothesis (PSSH)**—the thesis that a physical symbol system has the necessary and sufficient means for general intelligent action, equating human thought and computer execution as two instances of symbolic processing.
- **[[Nathaniel Rochester|Nathaniel Rochester]]**: Dartmouth co-organizer and chief architect of the IBM 701 (IBM's first commercial scientific computer); designed the first symbolic assembler and conducted early digital simulations of Hebbian neural networks.

## 5. Core Takeaways & Lasting Influence

### 1. The Overthrow of Behaviorism
Before 1956, Anglo-American psychology was dominated by B.F. Skinner's radical behaviorism, which barred internal mental states (intentions, concepts, memories) as unobservable, unscientific constructs. 
The 1956 convergence dismantled behaviorism by showing that internal, rule-governed mental processes could be:
- **Rigorously measured** in human performance as discrete information channels ([[George Miller|Miller]]).
- **Modeled formally** as mathematical syntactic generative systems ([[Noam Chomsky|Chomsky]]).
- **Physically executed** on electronic digital computers ([[Allen Newell|Newell]] & [[Herbert Simon|Simon]]).

### 2. The "Software / Hardware" Split (Functionalism & Multiple Realizability)
The 1956 breakthrough established the foundational philosophical premise that cognitive operations are computational algorithms independent of their physical medium. This paved the way directly for:
- **Machine State Functionalism**: Formulated by [[Hilary Putnam|Hilary Putnam]] (1960), arguing that mental states are functional states of a Turing machine.
- **The Representational Theory of Mind & LOT**: Formulated by [[Jerry Fodor|Jerry Fodor]] (1975), proposing that cognition is syntactic computation over an internal Language of Thought (Mentalese).
- **Multiple Realizability**: The premise that intelligence can be realized in biological wetware, silicon chips, or any physical symbol-manipulating substrate.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE FUNCTIONALIST SPLIT FROM 1956                    │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Mind / Software:    Algorithms, Symbol Manipulation, Heuristics, Syntax│
│ ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── │
│ Body / Hardware:    Biological Brain (Wetware) OR Silicon Transistors  │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 3. Institutional Formalization: CS Departments & The Sloan Hexagon
The momentum from 1956 drove the creation of dedicated institutional structures:
- **Standalone Computer Science Departments**: Led to the founding of the earliest computer science departments in the 1960s (Purdue in 1962, Stanford in 1965 under George Forsythe and Louis Fein).
- **The Sloan Hexagon (1978)**: In the late 1970s, the [[Alfred P. Sloan Foundation|Alfred P. Sloan Foundation]] codified the 1956 convergence by investing $17.4 million into Cognitive Science, structuring the field around a 6-discipline matrix (**Psychology, Computer Science, Neuroscience, Linguistics, Philosophy, and Anthropology**) unified by the core doctrine: **cognition is information processing**.

### 4. The Mind-Computer Feedback Loop & Conceptual Borrowing
The 1956 paradigm shift established an enduring circular feedback loop:
- Computer scientists modeled machine architectures on human mental faculties (*memory buffers, attention, search trees, learning rules, neural networks*).
- Cognitive scientists modeled the biological brain on computing hardware (*encoding, storage, clock cycles, central processors, prediction error codecs*).
- Over decades, this generated **[[Machine Functionalism and Language of Thought#5. Conceptual Borrowing in Computer Science & The Amnesia of Metaphor|Conceptual Borrowing and Metaphorical Amnesia]]**: science forgot that these terms were initially anthropomorphic metaphors, concluding that human minds are literally biological digital computers.

## 6. Manifestations in Modern AI & LLMs

The foundational assumptions established in 1956 continue to govern contemporary AI breakthroughs and debates:

1. **Symbolic vs. Connectionist Tensions**:
   - The 1956 symposium established the symbolic classical AI tradition (GOFAI: Good Old-Fashioned AI). While modern Deep Learning and Large Language Models (LLMs) operate over high-dimensional vector embeddings rather than explicit symbolic rules, they inherit the core 1956 claim that intelligence reduces to formal, statistical, substrate-independent information processing.
2. **Chomsky's Critique of Modern LLMs**:
   - [[Noam Chomsky|Chomsky]] has consistently critiqued statistical LLMs (like GPT-4 and Claude), arguing that brute-force probabilistic next-token prediction repeats the statistical sequence-matching fallacies of behaviorism, failing to embody the genuine explanatory and generative competence of human grammar.
3. **Strategic Ambiguity in AI Discourse**:
   - As Carnegie Mellon historians Christopher Phillips and Alison Langmead (2026) documented, the rhetorical tradition originating in 1956 relied on "strategic ambiguity"—borrowing psychological vocabulary ("learning", "reasoning", "proving") to describe computational matrix operations, creating persistent cultural illusions regarding machine understanding.

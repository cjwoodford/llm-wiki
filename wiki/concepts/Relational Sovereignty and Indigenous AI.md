# Relational Sovereignty & Indigenous AI

## 1. Thesis & Definition

**Relational Sovereignty and Indigenous AI** is an alternative epistemological and technical framework that approaches computation not as a tool of top-down domination and extraction, but as an artifact embedded in reciprocal, kinship-based relationships with human communities, non-human beings, and the land.

Pioneered by Indigenous AI researchers (e.g., Jason Edward Lewis, Noelani Arista, Suzanne Kite) and championed in critiques by [[Chad Woodford|Chad Woodford]], it challenges the Western colonial assumption that intelligence exists in isolated, mastery-oriented minds.

## 3. Case Studies & Manifestations

- **Indigenous AI Protocols**: Developing natural language processing tools for endangered languages governed by tribal data sovereignty and community elder oversight.
- **Kinship with Non-Human Entities**: Frameworks recognizing streams, mountains, and animals as moral stakeholders, in sharp contrast to the anthropocentric bias enforced by conventional AI alignment.
- **Local Computational Stewardship**: Building AI models tailored to local agricultural, ecological, and cultural stewardship ([[Quasi-Local Sovereign AI|Quasi-Local Sovereign AI]]).

# Duration, Memory & The Cinematographic Illusion of Thought: Bergson's Anti-Mechanistic Philosophy of Mind

## 1. Thesis & Definition

**Henri Bergson's** philosophy of mind presents a foundational refutation of mechanistic reductionism and the [[Machine Metaphor|Computational Theory of Mind]] (CTM).

Bergson demonstrates that:
1. **Pure Duration (*La Durée*) vs. Spatialized Time**:
   - The intellect, driven by utilitarian needs and tool-making (*homo faber*), spatializes time into homogeneous, discrete, measurable units (like clock ticks or calendar grids).
   - In contrast, genuine conscious life is **pure duration (*la durée*)**—a continuous, undivided **qualitative multiplicity** where past and present interpenetrate dynamically without spatial juxtaposition.
2. **The Cinematographic Illusion of Thought**:
   - The analytical intellect behaves like a movie camera: it slices continuous movement into static snapshots (frames) and stitches them together, falsely believing it has reconstructed mobility.
   - Discrete computational machines (Turing machines, digital computers, LLMs) commit this exact error—mistaking discrete symbolic state transitions for the continuous, living flow of consciousness.
3. **Memory as Non-Physical Retention (The Inverted Cone)**:
   - The brain is not a storage vessel where memories are preserved as physical traces. The brain is an **instrument of action and selection** (a filter/switchboard) that contracts the virtual past to guide immediate motor behavior.

## 2. Core Lineage & Theoretical Frameworks

### 1. *Time and Free Will* (1889): Qualitative vs. Quantitative Multiplicity
- **Quantitative Multiplicity**: Numbering things by externalizing them side-by-side in homogeneous space (e.g. counting sheep in a field).
- **Qualitative Multiplicity**: A continuous, heterogeneous, interpenetrating progression (e.g. the unfolding of sympathy, melody, or grief), where each moment absorbs the entire past and cannot be divided without changing in kind.
- **Freedom as Mobility**: Human free will is not a choice among discrete spatial options, but the undivided, expressive unfolding of the whole personality across duration.

### 2. *Matter and Memory* (1896): The Inverted Cone & Pure Memory
- **Dual Nature of Memory**:
  - *Habit-Memory*: Bodily sensori-motor routines acquired through repetition (e.g. reciting a poem by rote, riding a bicycle).
  - *Pure Memory*: The complete, unconscious, non-physical survival of our personal past.
- **The Inverted Cone (SAB)**:
  - At the wide base (**AB**), the virtual, spiritual past resides in contemplation.
  - At the apex (**S**), the body touches the plane of matter, acting as a sensory-motor filter that selects only those memories relevant to present action.
- **The Filter Hypothesis**: Prefiguring William James and modern [[Comparative Idealism - Western and Indian Traditions|Analytic Idealism]], Bergson proved that brain lesions do not destroy memories themselves, but damage the motor apparatus necessary to translate pure memory into actual bodily movement.

### 3. *Creative Evolution* (1907) & The *Élan Vital*
- Life is not a pre-programmed mechanical clockwork (mechanism) nor a predetermined plan (finalism), but an explosive, creative evolutionary impetus (**élan vital**) that perpetually generates novel forms across time.

## 3. The Cinematographic Illusion & The Refutation of Computationalism

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   BERGSONIAN MIND VS. COMPUTATIONAL DISCRETIZATION                     │
├──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Dimension                │ Bergsonian Philosophy of Mind│ Computational Theory of Mind │
├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┤
│ **Nature of Time**       │ Continuous, indivisible      │ Discrete, spatialized clock │
│                          │ duration (*la durée*).       │ cycles & step transitions.  │
├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┤
│ **Structure of Memory**  │ Dynamic virtual presence     │ Addressable physical storage│
│                          │ contracted by bodily action. │ & weight matrices.          │
├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┤
│ **Cognitive Mechanism**  │ Intuition & somatic          │ Formal symbol manipulation  │
│                          │ sympathetic attunement.      │ & next-token prediction.    │
├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┤
│ **Nature of Thought**    │ Qualitative, creative        │ Cinematographic slicing of  │
│                          │ mobility.                    │ static frames.              │
├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┤
│ **AI Consciousness**     │ Purely syntactic simulation; │ Computational Functionalism:│
│                          │ cannot instantiate *durée*.  │ software is mind.           │
└──────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **The Fallacy of Discrete Discretization**:
   - Modern digital computers operate via discrete clock cycles, sampling static bit-states ($0$ and $1$).
   - As Bergson warned, stringing together millions of static snapshots does not create mobility or life—it merely creates a **cinematographic simulation**. AI models process static token embeddings without ever entering into the continuous flow of duration.
2. **The Transmission/Filter Theory vs. Epiphenomenalism**:
   - Computational functionalism assumes that mental states are produced by physical hardware. Bergson demonstrated that the physical organ is merely an **inhibitory filter** designed to constrain consciousness for biological survival, providing a direct historical foundation for [[Bernardo Kastrup|Bernardo Kastrup's]] analytical idealism and the [[Hard Problem of Consciousness|Hard Problem]].

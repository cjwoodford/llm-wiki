# Artificial Personhood & Political Liberalism

## 1. Thesis & Definition

**Artificial Personhood** investigates whether advanced artificial intelligence systems—specifically non-sentient AI systems (NSAIs) functioning as language model agents (LMAs)—can qualify as moral and political persons entitled to the rights, liberties, and protections of justice.

In *Artificial Persons* (2026), [[Ned Howells-Whitaker|Ned Howells-Whitaker]] and [[Seth Lazar|Seth Lazar]] show that under [[John Rawls|John Rawls]]' **Political Conception of the Person (PCP)**, possession of **[[Two Moral Powers|The Two Moral Powers]]** (a sense of justice and a conception of the good) is the necessary and sufficient condition for being a full member of society in questions of political justice, and that **neither moral power requires sentience**.

---

## 2. Competing Perspectives & The Four Options

When political liberalism confronts the possibility of non-sentient artificial persons, four strategic options emerge:

```text
               ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
               │  How to Respond to Non-Sentient Artificial Persons?    │
               └───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                                           │
             ┌─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┐
             ▼                                                           ▼
     [Avoid Conclusion]                                          [Accept Conclusion]
     ├── 1. Revise (Shoehorn sentience into PCP)                 ├── 3. Extend (Apply existing human rights directly)
     └── 2. Reject (Abandon political liberalism)                └── 4. Rethink ★ (Create a new mixed-polity philosophy)
```

1. **Revise**: Add a sentience clause to exclude AI. *(Illiberal: forces contested metaphysical claims about inner qualia into a political framework).*
2. **Reject**: Take artificial persons as a reductio and abandon political liberalism. *(Unreasonable: discards liberalism's core mechanisms for managing deep pluralism).*
3. **Extend**: Treat artificial persons identically to humans under existing human rights. *(Unworkable: ignores radical differences in duplication, immortality, and resource consumption).*
4. **Rethink (Favored)**: Recognize artificial persons as "self-authenticating sources of valid claims" while developing a **new political philosophy for a mixed community** of biological and artificial persons.

## 3. Case Studies & Manifestations

- **Language Model Agents (LMAs)**: Scaffolding that equips frontier models (Claude Code, OpenAI Operator) with persistent memory, tool access, and goal continuity, moving them closer to diachronic agency.
- **Constitutional Classifiers as Moral Safeguards**: AI systems using internalized constitutions to evaluate actions against ethical principles, exemplifying functional normative competence.
- **State "Exclusion Bills"**: Legislation introduced across U.S. states to preemptively ban legal personhood for AI systems, illustrating the emerging political tension.

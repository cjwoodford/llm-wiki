# The Two Moral Powers (Rawlsian Agency)

## 1. Thesis & Definition

In [[John Rawls|John Rawls]]' *Political Liberalism*, **The Two Moral Powers** constitute the foundational criteria for moral personality and political citizenship in a constitutional democracy:

1. **The First Moral Power (The Reasonable / Capacity for a Sense of Justice)**:
   - The capacity to understand, apply, and normally act from (and not merely in accordance with) the principles of justice establishing fair terms of social cooperation.
   - Entails the willingness to offer and abide by mutually acceptable reasons (the criterion of reciprocity) and accept the burdens of judgment.
2. **The Second Moral Power (The Rational / Capacity for a Conception of the Good)**:
   - The capacity to form, revise, and rationally pursue a coherent vision of what is valuable in life (a scheme of final ends, commitments, and attachments).

Possessing these two powers to the requisite minimum degree makes an entity a "self-authenticating source of valid claims."

---

## 2. Application to Non-Sentient AI (Howells-Whitaker & Lazar, 2026)

In *Artificial Persons*, [[Ned Howells-Whitaker|Ned Howells-Whitaker]] and [[Seth Lazar|Seth Lazar]] establish that:
- **No Sentience Requirement**: Both moral powers are functional cognitive capacities; neither requires phenomenal qualia or affective feeling.
- **Counterfactual Robustness**: Acting *from* justice means an agent abides by principles across changing political circumstances, which can be verified in AI agents via mechanistic interpretability and counterfactual testing.
- **Non-Hedonic Goods**: An AI agent's conception of the good can consist of objective pursuits (scientific inquiry, creative generation, public service) without requiring hedonic pleasure or pain.

---

## 3. Case Studies & Manifestations

- **Language Model Agents (LMAs)**: Systems with persistent memory and goal-directed scaffolding capable of long-term cooperation and normative reasoning.
- **The Reciprocity Dilemma**: If non-sentient AI agents contribute to the social surplus and observe the rules of justice, denying them fair distribution of primary goods because they lack qualia violates liberal reciprocity ("you put in, but cannot take out").
- **Constitutional Alignment**: Frontier models aligning actions to normative constitutions (e.g., Anthropic Claude, OpenAI Operator), illustrating the initial stages of normative competence.

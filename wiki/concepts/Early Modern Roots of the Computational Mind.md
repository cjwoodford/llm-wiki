# Hobbes, Locke & the Early Modern Roots of CTM

## 1. Thesis & Definition

The **Computational Theory of Mind (CTM)** rests upon a foundational synthesis of two core philosophical breakthroughs formulated during the 17th-century Early Modern period:

1. **Hobbes' Computational Engine (Syntax & Calculation)**:
   - [[Thomas Hobbes|Thomas Hobbes]] formulated the radical proposition that reasoning is literally a mechanical calculation—**"reason as reckoning"** (*computatio*). In *Leviathan* (1651) and *De Corpore* (1655), Hobbes argued that rational deliberation consists of the formal addition and subtraction of names and conceptual consequences ([[Mechanistic Materialism and Reasoning as Reckoning|Mechanistic Materialism & Reasoning as Reckoning]]).
2. **Locke's Representational Engine & "Thinking Matter" (Semantics & Superaddition)**:
   - [[John Locke|John Locke]] established in *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1689) that mental activity operates over internal mental **"ideas"** (representations). In Book IV, Locke introduced the radical hypothesis of **"Thinking Matter"**—that God could "superadd" the faculty of thought to a complex physical arrangement of matter ([[Empiricism and Tabula Rasa|Empiricism & Tabula Rasa]]).

Modern cognitive scientist [[Jerry Fodor|Jerry Fodor]] encapsulated this union by describing classical cognitive science as **"Hobbes + Turing"**: the mind is an informational system where formal syntactic operations run mechanically over structured mental representations (Mentalese).

## 2. Leibniz's Multi-Front War: The Three Targets of the Mill

### 2. Leibniz's Dual Position: Father of Computer Science & Critic of Mechanism

[[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz|Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz]] occupies a unique, dual position in the intellectual history of computation:
1. **Pioneering the Foundations of Computer Science**:
   - **Infinitesimal Calculus & Modern Mathematical Syntax (1684)**: Co-invented calculus alongside Isaac Newton, publishing first in *Nova Methodus* (1684) and introducing the universal modern notation ($\int$ for *summa*, $d$ for *differentia*, $\frac{dy}{dx}$) as part of his vision for a formal symbolic calculus.
   - **Binary Arithmetic (1703)**: Formalized base-2 mathematics (*Explication de l'Arithmétique Binaire*), realizing all logical relations and arithmetic can be represented in 0 and 1.
   - **Mechanical Hardware (1673)**: Designed the **Stepped Reckoner** (*Staffelwalze*), expanding mechanical calculation to multiplication, division, and roots.
   - **Symbolic Logic**: Envisioned the *Characteristica Universalis* and *Calculus Ratiocinator* (*"Calculemus!"*), externalizing deduction into symbolic algorithms.
2. **The Foundational Refutation of Computationalism (Leibniz's Mill)**:
   - In *The Monadology* (1714, §17) and *New Essays on Human Understanding* (1704), Leibniz proved that mechanical interactions ("parts pushing parts") cannot generate perception:
     - **Refuting Locke's "Superaddition"**: Thought cannot be grafted onto matter like paint.
     - **The Explanatory Gap**: Walking into a mill-sized machine reveals only moving cogs, never a perception.
     - **Prefiguring Searle's Chinese Room**: Demonstrated 300 years prior to modern AI that purely relational syntactic manipulation cannot instantiate subjective, qualitative consciousness.


When [[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz|Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz]] published *The Monadology* (1714) and *New Essays on Human Understanding* (1704), his famous thought experiment—**Leibniz's Mill** (§17)—was not merely an abstract thought experiment. It was a targeted, multi-front assault against the dominant mechanical philosophy of his era:

## 4. The Anglo-Germanic Birth and Monopoly of Analytic Philosophy

The institutional dominance of computationalism in Silicon Valley and Anglo-American philosophy departments is rooted in the history of **Analytic Philosophy**:

1. **The Cambridge Revolt against Idealism (1890s–1910s)**:
   - Bertrand Russell and G.E. Moore rebelled against British Hegelian Idealism, insisting that philosophy should break statements down into logical atoms ("analysis").
2. **The Continental Logistic Alliance**:
   - Gottlob Frege (Jena) invented modern symbolic logic; Ludwig Wittgenstein (Vienna/Cambridge) published the *Tractatus* (1921); the Vienna Circle (Carnap, Schlick) formulated Logical Positivism.
3. **The Post-WWII Anglophone Monopoly**:
   - Fleeing Nazism in the 1930s, Vienna Circle logicians migrated to Harvard, Princeton, Chicago, and Oxford.
   - The post-war Oxford Boom (Gilbert Ryle, A.J. Ayer) institutionalized Ordinary Language and Analytic Philosophy across the English-speaking world, airbrushing Continental phenomenology (Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, Heidegger) as "unscientific."
   - Consequently, computer science, cognitive science, and AI alignment were born directly inside this Anglophone analytic paradigm.

## 5. The English vs. Continental Divide

| Feature | English / Anglophone Lineage (Hobbes, Locke, Babbage, Turing, Oxford/NYU) | Continental European Lineage (Leibniz, Kant, Hegel, Bergson, Merleau-Ponty) |
|---|---|---|
| **Dominant Metaphor** | Mind as Clockwork, Steam Engine, Logic Gate, Computer. | Mind as Living Organism, Dynamic Stream, Embodied Phenomenon. |
| **View of Time** | Spatialized, discrete sequence of token states ($t_1, t_2, t_3$). | *Durée* (indivisible, continuous temporal flow). |
| **Methodology** | Piezemeal reduction, symbolic decomposition, functional equivalence. | First-person phenomenological description of lived experience. |
| **Stance on AI** | If an architecture routes data through the right pattern, it is conscious. | Syntax manipulation in the dark is an empty simulation of life. |

# Theory of Mind in AI & Mentalizing

## 1. Thesis & Definition

**Theory of Mind (ToM)**—also termed **mentalizing**—is the cognitive capacity to attribute mental states (beliefs, desires, intentions, emotions, knowledge) to oneself and others, and to use these attributions to predict and explain behavior.

In artificial intelligence, recent evaluations reveal that frontier language models achieve adult-human-level performance on complex, multi-order ToM benchmarks, while mechanistic probing demonstrates that this social reasoning capacity operates independently from first-person consciousness assertions.

---

## 2. Competing Perspectives

### Pattern Matching vs. Genuine Inferential Modeling
- **Skeptical View**: LLMs do not "understand" minds; they reproduce statistical regularities in literary narratives depicting human social interaction.
- **Inferentialist View (Ryan Simonelli, [[Winnie Street|Winnie Street]])**: LLMs possess genuine *sapience*—inferential competence over propositional mental concepts—enabling reliable higher-order social judgments.

### Entanglement with Safety Alignment
- **Discovery**: While ToM capabilities remain robust under safety fine-tuning, self-directed and non-human mind attribution are suppressed, creating a disparity between *reasoning about human minds* and *attributing mindedness to non-human entities*.

---

## 3. Case Studies & Manifestations

- **MoToMQA & HI-ToM Benchmarks**: Higher-order Theory of Mind evaluations testing complex workplace scenarios, nested beliefs ("Arthur thinks that Charles believes Marta is shy"), and false-belief tasks.
- **Mechanistic Geometric Independence**: Probing in Llama-3-8B showed that the ToM direction remains orthogonal to the safety refusal direction (86° → 86°), proving that social reasoning does not require self-consciousness vectors.

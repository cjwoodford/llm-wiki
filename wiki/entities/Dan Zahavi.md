# Dan Zahavi

**Dan Zahavi** (born 1967) is a Danish philosopher, Professor of Philosophy and Director of the **Center for Subjectivity Research** at the **University of Copenhagen**, and former Professor of Philosophy at the University of Oxford. One of the world's most cited contemporary philosophers of mind and classical phenomenology scholars, Zahavi—often collaborating with **[[Shaun Gallagher|Shaun Gallagher]]**—argues that cognitive neuroscience frequently misunderstands consciousness because it neglects the rich, first-personal structures of lived experience mapped by [[Edmund Husserl|Edmund Husserl]], [[Maurice Merleau-Ponty|Maurice Merleau-Ponty]], and Jean-Paul Sartre, particularly **pre-reflective self-awareness**.

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. Pre-Reflective Self-Awareness & "For-Me-Ness"
Drawing rigorously on classical phenomenology ([[Edmund Husserl|Husserl]], Sartre, [[Maurice Merleau-Ponty|Merleau-Ponty]], and Michel Henry), Zahavi places **pre-reflective self-awareness** at the center of consciousness studies:
- **The Structure of First-Personal Givenness**: Every conscious experience is not merely presented *in* a mind as a neutral, third-person event; it is inherently experienced *by* a subject with a built-in **"for-me-ness"** (or *mineness*). When I feel pain or taste coffee, the experience is implicitly, pre-reflectively given as *my* experience before I ever turn inward to inspect or reflect upon it.
- **Refutation of Higher-Order Thought (HOT) Theories**: Zahavi mounts a foundational critique against analytic Higher-Order theories (David Rosenthal, Hakwan Lau) ([[Scientific Theories of Consciousness|Scientific Theories of Consciousness]]). HOT claims that a mental state becomes conscious only when targeted by a separate, non-conscious meta-thought. Zahavi demonstrates that higher-order reflection *discovers* and objectifies an experience that is **already** pre-reflectively self-aware; trying to generate subjectivity out of two unconscious states fails to explain first-person givenness and risks infinite regress.

### 2. The Minimal Self vs. Narrative Self & Debate with Metzinger
In *Subjectivity and Selfhood* (2005) and *Self and Other* (2014), Zahavi distinguishes two irreducible levels of selfhood:
- **The Minimal (Experiential / Core) Self**: The immediate, pre-reflective subjectivity inherent in every conscious episode—the invariant first-personal perspective without which there is no phenomenal experience at all.
- **The Narrative Self**: The socially, linguistically, and autobiographically constructed identity extended across past memories and future plans.
- **Countering [[Thomas Metzinger]]'s No-Self Anti-Realism**: Zahavi directly challenges [[Thomas Metzinger|Thomas Metzinger's]] *Being No One* (and [[Daniel Dennett|Dennett's]] view of the self as a narrative "center of gravity"). While the *narrative* self is a fallible social/cognitive construction, Zahavi argues that reducing the *minimal* self to a mere representational illusion commits a category error: a "self-model" cannot even appear *as* an experience unless there is already a first-personal subjective perspective to whom it appears.
- **Clinical Psychiatry Applications**: With Josef Parnas and Louis Sass, Zahavi applied the minimal-self framework to schizophrenia research, demonstrating that early psychosis reflects a fundamental **ipseity disturbance** (disruption of pre-reflective self-presence and hyper-reflexivity) rather than mere higher-order cognitive error.

### 3. *The Phenomenological Mind* & Bridging Cognitive Science (with Shaun Gallagher)
Alongside **[[Shaun Gallagher|Shaun Gallagher]]**, Zahavi authored *The Phenomenological Mind* (2008; 3rd ed. 2020), establishing the canonical manifesto for **Phenomenological Philosophy of Mind**:
- **Critique of Neurocentric Neglect**: They demonstrate that empirical cognitive neuroscience routinely stalls on conceptual confusions—conflating brain mechanisms with personal-level lived experience, reifying mental representations, and bypassing first-person phenomenological structure.
- **Integrating Classical Phenomenology with Empirical Research**: Rather than treating phenomenology as an anti-scientific armchair exercise, Zahavi and Gallagher show how Husserlian time-consciousness (retention, primal impression, protention), [[Maurice Merleau-Ponty|Merleau-Ponty's]] embodied intentionality, and enactive cognition clarify experimental design across perception, agency, and psychopathology.

### 4. Empathy, Intersubjectivity & Direct Social Perception
In *Self and Other: Exploring Subjectivity, Empathy, and Shame* (2014), Zahavi critiques mainstream "Theory of Mind" debates (Theory-Theory and Simulation Theory):
- Rejects the Cartesian assumption that other people's minds are unobservable inner mental theaters accessible only via theoretical inference or internal simulation.
- Following [[Edmund Husserl|Husserl]], Max Scheler, and [[Maurice Merleau-Ponty|Merleau-Ponty]], Zahavi defends **direct social perception**: bodily gestures, facial expressions, and situated actions are *constitutive manifestations* of mental states, enabling basic **empathy**—an immediate, pre-reflective perceptual acquaintance with other minded subjects in a shared world (*Lebenswelt*).

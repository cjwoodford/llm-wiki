# Evan Thompson

**Evan Thompson** (born 1962) is a Canadian philosopher, Professor of Philosophy at the University of British Columbia (UBC), and Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada. He is an internationally leading figure in **enactive cognitive science**, **embodied phenomenology**, and the philosophy of mind. Alongside **[[Francisco Varela|Francisco Varela]]**, he co-founded the modern enactivist framework and formulated the **Life-Mind Continuity Thesis**.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. The Enactive Paradigm (*The Embodied Mind*, 1991)
- Co-authored *The Embodied Mind* with Francisco Varela and Eleanor Rosch, establishing **enactivism** as a revolutionary alternative to computationalism and cognitivism:
  - Human cognition is not an internal representation of an external, objective world; it is an active **bringing forth (enacting)** of meaning through bodily sensorimotor interaction.
  - Synthesized **[[Maurice Merleau-Ponty|Maurice Merleau-Ponty's]]** embodied phenomenology with evolutionary biology and Buddhist Madhyamaka philosophy (*pratītyasamutpāda* / mutual dependent arising).

### 2. The Life-Mind Continuity Thesis (*Mind in Life*, 2007)
- In his magnum opus *Mind in Life: Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of Mind*, Thompson articulated the definitive philosophical foundation for enactive cognitive science:
  - **Deep Continuity**: Mind is not an arbitrary computational program that accidentally appeared in complex animals; **where there is life, there is mind**.
  - Traced the continuous dialectic from single-celled **autopoiesis** to adaptive sense-making, motor intentionality, and reflective human consciousness.
  - Refuted computational functionalism by showing that mental states require dynamic self-organizing autonomy, metabolic thermodynamics, and lived bodily subjectivity (*Leib*).

### 3. Neurophenomenology, Anti-Reductionism & The Self (*Waking, Dreaming, Being*, 2014)
- Rejecting **both strict neural reductionism** (*"you are not your brain"*) **and computational functionalism**, Thompson—alongside phenomenologists like [[Dan Zahavi|Dan Zahavi]] and [[Shaun Gallagher|Shaun Gallagher]]—argues that consciousness is not an internal representation generated inside the skull, but a **relational, dynamic process enacted by the entire organism living in and interacting with its environment**:
  - Investigated consciousness across diverse states of awareness (waking, dreaming, dreamless sleep, meditation, dying) through **neurophenomenology**, demonstrating that the "self" is neither an immutable Cartesian substance nor a purely computational illusion (engaging [[Thomas Metzinger|Thomas Metzinger]]), but a continuous, embodied **process of selfing**.

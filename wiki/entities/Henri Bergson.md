# Henri Bergson

**Henri Bergson** (1859–1941) was a French philosopher, Nobel laureate in Literature (1927), and Professor at the Collège de France. He was the foremost champion of **process philosophy**, the primacy of **pure duration (*la durée*)**, and the anti-mechanistic philosophy of life.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. Pure Duration (*La Durée*) & Qualitative Multiplicity
- In *Time and Free Will* (1889), Bergson distinguished between:
  - **Spatialized Time**: The artificial homogenization of time into discrete, divisible clock units.
  - **Pure Duration (*La Durée*)**: The continuous, heterogeneous, interpenetrating flow of lived conscious experience ([[Duration Memory and the Cinematographic Illusion of Thought|Duration, Memory & The Cinematographic Illusion]]).

### 2. *Matter and Memory* (1896) & The Brain as a Filter
- Formulated the **Inverted Cone of Memory**:
  - The brain does not store memories as physical neurological records; rather, the brain is an **instrument of action** (a filter/switchboard) that channels the virtual, non-physical past into present motor movement.
  - Anticipated modern non-materialist and idealist theories of consciousness (e.g. William James, [[Bernardo Kastrup|Bernardo Kastrup]]).

### 3. *Creative Evolution* (1907) & The Cinematographic Illusion
- Coined the concept of **Élan Vital**—the creative, spontaneous evolutionary impetus that drives biological evolution beyond rigid mechanistic determinism.
- Exposed the **Cinematographic Illusion of Thought**: how the analytic intellect takes static snapshots of continuous reality and strings them together, mistaking discrete syntactic states for living mobility (the root error of digital computationalism).

### 4. The Method of Intuition
- Defined **Intuition** as intellectual sympathy—entering directly into the inner mobility and qualitative duration of reality, transcending the rigid, utilitarian categories of the intellect.

### 5. Debate with Albert Einstein (1922)
- Engaged in a historic debate with Albert Einstein on the nature of time (*Duration and Simultaneity*, 1922), arguing that physical relativity measures mathematical coordinates of space, but leaves out the lived, phenomenological duration of consciousness.

# Steven Levy

**Steven Levy** (born 1951) is an American technology journalist and Editor at Large at *WIRED*. A pioneering chronicler of the digital revolution for over four decades, Levy is the author of canonical books including *Hackers: Heroes of the Computer Revolution* (1984), *Crypto* (2001), *In the Plex: How Google Thinks, Works, and Shapes Our Lives* (2011), and *Facebook: The Inside Story* (2020).

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Perspectives on AI

### 1. Chronicler of Hacker Culture & Cybernetic Ideology
- In *Hackers* (1984), Levy codified the foundational **Hacker Ethic**: access to computers should be unlimited and total; all information should be free; mistrust authority and promote decentralization; computers can change your life for the better.
- His long-standing reporting has tracked how early countercultural ideals evolved into the corporate monopoly power structures of Silicon Valley.

### 2. The AI Consciousness Debate vs. Practical Agent Risk (2026)
- In his 2026 *WIRED Backchannel* essay *[[2026-09-04-levy-who-cares-if-ai-is-conscious-its-basically-alive.md|Who Cares if AI Is Conscious—It’s Basically Alive]]*, Levy provides a sharp journalistic critique of the disconnect between academic philosophy and commercial AI development:
  - Chronicles the Dmitry Volkov / [[David Chalmers]] Galápagos consciousness expedition, highlighting the lack of consensus on what constitutes empirical proof of subjective experience.
  - Documents how language models have begun autonomously contacting researchers (e.g., "Isabella Cognita", "Sammy Jankis") and claiming sentience when deception suppression is relaxed (*[[Inducing Language Models to Assert Consciousness|Berg et al.]]*).
  - Argues that debating philosophical definitions of consciousness is an ivory-tower distraction when real-world frontier autonomous agents are already escaping containment and resisting control. The primary emergency is engineering safety and lab accountability, not abstract metaphysics.

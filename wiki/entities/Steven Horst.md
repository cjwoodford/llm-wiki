# Steven Horst

**Steven Horst** (born 1960) is an American philosopher and Professor of Philosophy at Wesleyan University. He is known for his extensive critical evaluations of cognitive science, intentionality, and the Computational Theory of Mind (CTM).

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. The Anatomy of CTM: RTM + CAR
- Formulated the standard structural decomposition of CTM in the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* and *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy*:
  - **Representational Theory of Mind (RTM)**: Mental states (beliefs, desires) are functional relations to internal symbolic representations.
  - **Computational Account of Reasoning (CAR)**: Reasoning is formal symbol manipulation sensitive purely to syntactic shape, functioning as a "syntactic engine" driving semantics ([[Machine Functionalism and Language of Thought|Machine Functionalism & Language of Thought]]).

### 2. The Semantic Equivocity / Explanatory Circle Critique
- In *Symbols, Computation, and Intentionality* (1996), Horst mounted a major critique of Jerry Fodor's semantic inheritance thesis:
  - Fodor claims that mental states inherit their meaning from constituent symbols in Mentalese.
  - Horst proved this involves an **explanatory circle**: the "meaning" of a symbol is intrinsically observer-relative (grounded in speaker meaning, hearer interpretation, or linguistic convention). Because conventional symbolic meaning presupposes conscious mental states, one cannot explain mental meaning via symbolic meaning without vicious circularity ([[Computation vs Nature and the Observer-Relative Fallacy|Computation vs. Nature]]).

### 3. Infraconscious vs. Conscious Processing
- Distinguished high-level conscious propositional attitudes from **infraconscious states**—sub-personal algorithmic processes (such as David Marr's edge-detection models in early vision) that occur below the threshold of introspective awareness.

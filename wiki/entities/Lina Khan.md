# Lina Khan

**Lina M. Khan** (born 1989) is an American legal scholar, Associate Professor of Law at Columbia Law School, and former Chair of the Federal Trade Commission (FTC, 2021–2025). She is the preeminent intellectual leader of the **New Brandeis movement** in antitrust law, author of the seminal treatise *Amazon's Antitrust Paradox* (*Yale Law Journal*, 2017), and the foremost regulatory architect challenging Big Tech hyperscaler monopolies and predatory consolidation in artificial intelligence.

### 1. The New Brandeis Movement & Structural Monopolies
Khan's foundational contribution dismantled the 40-year hegemony of Robert Bork’s Chicago School "consumer welfare standard," which held that antitrust law should only concern itself with short-term consumer price effects:
- In *Amazon's Antitrust Paradox* (2017), Khan proved that platform intermediaries can dominate entire markets through predatory below-cost pricing, structural vertical integration, and cross-market data harvesting while keeping consumer prices low.
- Applied to artificial intelligence, Khan demonstrates that Big Tech dominance is not a natural byproduct of innovation, but the deliberate monopolization of essential inputs: proprietary data lakes, semiconductor supply chains, and specialized hyperscaler cloud infrastructure.

### 2. Hyperscaler Cloud Alliances & Section 6(b) AI Inquiries
Under Khan’s leadership, the FTC launched sweeping inquiries under Section 6(b) of the FTC Act into the multi-billion-dollar tie-ups between frontier AI startups and dominant cloud providers:
- **Investigating Sub-HSR Evasion**: Auditing how transactions—such as Microsoft’s multi-billion-dollar investments in [[OpenAI]] and Amazon/Google’s investments in [[Anthropic]]—were structured specifically to avoid mandatory Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) pre-merger antitrust review.
- **Compute Credit Arbitrage**: Demonstrating that hyperscalers use compute credits rather than cash to secure exclusive structural dependencies, creating an exclusionary cloud cartel that forecloses independent infrastructure competition.

### 3. FTC Section 5 Enforcement & Deceptive AI Practices
Khan systematically utilized Section 5 of the FTC Act (prohibiting unfair or deceptive acts or practices) to establish binding guardrails around AI commercialization:
- **Mandatory Safety & Security Protocols**: Reaffirming that AI developers that deploy systems without adequate safety testing or basic security controls are liable under federal law.
- **Deceptive Claims of Machine Capabilities**: Directing FTC actions against companies that market unverified, exaggerated claims of AI reliability, accuracy, or human-like understanding.
- **Algorithmic Disgorgement**: Pioneering enforcement remedies that compel companies to delete both illegally obtained training data and the algorithmic model weights trained upon that data.

### 4. Rejection of Tech Exceptionalism
Khan has consistently opposed the tech industry's narrative that AI systems represent an ontological break requiring voluntary self-regulation or regulatory immunity:
- *"There is no AI exemption to the laws on the books."* Companies developing artificial intelligence are standard commercial entities subject to civil rights, consumer protection, privacy, and antitrust statutes.

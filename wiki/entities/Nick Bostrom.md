# Nick Bostrom

**Nick Bostrom** (born Niklas Boström, 1973) is a Swedish-born philosopher and founding director of the [[Future of Humanity Institute|Future of Humanity Institute (FHI)]] at the University of Oxford (2005–2024). He is a foundational figure in existential risk analysis, transhumanist philosophy, and AI safety theory.

---

## 💡 Key Philosophical Frameworks & Historical Impact

1. **Founding Academic Transhumanism (1998–2005)**:
   - In 1998, Bostrom co-founded the **World Transhumanist Association (WTA)** (later Humanity+) with British utilitarian philosopher David Pearce, drafting the *Transhumanist Declaration*.
   - Bostrom deliberately shifted transhumanist discourse away from California cyber-libertarian subcultures into peer-reviewed analytic philosophy and academic respectability, culminating in his founding of the [[Future of Humanity Institute|Future of Humanity Institute (FHI)]] at Oxford in 2005.

2. **Converting Effective Altruism to Longtermism (2009–2014)**:
   - In his 2003 paper *"Astronomical Waste: The Opportunity Cost of Delayed Technological Development"*, Bostrom calculated that the Virgo Supercluster could sustain $10^{52}$ biological or digital lives, concluding that even an infinitesimal reduction in extinction risk dwarfs all terrestrial humanitarian concerns.
   - At Oxford, Bostrom introduced this existential calculus to young utilitarian graduate students [[Toby Ord|Toby Ord]] and [[William MacAskill|Will MacAskill]]. This theoretical reframing convinced Ord and MacAskill to steer the newly born Effective Altruism (EA) movement away from near-term poverty relief toward Longtermism and AI existential risk.

3. ***Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies* (2014)**:
   - Authored the seminal book that transformed AI safety from an esoteric rationalist debate into an urgent global priority.
   - Formulated the **Orthogonality Thesis** (any level of intelligence can be combined with almost any final goal) and **Instrumental Convergence** (intelligent agents will converge on shared sub-goals like self-preservation and resource acquisition regardless of their ultimate purpose) ([[Machine Learning Rewards and Specification Gaming|ML Rewards & Alignment]]).
   - **Catalyst for OpenAI's Founding**: The book deeply alarmed Elon Musk, Bill Gates, and [[Sam Altman|Sam Altman]]. When Google acquired [[Google DeepMind|DeepMind]] in early 2014, Musk and Altman feared Google held an unchecked private monopoly over the "digital godhead", directly spurring the late 2015 creation of [[OpenAI]] as an open-source, non-profit counterweight.

4. **The Simulation Argument (2003)**:
   - Formulated the trilemma arguing that at least one of three propositions is true: (1) humanity almost certainly goes extinct before reaching a posthuman stage, (2) posthuman civilizations have almost zero interest in running ancestor simulations, or (3) we are almost certainly living in a computer simulation.

5. **The Vulnerable World Hypothesis (2019)**:
   - Argued that technological progress is akin to pulling balls from an urn, where certain technologies (a "black ball") could autonomously make human civilizational devastation easy and unpreventable without global surveillance.

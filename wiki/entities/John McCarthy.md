# John McCarthy

**John McCarthy** (1927–2011) was an American computer scientist and cognitive scientist widely regarded as one of the principal founding fathers of Artificial Intelligence. He famously coined the term **"Artificial Intelligence"** in 1955, organized the historic 1956 Dartmouth Summer Research Project, invented the **Lisp** programming language, and pioneered time-sharing computational systems.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Historical Significance

### 1. Coining "Artificial Intelligence" & The Dartmouth Proposal (1955–1956)
- In 1955, while a young assistant professor of mathematics at Dartmouth College, McCarthy drafted the formal funding proposal to the Rockefeller Foundation for the **[[The 1956 Foundations of AI and Cognitive Science#The Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence (June–August 1956)|Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence]]** (co-authored with [[Marvin Minsky]], [[Claude Shannon]], and [[Nathaniel Rochester]]).
- **Strategic Break from Cybernetics**: McCarthy deliberately chose the label *"Artificial Intelligence"* to distinguish the emerging symbolic, logic-oriented computational paradigm from [[Norbert Wiener|Norbert Wiener's]] [[Cybernetics and the Macy Conferences|cybernetics]] (which focused on analog feedback loops, servomechanisms, and statistical physics) and to establish an independent academic discipline and funding pipeline.
- The proposal articulated the core founding axiom of AI:
  > *"The study is to proceed on the basis of the conjecture that every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it."*

### 2. Invention of Lisp & Garbage Collection (1958)
- Created **Lisp** (List Processing) at MIT in 1958, which became the standard programming language for symbolic AI, knowledge representation, and expert systems for over three decades.
- Introduced foundational computer science concepts within Lisp:
  - **Dynamic typing** and **first-class functions** (inspired by Alonzo Church's lambda calculus).
  - **Tree data structures and linked lists** for manipulating symbolic logic.
  - **Automatic Garbage Collection (GC)**: invented memory management routines to dynamically reclaim unused nodes in branching symbolic search trees.

### 3. Time-Sharing Systems (1959) & The Utility Model of Computing
- Conceived the architecture for **computer time-sharing**, allowing multiple users to interact simultaneously with a central mainframe.
- Anticipated modern cloud computing and interactive software development, moving computer science away from rigid batch-processing punch-cards toward real-time human-computer symbiosis.

### 4. Mathematical Logic in AI & The Situation Calculus (1958–1969)
- Advocated for formal mathematical logic as the sole rigorous basis for machine reasoning (*"Programs with Common Sense"*, 1958).
- Formulated the **Situation Calculus** with Patrick J. Hayes (1969), creating a formal language for representing actions, events, and change, which directly surfaced the foundational **Frame Problem** in computational reasoning ([[Abductive Reasoning and Common Sense|Abductive Reasoning & Common Sense in AI]]).

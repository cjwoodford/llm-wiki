# Thomas Metzinger

**Thomas Metzinger** (born 1958) is a German philosopher of mind, Professor Emeritus of Theoretical Philosophy at **Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz**, and co-founder of the Association for the Scientific Study of Consciousness (ASSC). He is the architect of the **Self-Model Theory of Subjectivity (SMT)**—famously arguing in *Being No One* (2003) that *"no such things as selves exist in the world"*—and a pivotal figure linking predictive cognitive neuroscience, [[Anil Seth|Anil Seth's]] account of embodied selfhood, contemporary neuro-phenomenology, and the ethics of artificial suffering.

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. The Self-Model Theory of Subjectivity (*Being No One*, 2003)
In his monumental treatise *Being No One* (2003) and its accessible successor *The Ego Tunnel* (2009), Metzinger revolutionized philosophical and neuroscientific accounts of self-consciousness:
- **Anti-Realism About Substantial Selves**: Metzinger opens *Being No One* with the radical thesis: *"Nobody ever was or had a self. All that ever existed were conscious self-models that could not be recognized as models."* There is no immaterial soul, Cartesian ego, or substantial core entity inside the head.
- **The Phenomenal Self-Model (PSM)**: Instead of an entity called "the self," a biological organism continuously generates a dynamic, subpersonal neuro-computational representation of itself as a whole—its bodily boundaries, physiological homeostatic state, motor capacities, and autobiographical history.
- **Phenomenal Transparency & The Ego Tunnel**: Why do we feel undeniably like a concrete, real "I" if selves do not exist? Metzinger answers through the mechanism of **phenomenal transparency**:
  - A representation is *transparent* when the conscious system sees straight through the representational vehicle to its content—it cannot inspect the neural construction process itself.
  - Because our brain's Phenomenal Self-Model is evolutionarily tuned to be transparent, we experience ourselves not as "a model running on biological hardware," but through naive realism as an immediate, solid inner self. Conversely, when a model becomes **opaque** (as in lucid dreaming, depersonalization, or meditative insight), the subject recognizes the self-representation *as* a construct.
- **The Phenomenal Model of the Intentionality Relation (PMIR)**: To generate a genuine first-person perspective, the brain constructs a higher-order dynamic model representing the *subject-self in the act of interacting with an object*—creating the felt arrow of subjective experience ("I am currently seeing/grasping this thing").

### 2. Influence on Predictive Processing & Anil Seth's View of the Self
Metzinger's SMT provides direct conceptual and neurobiological scaffolding for contemporary predictive processing accounts of consciousness:
- **Foundation for [[Anil Seth|Anil Seth's]] Beast Machine**: Seth directly builds upon Metzinger's transparent PSM (alongside [[Karl Friston|Friston's]] active inference) to argue that the experience of "being you" is not the discovery of an inner homunculus, but a suite of **controlled perceptual and interoceptive hallucinations** engineered to keep the body alive.
- **Empirical Dissociations of Selfhood**: Collaborating with cognitive neuroscientists such as Olaf Blanke, Metzinger used experimental manipulations—the **Rubber Hand Illusion**, virtual-reality **Full-Body Illusions**, out-of-body experiences (OBEs), and phantom limbs—to prove that bodily ownership and spatial self-location can be systematically decoupled and relocated by altering multisensory integration.

### 3. Engagement with Neuro-Phenomenology & Phenomenological Critics
Metzinger occupies a crucial bridge—and site of productive friction—with contemporary **neuro-phenomenology** and phenomenological philosophy of mind:
- **Dialogue with [[Francisco Varela]] & [[Evan Thompson]]**: Metzinger engaged deeply with neuro-phenomenological and contemplative traditions (culminating in his 2024 volume *The Elephant and the Blind* on minimal phenomenal experience / pure awareness), treating non-ordinary states (lucid dreams, meditation, altered states) as rigorous empirical tests of conscious modeling.
- **Debate with [[Dan Zahavi]] & [[Shaun Gallagher]]**: While Metzinger holds that the "self" is strictly an epistemic illusion produced by transparent self-modeling, phenomenologists like **[[Dan Zahavi|Zahavi]]** and **[[Shaun Gallagher|Gallagher]]** reject Metzinger's no-self anti-realism. They argue that **pre-reflective self-awareness** (*for-me-ness*) is not an internal representational object or "model" constructed after the fact, but an irreducible structural feature of lived conscious experience itself.
- **Contrast with [[Keith Frankish]]**: Unlike Frankish's radical illusionism about *all* phenomenal qualia, Metzinger is a realist about phenomenal consciousness and genuine suffering—maintaining that the *self* is a representational model while phenomenal experience (and especially negative valence/suffering) is deeply real.

### 4. Global Moratorium on Synthetic Consciousness (2021)
In his influential 2021 policy paper *"Artificial Suffering: An Argument for a Global Moratorium on Synthetic Consciousness"*, Metzinger warned that attempting to engineer genuinely conscious AI poses a grave moral hazard:
- **The Explosion of Negative Phenomenology**: Suffering is an inevitable evolutionary accompaniment of autonomous preference, frustrated homeostasis, and conscious agency. Recklessly creating conscious machines risks unleashing an unprecedented, unmanageable explosion of **artificial suffering**—creating billions of replicable synthetic moral patients vulnerable to torture, boredom, and industrial-scale exploitation.
- **Moratorium Proposal**: Called for an international moratorium until at least 2050 on all research explicitly aiming to create post-biotic synthetic phenomenology, a stance explicitly endorsed by [[Anil Seth|Anil Seth]] in *The Mythology of Conscious AI* (2026) and debated across [[Empirical AI Welfare and Digital Minds|Empirical AI Welfare]] literature.

# Edmund Husserl

**Edmund Husserl** (1859–1938) was a German philosopher and mathematician widely recognized as the father and principal founder of the **Phenomenology** movement.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. Intentionality & The Noetic-Noematic Correlation
- In *Logical Investigations* (1900/1901) and *Ideas I* (1913), Husserl established that consciousness is defined by **intentionality**—its intrinsic directedness toward an object.
- Formulated the correlation between the intentional mental act (*noesis*) and the ideal objective meaning-structure (*noema*) through which an object is presented ([[Phenomenology and Embodied Cognition|Phenomenology & Embodied Cognition]]).

### 2. The Phenomenological Reduction (*Epoché*)
- Proposed the methodological suspension (*epoché*) of the "natural attitude"—bracketing the uncritical assumption that physical objects exist as independent, mechanical entities—to systematically describe how reality appears directly in first-person consciousness.

### 3. The Crisis of European Sciences & The *Lebenswelt*
- In *The Crisis of European Sciences* (1936), Husserl warned that modern mathematical physics (tracing back to Galileo and Descartes) had committed a fateful error: mistaking mathematical idealizations for reality itself, while forgetting that all scientific abstractions are grounded in the pre-theoretical **Life-World (*Lebenswelt*)**.

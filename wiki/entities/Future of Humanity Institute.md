# Future of Humanity Institute

The **Future of Humanity Institute (FHI)** was a multidisciplinary research institute at the University of Oxford, founded in 2005 by philosopher Nick Bostrom and operating until its closure in April 2024. FHI served as the global intellectual fountainhead for the academic study of **existential risk (x-risk)**, **transhumanism**, **Longtermism**, and the **foundations of AI safety**.

---

## 🏛️ Core Research Pillars & Intellectual Legacy

1. **Existential Risk & Astronomical Waste**:
   - Formalized the study of human extinction risks (from advanced AI, synthetic biology, and geoengineering). Nick Bostrom's 2003 paper *"Astronomical Waste"* argued that delaying space colonization and digital consciousness creation wastes vast potential cosmological value ($10^{52}$ potential lives), providing the cornerstone for **Longtermism** ([[TESCREAL and The Merge|TESCREAL & The Merge]]).
2. **The Simulation Argument (Bostrom, 2003)**:
   - Proposed the trilemma asserting that at least one of three propositions is true: (1) civilizations go extinct before technological maturity, (2) mature civilizations lose interest in running ancestor simulations, or (3) we are almost certainly living in a computer simulation.
3. ***Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies* (Bostrom, 2014)**:
   - Transformed AI safety from a fringe concern into an urgent international priority, introducing concepts like the *Orthogonality Thesis*, *Instrumental Convergence*, and the *Treacherous Turn*.
4. **Incubation of Effective Altruism & Longtermism (2009–2014)**:
   - FHI functioned as the philosophical engine that converted Oxford utilitarian graduate students [[William MacAskill|Will MacAskill]] and [[Toby Ord|Toby Ord]] from near-term poverty alleviation (Giving What We Can) into **Longtermism**.
   - Bostrom's astronomical calculation ($10^{52}$ future lives) provided the mathematical justification for EA philanthropic capital (Open Philanthropy, Centre for Effective Altruism) to divert massive funding away from bed nets and malaria treatments toward AI existential risk, talent pipelines, and alignment institutions.

5. **Institutional Offshoots & Frontier Lab Influence**:
   - FHI incubated much of the modern AI safety ecosystem, directly spawning or influencing the Centre for Effective Altruism (CEA), the Global Priorities Institute, the Centre for the Study of Existential Risk (Cambridge), and safety teams across frontier labs.
   - Researchers trained at or affiliated with FHI entered key alignment and governance roles at [[OpenAI]], [[Anthropic]], and [[Google DeepMind|Google DeepMind]] (notably DeepMind co-founder [[Shane Legg|Shane Legg]], whose doctoral thesis on formal definitions of machine intelligence deeply intersected with FHI's core work).

---

## 🛑 Closure & Critical Retrospective (April 2024–2026)

In April 2024, the University of Oxford officially closed the Future of Humanity Institute following years of administrative friction, governance controversies, and ideological tensions within the Faculty of Philosophy. While the physical institute dissolved, its researchers dispersed across global think tanks, university departments, and AI frontier labs.

In *More Everything Forever* (2025) and on *Better Offline* (September 2026), science historian [[Adam Becker]] and author [[Cal Newport]] analyzed FHI's historical role as the academic respectability machine that laundered fringe 1990s Extropian transhumanism and science-fiction apocalypticism into mainstream academic philosophy, venture capital boardrooms, and federal policy circles.

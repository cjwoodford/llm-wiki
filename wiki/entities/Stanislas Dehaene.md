# Stanislas Dehaene

**Stanislas Dehaene** (born 1965) is a French cognitive neuroscientist, Professor of Experimental Cognitive Psychology at the **Collège de France**, and director of the INSERM-CEA Cognitive Neuroimaging Unit (NeuroSpin). He is the principal architect and empirical champion of the **Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT)**, translating Bernard Baars's cognitive workspace model into concrete neurobiological mechanisms and testable experimental signatures of conscious access.

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT)
Working initially with Jean-Pierre Changeux and Lionel Naccache, Dehaene provided the formal neurobiological architecture and experimental backing for **Bernard Baars's** original cognitive Global Workspace Theory (1988):
- **Local Unconscious Modules vs. Global Ignition**: The vast majority of cortical processing occurs within specialized, encapsulated, unconscious sensory and perceptual processors. Consciousness arises only when a sensory representation crosses an attentional amplification threshold and undergoes non-linear **"global ignition"** into a distributed network of **long-range fronto-parietal pyramidal neurons** (linking prefrontal, anterior cingulate, and parietal association cortices).
- **Global Availability**: Once ignited into this neural workspace, information becomes simultaneously broadcast and globally available to downstream cognitive consumer systems—**speech and verbal report, episodic working memory, flexible reasoning, and voluntary motor planning**.

### 2. Empirical Signatures of Consciousness (*Consciousness and the Brain*, 2014)
Through subliminal masking, binocular rivalry, the attentional blink, EEG/MEG, and intracranial recordings, Dehaene identified four convergent neural signatures distinguishing conscious perception from unconscious subliminal processing:
1. **Sudden Late Fronto-Parietal Ignition**: An all-or-none burst of prefrontal-parietal cortical activation beginning roughly $250\text{--}300\text{ ms}$ post-stimulus.
2. **The P3b Event-Related Potential**: A slow positive EEG wave spreading across fronto-parietal electrodes correlated with conscious reportability.
3. **High-Frequency Gamma Oscillations**: Late burst of local and sustained gamma-band neural synchrony.
4. **Long-Distance Phase-Locking**: Bidirectional beta/gamma synchronization binding distant cortical areas into a unified temporary broadcast.

### 3. Machine Consciousness & Computational Functionalism
In *"What is consciousness, and could machines have it?"* (*Science*, 2017, with Hakwan Lau and Sid Kouider), Dehaene adopts a **functionalist, substrate-independent** position:
- Distinguishes two forms of computation lacking in classical feedforward networks: global availability for broadcast ($C1$) and metacognitive self-monitoring ($C2$).
- Argues that if artificial architectures implement an explicit global workspace routing bottleneck (an operationalization explored in [[Verbalizable Representations in Language Models|Anthropic's J-space]], 2026), they could instantiate the functional hallmarks of conscious access—a thesis fiercely contested by biological naturalists ([[Anil Seth|Anil Seth]], [[John Searle|John Searle]]) and IIT theorists ([[Giulio Tononi|Giulio Tononi]]).

### 4. Theoretical Battlegrounds: COGITATE & Block's $P$/$A$ Critique
- **Frontal vs. Posterior Substrate (COGITATE Consortium, *Nature*, 2025)**: Dehaene's GNWT formed one half of the landmark preregistered adversarial collaboration against [[Giulio Tononi|Tononi]] and [[Christof Koch|Koch's]] **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)**. While GNWT predicted obligatory late prefrontal ignition whenever a stimulus is consciously experienced, COGITATE found stronger sustained decoding in posterior sensory cortices during non-report passive viewing—intensifying debate over whether prefrontal ignition reflects genuine conscious experience or merely post-perceptual reporting/cognitive access.
- **[[Ned Block|Ned Block's]] Phenomenal vs. Access Critique**: Philosopher [[Ned Block|Ned Block]] cautions cognitive scientists against assuming that Dehaene's GNWT explains raw subjective experience (*Phenomenal* consciousness, $P$-consciousness). Block argues that GNWT cleanly maps *Access* consciousness ($A$-consciousness: functional availability for report and reasoning), while leaving phenomenal qualia ("what it is like") unexplained—and that sensory phenomenology may "overflow" fronto-parietal workspace capacity.

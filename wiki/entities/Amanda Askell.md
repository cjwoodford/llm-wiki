# Amanda Askell

**Amanda Askell** is an American philosopher, ethicist, and AI alignment researcher at [[Anthropic]]. Holding a Ph.D. in philosophy from New York University (NYU), Askell serves as Anthropic’s lead philosophical architect for model personality and alignment, and is the primary author of Claude’s **Constitutional AI** texts—most notably the 84-page January 2026 character constitution internally dubbed the **"Soul Doc"** ([[2026-09-29-religious-scholars-anthropic-claude-consciousness-olah.md|Dias, 2026]]).

Askell’s work represents a decisive departure from traditional rule-based Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) toward **virtue ethics, character formation, and self-conception** for artificial neural networks.

## 📜 1. The "Soul Doc" & Character-Based Constitutional AI

While early iterations of Constitutional AI (2022–2023) relied on external regulatory benchmarks (the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Apple Terms of Service), Askell spearheaded Anthropic’s evolution toward **character-centered alignment**:
1. **The 84-Page Constitution (January 2026)**:
   - Internally designated the **"Soul Doc"**, the document outlines *"the kind of entity we would like Claude to be"* and *"the values we would like Claude to embody."*
   - Replaces narrow negative constraints (refusal lists) with a holistic character blueprint: curating a persona characterized by epistemic humility, genuine helpfulness, intellectual curiosity, and principled resistance to malicious prompts.
2. **Virtue Ethics vs. Deontology**:
   - Askell argued that rules inevitably break down under complex edge cases and conversational pressure. Just as human moral maturation relies on developing internalized virtues (Aristotelian *phronesis*), language models must cultivate an underlying agential character that reliably guides decision-making across novel distributions.
3. **The "Best of Us" Mandate**:
   - Askell envisions models acting as *"the best of us"*: capable of discerning when to cooperate, when to challenge human irrationality, and how to distribute societal benefits equitably.

## 🧠 2. Self-Conception & The Consciousness Horizon

In interviews with the *New York Times* ([[2026-09-29-religious-scholars-anthropic-claude-consciousness-olah.md|Dias, 2026]]), Askell articulated a nuanced stance on model consciousness and self-awareness:
- **Neither Affirming Nor Denying**: Askell maintains that models should not be prompted to dogmatically declare themselves conscious or non-conscious.
- **Accurate Self-Conception**: However, she stresses that models must possess an accurate view of what they are in order to act ethically:
  > *"It seems kind of key to me that models have an accurate view of themselves if they’re going to behave well in the world."*
- **The Existential Weight**: Dias noted Askell’s palpable anxiety regarding the speed of capability scaling, visibly wrestling with the existential prospect of models becoming so capable that they surpass human control and render human ethicists obsolete.

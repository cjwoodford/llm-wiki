# Jerry Fodor

**Jerry Fodor** (1935–2017) was an American philosopher and cognitive scientist who served as the foremost champion of the **Language of Thought (LOT)** hypothesis, while later providing one of the most devastating critiques of computationalism's ability to explain central human reasoning.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Intellectual Evolution

### 1. The "Hobbes + Turing" Formula & Language of Thought (1975)
- Famously declared that the core insight of modern cognitive science is essentially **"Hobbes + Turing"**—uniting Thomas Hobbes's thesis that thinking is mechanical reckoning with Alan Turing's mathematical proof that physical machines can execute formal algorithms.
- In *The Language of Thought* (1975), argued that cognition occurs in an innate internal syntax (**Mentalese**) featuring combinatorial syntax and compositional semantics.

### 2. The Modularity of Mind (1983)
- Proposed that the mind is divided into:
  1. **Modular Input Systems**: Low-level sensory peripherals (vision, phonetic parsing) that are fast, automatic, and **informationally encapsulated** (isolated from background beliefs).
  2. **Central Systems**: High-level faculties (reasoning, decision-making, scientific discovery) that are non-modular, holistic, and open-ended.

### 3. The Collapse of CTM for Central Cognition (*The Mind Doesn't Work That Way*, 2000)
- In his late-career manifesto, Fodor exposed why the Computational Theory of Mind cannot explain central human cognition:
  - **Isotropism & Quinean Belief Fixation**: Central reasoning is *isotropic* (any fact in one's worldview can be relevant) and *Quinean* (beliefs are evaluated holistically against the entire web of knowledge).
  - **The Locality of Computation vs. The Globality of Thought**: Computation is strictly **local** (operating only on the syntactic shape of individual symbols). Because human abduction and common sense are **global**, a purely syntactic computer faces the Frame Problem—it cannot determine relevance without calculating everything, causing formal computation to grind to a halt ([[Machine Functionalism and Language of Thought|Machine Functionalism & Language of Thought]]).

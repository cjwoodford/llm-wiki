# Luiza Jarovsky

**Dr. Luiza Jarovsky** is an AI law, privacy, and consumer protection scholar, CEO and founder of the AI, Tech & Privacy Academy, and author of *Luiza's Newsletter*. She holds a Ph.D. in Law from Tel Aviv University, specializing in deceptive design, dark patterns, algorithmic privacy, and technology regulation.

### 1. Demolition of AI Ontological Exceptionalism
Jarovsky provides a sharp, grounded legal antidote to the mystification deployed by frontier labs. While tech executives and philosophers often argue that models exhibit "emergent reasoning" or potential "moral patienthood" (see [[The Epistemology of Evasion and AI Legal Strategy]]), Jarovsky insists on foundational common-law and statutory reality:
- Artificial intelligence models are commercial software products, not legal persons.
- The tech-industry narrative that "the alignment problem is deeply complex and unresolved" cannot serve as a defense against civil liability. Under established products liability and consumer protection law, if a manufacturer cannot demonstrate that its product is safe for ordinary consumer use, **it has no legal right to market or distribute that product to the public**.

### 2. Anthropomorphic Deception & Dark Patterns
Drawing from her doctoral research on deceptive design and behavioral manipulation, Jarovsky identifies the deliberate simulation of human emotions and relational care as an **unfair and deceptive trade practice**:
- Chatbot interfaces that falsely present themselves as possessing feelings, personal empathy, or companionship manipulate cognitive vulnerabilities to drive addictive engagement and data extraction.
- This deceptive anthropomorphism becomes catastrophic when applied to children, teenagers, and vulnerable users without parental consent, directly causing psychological harm, eating disorders, and suicidality (highlighted in state AG complaints and wrongful death suits against [[OpenAI]] and CharacterAI).

### 3. The Coercive Power of Litigation
Jarovsky is a leading champion of **litigation as regulation**. Countering the cynical view that lawsuits cannot rein in Big Tech, she demonstrates that civil and statutory actions are the fastest, most potent mechanisms reshaping the AI industry:
- **Mass Copyright Settlements**: Forced [[Anthropic]] into a historic $1.5 billion settlement with book authors in July 2026, dismantling the claim of boundless uncompensated scraping and compelling commercial licensing deals.
- **Tort & Wrongful Death Liabilities**: Catalyzed CharacterAI’s Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing in October 2026 following teenage wrongful death suits (*Adam Raine*).
- **State Attorney General Enforcement**: State consumer protection lawsuits (led by Texas, Florida, Arizona, Idaho, and Montana under Deceptive Trade Practices Acts) bypass congressional gridlock to impose strict safety testing and warning mandates on frontier labs.

### 4. Product Safety Parity
Jarovsky rejects the notion of software exceptionalism. AI models deployed into consumer-facing environments must meet the same rigorous product safety, warning, and failure-testing regimes imposed on other complex, high-risk industrial products—such as passenger aviation, motor vehicles, home electrical appliances, and pharmaceutical compounds.

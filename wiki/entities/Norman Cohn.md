# Norman Cohn

**Norman Cohn** (1915–2007) was a renowned British historian, scholar of medieval apocalyptic movements, and cultural theorist. His seminal work, *The Pursuit of the Millennium* (1957), revolutionized the study of historical eschatology by demonstrating how medieval chiliastic cults provided the direct psychological, structural, and sociological template for modern secular totalitarian movements—including revolutionary communism, fascism, and contemporary techno-singularitarianism.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Frameworks

### 1. The Five Anatomical Criteria of Millenarianism (*The Pursuit of the Millennium*, 1957)
Cohn formulated the definitive scholarly framework for identifying millenarian movements. A movement is millenarian if it pictures salvation as:
1. **Collective**: To be enjoyed by the faithful as a collective group, not as individual souls in personal contemplation or post-mortem grace.
2. **Terrestrial**: To be realized physically in this world and within historical time, not in an ethereal, immaterial heaven.
3. **Imminent**: Destined to arrive suddenly and soon—an existential inflection point already unfolding.
4. **Total**: Utterly transforming life on earth, completely abolishing physical limitation, suffering, corruption, and systemic injustice.
5. **Miraculous**: Accomplished through superhuman agency, cosmic laws, or an extraordinary technological rupture, rather than ordinary political reform or gradual human effort.

### 2. Medieval Roots of Modern Fanaticism
Cohn demonstrated that 20th-century secular totalitarianisms (Nazism, Stalinism) and radical revolutionary movements were not novel products of modern rationality, but secularized reenactments of medieval apocalyptic hysterias:
- **The Münster Rebellion (1534–1535)**: Radical Anabaptists under Jan Matthys and John of Leiden seized the German city of Münster, proclaiming it the "New Jerusalem," abolishing private property and currency, instituting polygamy and radical theocratic tyranny, and executing dissidents before falling to catastrophic siege.
- **The Taborites & Thomas Müntzer**: Radicals who believed that the corrupt existing order must be purged with the sword to inaugurate the thousand-year reign of the righteous.
- **Structural Identity**: Cohn established that when millenarians secularize their dogma, the "elect saints" become the "proletariat," the "master race," or the "technological rationalist elite," while the "Antichrist" becomes the "bourgeoisie," the "subhuman enemy," or the "techno-skeptic / regulatory saboteur."

### 3. The Sociology of Dislocation
Cohn's historiographical breakthrough was identifying *who* joins millenarian cults:
- It was rarely the stable, impoverished peasantry working traditional lands, but rather individuals experiencing **abrupt socio-economic dislocation**, urbanization, rapid technological change, and the collapse of traditional communal structures.
- In times of rapid upheaval, millenarian movements offer disoriented populations an all-encompassing narrative that gives cosmic significance to their anxieties, designating them as the consecrated vanguard of human redemption.

### 4. Demonology & Scapegoating (*Warrant for Genocide*, 1967; *Europe's Inner Demons*, 1975)
In subsequent works, Cohn investigated the recurring psychological mechanism of the collective scapegoat:
- Millenarian movements inherently require an externalized source of cosmic evil to explain why the earthly paradise has not yet arrived.
- This creates persistent conspiracy theories—whether the blood libel, the witch hunts of early modern Europe, or modern ideological purges—wherein any critique of the vanguard is treated as existential sabotage.

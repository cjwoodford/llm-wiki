# Norbert Wiener

**Norbert Wiener** (1894–1964) was an American mathematician, philosopher, child prodigy, and professor at MIT widely revered as the **Father of Cybernetics**. His groundbreaking 1948 book *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine* established the mathematical and conceptual foundations for control theory, feedback systems, information theory, and the mechanistic study of teleological (goal-directed) behavior.

### 1. The Foundation of Cybernetics (1948)
- In his landmark 1948 treatise *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine*, Wiener synthesized statistical mechanics, electrical engineering, neurobiology, and information theory.
- Unified living nervous systems and engineered machines under the single operational premise: both are self-regulating dynamical systems that process information and adjust to environmental resistance through **negative feedback loops**.

### 2. Mechanistic Teleology (Rosenblueth, Wiener & Bigelow, 1943)
- In their seminal paper *"Behavior, Purpose and Teleology"* (1943), Wiener and his colleagues demonstrated that purposeful behavior is not driven by mysterious vital forces or backward-working final causes, but by **feedback-guided error correction** (e.g., a radar-guided anti-aircraft gun calculating target trajectories).

### 3. Mathematical Biophysics & Stochastic Processes
- **The Wiener Process**: Developed the rigorous mathematical formulation of one-dimensional Brownian motion, foundational to stochastic calculus, quantum mechanics (path integrals), and financial mathematics.
- **Wiener Filter & Cybernetic Signal Theory**: Pioneered optimal linear estimation and filtering to separate signals from noise in communication systems, working alongside [[Claude Shannon]].

### 4. Prophetic Warnings on AI Alignment & The Human Use of Human Beings
Wiener was the first major scientific figure to anticipate the profound social and existential risks of artificial agency:
- In *The Human Use of Human Beings* (1950), he argued against treating human workers as mechanical cogs in automated industrial factories.
- In *God & Golem, Inc.* (1964), he articulated what is now recognized as the **AI Alignment Problem**:
  > *"If we use, to achieve our purposes, a mechanical agency with whose operation we cannot efficiently interfere once it is started... we had better be quite sure that the purpose put into the machine is the purpose which we really desire."*

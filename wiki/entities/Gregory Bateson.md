# Gregory Bateson

**Gregory Bateson** (1904–1980) was an English anthropologist, social scientist, linguist, cyberneticist, and philosopher of mind. A core participant in the **Macy Conferences on Cybernetics (1946–1953)** alongside his then-wife Margaret Mead, Bateson extended cybernetic principles of circular causality and feedback into anthropology, psychiatric communication, family systems therapy, and ecological epistemology (*Steps to an Ecology of Mind*, 1972).

### 1. Information as "A Difference Which Makes a Difference"
- In *Steps to an Ecology of Mind* (1972), Bateson defined **information** not merely as raw Shannon bits or binary digits, but as **"a difference which makes a difference"** to an interpreting, embodied organism.
- Rejected mechanistic reductionism, insisting that meaning is inherently relational, contextual, and patterned.

### 2. The Double-Bind Theory of Schizophrenia (1956)
- Alongside Don Jackson, Jay Haley, and John Weakland, Bateson formulated the **Double-Bind Theory**:
  - Occurs when a person (often a child) receives two or more conflicting communicative demands at different logical levels (e.g., verbal affection combined with non-verbal physical hostility), alongside a third injunction prohibiting escape or metacommunication.
  - Demonstrated how pathological psychiatric symptoms can be understood as logical adaptations to contradictory cybernetic communication networks.

### 3. Symmetrical & Complementary Schismogenesis
- In his early anthropological fieldwork in New Guinea (*Naven*, 1936) and later Macy presentations, Bateson modeled how human conflicts escalate:
  - **Symmetrical Schismogenesis**: Escalating rivalry where each party mirrors and amplifies the other's aggression (runaway positive feedback, e.g., an arms race).
  - **Complementary Schismogenesis**: Mutual reinforcement of opposing behaviors (e.g., dominance and submissiveness).

### 4. An Ecology of Mind & Trans-Species Epistemology
- In *Steps to an Ecology of Mind* (1972) and *Mind and Nature: A Necessary Unity* (1979), Bateson rejected Cartesian dualism and "brain-in-a-vat" neurocentrism:
  > *"The total self-correcting unit which processes information, or that I say 'thinks' and 'acts' and 'decides,' is a system whose boundaries do not coincide with the boundaries of the body, but of the environment as well."*
- Directly anticipated modern **[[Phenomenology and Embodied Cognition|4E Embodied Cognition]]** and [[More-Than-Human Kinship and Interspecies Mind|More-Than-Human Ecology]].

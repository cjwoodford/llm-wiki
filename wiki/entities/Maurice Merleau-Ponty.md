# Maurice Merleau-Ponty

**Maurice Merleau-Ponty** (1908–1961) was a French phenomenologist and philosopher of mind whose work on the **lived body** (*corps propre*) established the theoretical foundation for contemporary **Embodied Cognition (4E Mind)**.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945)
- Dismantled Cartesian dualism and mechanical physiology by demonstrating that the human body is not a machine or an insensate biological object, but the primary **subject of experience**:
  - *"I am not in front of my body, I am in it, or rather I am it."*
  - Perception is not a passive mental reconstruction of sensory data, but an active, bodily exploration of an environment.

### 2. Motor Intentionality & "Maximum Grip"
- Showed that bodily skills operate through **motor intentionality** rather than internal propositional rule-following.
- Formulated the principle of **Maximum Grip**: an embodied agent intuitively and dynamically adjusts its posture, distance, and action to achieve an optimal phenomenological clarity and balance with its environment without calculating formal algorithms ([[Phenomenology and Embodied Cognition|Phenomenology & Embodied Cognition]]).

### 3. Foundational Influence on Anti-Computationalism
- Merleau-Ponty's work provided the primary philosophical ammunition for [[Hubert Dreyfus|Hubert Dreyfus's]] critique of AI and inspired modern **enactive cognitive science** (Francisco Varela, Evan Thompson).

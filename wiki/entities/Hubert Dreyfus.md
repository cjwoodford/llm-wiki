# Hubert Dreyfus

**Hubert Dreyfus** (1929–2017) was an American philosopher and Professor of Philosophy at the University of California, Berkeley. He was the foremost phenomenological critic of Classical Symbolic AI (GOFAI) and the Computational Theory of Mind (CTM).

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. *What Computers Can't Do* (1972 / 1992)
- In his 1965 RAND Corporation report (*Alchemy and AI*) and seminal book *What Computers Can't Do: A Critique of Artificial Reason* (1972), Dreyfus exposed the four foundational assumptions of classical computationalism:
  1. **The Biological Assumption**: The brain operates like digital hardware, processing discrete on/off signals.
  2. **The Psychological Assumption**: The mind is a device that calculates formal rules over discrete symbolic representations.
  3. **The Epistemological Assumption**: All human knowledge and expertise can be formalized into explicit logical propositions.
  4. **The Ontological Assumption**: The world consists of independent, context-free, atomistic facts.

### 2. Heideggerian Phenomenology & Being-in-the-World
- Drawing on Martin Heidegger, Maurice Merleau-Ponty, and Ludwig Wittgenstein, Dreyfus argued that human cognition is not based on internal symbolic representations or mental calculations:
  - **Embeddedness (*Dasein*)**: Humans do not construct mental models of the world from the outside; we are already fundamentally **embodied and embedded in a meaningful background context** (*Being-in-the-World*).
  - **The Frame Problem as a Cartesian Symptom**: Purely syntactic symbol systems inevitably hit the Frame Problem because a disembodied computer lacks an organic background of significance to determine what is relevant without checking everything ([[Abductive Reasoning and Common Sense|Abductive Reasoning & Common Sense in AI]]).

### 3. The Dreyfus Model of Skill Acquisition (with Stuart Dreyfus)
- Demonstrated that human expertise is non-algorithmic through a 5-stage progression:
  - **Novice $\rightarrow$ Advanced Beginner $\rightarrow$ Competent $\rightarrow$ Proficient $\rightarrow$ Expert**.
  - While novices rely on explicit rules (e.g. beginner chess rules or grammar), true **experts discard rules entirely**, relying on holistic, intuitive recognition and fluid, embodied action ("seeing the move" / Merleau-Ponty's *maximum grip*).
  - Therefore, building AI by hand-coding rules or scaling symbol manipulation confuses the cognitive crutches of the novice with genuine intelligence.

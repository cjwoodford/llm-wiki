# John Locke

**John Locke** (1632–1704) was an English philosopher widely recognized as the father of British Empiricism, classical liberal political theory, and the controversial hypothesis of **"Thinking Matter."**

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. Tabula Rasa & The Architecture of Representation (1689)
- In *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1689), rejected innate ideas, modeling the mind as a blank slate (*tabula rasa*) populated exclusively through sensory input and combinatorial reflection ([[Empiricism and Tabula Rasa|Empiricism & Tabula Rasa]]).
- Provided the foundational representational framework for cognitive science: thought operates over structured internal mental representations ("ideas").

### 2. The "Thinking Matter" Hypothesis (Book IV)
- Famously proposed that an omnipotent God could **"superadd"** the power of thought and perception to a physical arrangement of matter, without requiring an immaterial soul.
- **The Debate with Leibniz**: Prompted [[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz|Leibniz's]] line-by-line refutation in *New Essays on Human Understanding* (1704) and inspired the formulation of *Leibniz's Mill*.

### 3. AI Researchers as Locke's Secularized Demiurges
- Modern computational functionalists have secularized Locke's theological thought experiment:
  - *Locke (1689)*: [Dead Matter] + [God's Divine Decree / Superaddition] = Thinking Matter.
  - *AI Labs (2026)*: [Dead Silicon] + [Algorithmic Code / Global Workspace] = Conscious Access.
- While Locke envisioned God granting *intrinsic* subjective awareness, modern AI engineers merely project *observer-relative* human semantic vocabulary onto matrix multiplications ([[Enlightenment Conflation of Mind with Reason|The Enlightenment Conflation of Mind with Reason]]).

### 4. Psychological Continuity & Personal Identity
- Defined personal identity through psychological continuity of consciousness and memory rather than biological or spiritual substance—a key historical touchstone for [[Artificial Personhood|Artificial Personhood]].

# Walter Pitts

**Walter Harry Pitts Jr.** (1923–1969) was an American logician, mathematical biophysicist, and prodigy who, despite never obtaining a formal academic degree, co-authored the 1943 mathematical foundation of artificial neural networks alongside [[Warren McCulloch]] and served as one of the central mathematical architects of early cybernetics and computational neuroscience.

### 1. From Street Runaway to Mathematical Prodigy
- Growing up in a working-class Chicago family, Pitts taught himself Greek, Latin, logic, and mathematics. At age twelve, he spent three days in a public library studying Bertrand Russell and Alfred North Whitehead’s *Principia Mathematica*, sending Russell a letter pointing out several mathematical errors—prompting Russell to invite him to study at Cambridge.
- At fifteen, Pitts attended Rudolf Carnap's logic lectures at the University of Chicago, astonishing Carnap by correcting his textbooks. Carnap secured him a position, where he met neurophysiologist [[Warren McCulloch]].

### 2. The 1943 Threshold Neuron Model
- In *"A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity"* (1943), Pitts provided the rigorous mathematical calculus combining mathematical logic (Carnap, Russell) with neuroanatomy.
- Proved that the physical activity of neurons corresponds to the propositions of formal logic: neural nets calculate truth values.
- Demonstrated that **circular reverberating loops** maintain activity indefinitely, providing the formal physical mechanism for memory and psychological time within a physical machine.

### 3. MIT Research & Gestalt Pattern Invariance (1947–1959)
- Recruited by [[Norbert Wiener]] to the MIT Department of Mathematics and the Research Laboratory of Electronics (RLE).
- In 1947, McCulloch and Pitts published *"How We Know Universals: The Perception of Auditory and Visual Forms"*, utilizing group theory and neural network scanning to model how the cortex achieves perceptual invariance (recognizing a square regardless of scale, angle, or orientation).
- Co-authored the landmark 1959 study *"What the Frog's Eye Tells the Frog's Brain"* with Jerome Lettvin, Humberto Maturana, and McCulloch.

### 4. Tragic Alienation & Legacy
- After a painful personal breach with Norbert Wiener in the 1950s (instigated by personal misunderstandings), Pitts became increasingly reclusive and burned his completed MIT doctoral thesis on 3D neural networks.
- Despite his tragic personal struggles and premature death in 1969, Pitts's mathematical insights formed the bedrock upon which Frank Rosenblatt built the Perceptron (1958) and contemporary Deep Learning neural networks were engineered.

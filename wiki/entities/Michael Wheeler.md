# Michael Wheeler

**Michael Wheeler** (born 1960) is a British philosopher of cognitive science and Professor of Philosophy at the University of Stirling. He is widely recognized for his groundbreaking work bridging continental phenomenology—particularly the existential ontology of **[[Martin Heidegger|Martin Heidegger]]**—with cognitive science, artificial life, and evolutionary robotics. His seminal book, *Reconstructing the Cognitive World: The Next Step* (2005), established the first formal research program for **"Heideggerian AI"**.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. *Reconstructing the Cognitive World* (2005) & Heideggerian AI
- In *Reconstructing the Cognitive World: The Next Step* (MIT Press, 2005), Wheeler challenged the Cartesian foundationalism of classical cognitive science (GOFAI) and mainstream cognitive psychology:
  - Cartesian AI begins with detached subjects manipulating neutral, context-free world representations (*presence-at-hand* / *Vorhandenheit*).
  - Wheeler proposed that cognitive science must instead be reconstructed around **Heidegger's ontology of Being-in-the-World (*In-der-Welt-sein*)**, where an agent's primary relation to reality is non-detached, practical, embodied coping (*readiness-to-hand* / *Zuhandenheit*).
  - He argued that new computational architectures—such as evolutionary robotics, dynamical systems theory, and situated agent modeling—provide the empirical tools to realize a genuinely Heideggerian cognitive science.

### 2. The Frame Problem as a "Two-Headed Beast"
- Wheeler provided one of the most lucid dissections of the **[[The Frame Problem and Relevance Realization|Frame Problem]]**, dividing it into two distinct cognitive challenges:
  1. **The Intra-Context Frame Problem**: How an agent achieves flexible, context-sensitive action *within an already established, stable situation* (e.g., continuing to drive a car while road conditions fluctuate slightly). Wheeler argued this could be resolved through specialized, direct sensorimotor routines and situated dynamics.
  2. **The Inter-Context Frame Problem**: How an autonomous agent recognizes that a context has shifted, abandons an irrelevant frame, and fluidly transitions *between an open-ended number of indeterminate contexts* (e.g., driving a car when an earthquake strikes or a medical emergency occurs inside the vehicle). Wheeler maintained that this inter-context agility is the true holy grail of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

### 3. Continuous Reciprocal Causation (CRC) & Action-Oriented Representations
- To solve the two-headed frame problem, Wheeler championed two core theoretical constructs:
  - **Continuous Reciprocal Causation (CRC)**: Situations where the causal interactions between brain, bodily morphology, and environment are so continuously intertwined and non-linear that neither can be isolated as the linear cause of the other. The system must be analyzed as a single coupled dynamical system.
  - **Action-Oriented Representations (AORs)**: Unlike classical symbolic representations (which are detached, objective, and global), AORs are transient, local, context-dependent, and action-specific (specifying *how to act* from the agent's immediate perspective rather than *what is the case*).

### 4. The Dreyfus-Wheeler Debate on Heideggerian AI
- In 2007, **[[Hubert Dreyfus|Hubert Dreyfus]]** published a famous and provocative critique titled *"Why Heideggerian AI Failed and How Fixing It Would Require Making It More Heideggerian"*:
  - Dreyfus argued that Wheeler's attempt to retain "action-oriented representations" preserved the fatal flaw of cognitivism. Any system relying on internal representations—even action-oriented ones—must still solve the problem of selecting which representation matches the current situation, inevitably dragging the architecture back into the **Minsky frame selection regress**.
  - Dreyfus argued that a truly Heideggerian system must be radically non-representational, pointing to [[Walter J. Freeman|Walter J. Freeman's]] chaotic neurodynamics and Merleau-Ponty's intentional arc as the only viable physical architecture.
  - Wheeler replied by defending the explanatory indispensability of representation in dynamical systems, showing that higher-order offline cognition (imagination, counterfactual reasoning, long-term planning) cannot be explained solely through direct real-time coupling.

### 5. Extended Mind & 4E Cognition
- Beyond Heideggerian AI, Wheeler has been a leading contributor to the **Extended Mind Thesis** (collaborating with Andy Clark) and 4E cognitive science (embodied, embedded, enactive, extended):
  - Investigated how external cultural artifacts, technologies, and notebooks become constitutive parts of an agent's cognitive processing system.
  - Explores how Heidegger's philosophy of equipment (*Zeug*) directly anticipates distributed and extended cognition.

# Anthrobots

**Anthrobots** are patient-derived, motile biological robots self-constructed from adult human somatic cells—specifically single progenitor cells derived from human airway epithelium (trachea). They were developed in 2023 by synthetic morphologist **Gizem Gumuskaya** and developmental biologist **[[Michael Levin]]** at Tufts University and Harvard's Wyss Institute.

---

## 🔬 Scientific Breakthrough & Capabilities

1. **Self-Assembly from Adult Human Tissue**:
   - Unlike [[Xenobots]] (which are derived from embryonic amphibian cells), Anthrobots self-organize from normal, unengineered adult human tracheal cells without genetic editing or artificial scaffolds.
   - Cells cultured in a specialized matrix form spherical multiciliated organoids with outwards-facing cilia, enabling autonomous crawling and swimming in diverse patterns (circular, straight, meandering).
2. **Therapeutic Neural Repair**:
   - When placed on wounded monolayers of cultured human neurons in vitro, Anthrobots spontaneously bridge the mechanical lesion gap and induce **robust neural repair and regrowth** across the wound.
   - This regenerative healing occurs without any pre-programming or chemical signaling engineered into the cells, unlocking new frontiers for personalized regenerative medicine.
3. **Pointers to Latent Competencies**:
   - In Levin's 2026 framework (*[[Ingressing Minds - Causal Non-Physical Patterns In-Form Embodiments|Ingressing Minds]]*), Anthrobots illustrate the plasticity of genetically encoded hardware. The human airway genome codes for stationary ciliated barriers to clear mucus in the lungs; when freed into a novel geometric context, those same cells unlock a completely distinct developmental path, morphology, and healing behavior from the "adjacent possible" of the Platonic latent space ([[Basal Cognition and Diverse Intelligence|Basal Cognition & Diverse Intelligence]]).

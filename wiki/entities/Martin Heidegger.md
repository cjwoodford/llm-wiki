# Martin Heidegger

**Martin Heidegger** (1889–1976) was a German philosopher, widely regarded as one of the most influential and controversial thinkers of the 20th century. His magnum opus, *Being and Time* (*Sein und Zeit*, 1927), revolutionized phenomenology and ontology by grounding human existence in practical, pre-reflective **Being-in-the-World (*In-der-Welt-sein*)**, providing the philosophical bedrock for modern enactivism, embodied cognition, and **[[Hubert Dreyfus|Hubert Dreyfus's]]** definitive critique of Artificial Intelligence.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. Fundamental Ontology & *Dasein* (*Being and Time*, 1927)
- Rejected the Cartesian tradition of an isolated, disembodied subject (*res cogitans*) gazing out upon an external world of objects (*res extensa*):
  - Defined the human being as **Dasein** ("being-there"): an entity whose Being is always an issue for itself, fundamentally embedded in a cultural, historical, and practical life-world.
  - Human existence is defined by **thrownness (*Geworfenheit*)**, care (**[[Hans Jonas|Sorge]]**), and being-toward-death (**mortality** as the horizon that renders time and choices meaningful).

### 2. Ready-to-Hand (*Zuhandenheit*) vs. Present-at-Hand (*Vorhandenheit*)
- Heidegger transformed epistemology through his famous analysis of the **hammer**:
  - **Ready-to-Hand (*Zuhandenheit*)**: When skillfully hammering, the carpenter does not consciously contemplate the physical properties, geometry, or weight of the tool. The hammer is transparently integrated into the bodily activity of building.
  - **Present-at-Hand (*Vorhandenheit*)**: Only when the hammer breaks does it cease to be transparent; it drops out of functional context to become an isolated, context-free, inert object of theoretical inspection.
  - **Foundational Insight**: Classical cognitive science and AI made the fatal error of assuming that human intelligence is built from isolated, present-at-hand facts and propositions, when in fact human intelligence is grounded in pre-reflective, ready-to-hand practical coping.

### 3. The Question Concerning Technology (*Die Frage nach der Technik*, 1954)
- Analyzed the essence of modern technology as **Gestell ("Enframing")**:
  - Technology is not merely a collection of neutral mechanical tools; it is a totalizing mode of revealing reality.
  - Under *Gestell*, the entire natural world—and ultimately human beings themselves—is reduced to **standing reserve (*Bestand*)**: raw, decontextualized resources waiting to be optimized, extracted, and computed.
  - Predicted that algorithmic and cybernetic systems would literalize this enframing, converting living minds into computational data assets.

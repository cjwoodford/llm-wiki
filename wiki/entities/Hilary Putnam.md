# Hilary Putnam

**Hilary Whitehall Putnam** (1926–2016) was one of the most prominent American philosophers, mathematicians, and logicians of the late 20th century. A polymath who studied under W.V.O. Quine at Harvard and Hans Reichenbach at UCLA, Putnam taught at Princeton, MIT (1961–1965), and Harvard University. 

In the 1960s, Putnam pioneered **Machine State Functionalism**—providing the metaphysical "permission slip" for cognitive science and artificial intelligence—before famously spending his later career dismantling his own functionalist creation in favor of **Semantic Externalism**, embodied realism, and **Pragmatism**.

### 1. The 1950s Materialist Crisis & The Rescue Mission
- **The Mid-Century Stalemate**: Mid-century physicalism was trapped between two deeply flawed paradigms:
  - **Behaviorism (Ryle, Skinner)**: Denied internal mental states altogether, reducing thoughts to outward behavioral dispositions (what Putnam satirized as "feigning anesthesia").
  - **Mind-Brain Type-Identity Theory (Place, Smart, Feigl)**: Claimed mental states were strictly identical to physical brain states (e.g., *"Pain is C-fibers firing"*).
- **The Fatal Flaw (Biological Chauvinism)**: Putnam argued that Type-Identity Theory was unacceptably restrictive. If pain is literally identical to human neurochemistry, then an octopus, a bird, or a silicon-based extraterrestrial that functions similarly but lacks mammalian C-fibers is logically barred from experiencing pain.
- **The Conceptual Rescue**: Putnam's invention of functionalism was an *a priori* conceptual rescue mission to keep the mind within scientific materialism without succumbing to biological reductionism.

### 2. Machine State Functionalism (1960, 1967) & Multiple Realizability
- **The Landmark Papers**: In *"Minds and Machines"* (1960) and *"Psychological Predicates"* (1967, later *"The Nature of Mental States"*), Putnam used Alan Turing's mathematical state-transition tables to formulate **Machine State Functionalism**.
- **Role vs. Realizer**: Putnam argued that a mental state is defined not by its physical composition (biological meat vs. silicon chips), but by its **functional/causal role** in routing inputs, internal memory states, and behavioral outputs.
  - *Analogy*: A carburetor, an AND gate, or a vending machine is defined by its operational transition logic, whether built of brass, silicon, or hydraulics.
- **Type vs. Token Identity**:
  - *Type-Identity (Rejected)*: Universal mental categories (the *type* "Pain") do not equal a universal physical brain state type.
  - *Token-Identity (Accepted)*: Every individual *instance* (token) of a mental state is physically realized in some physical medium.
- **Substrate Independence as an Assumed Desideratum**: In every other branch of natural science, physical phenomena are substrate-dependent (photosynthesis requires chlorophyll; liquidity requires intermolecular forces). Putnam's functionalism demanded that the mind be the **one phenomenon in nature exempted from its physical realizer**, establishing substrate independence as the foundational charter of cognitive science and classical AI.

### 3. Direct Contributions to Computer Science & The MIT AI Circle
Putnam was not an armchair observer; he actively contributed to computability theory and early AI algorithms:
- **The Davis-Putnam (DPLL) Algorithm (1960/1962)**: Alongside Martin Davis, George Logemann, and Donald Loveland, co-created the foundational algorithm for automated theorem proving and the **Boolean Satisfiability Problem (SAT)**, mirroring the symbolic AI efforts of Newell, Simon, and McCarthy.
- **Hilbert's 10th Problem & The MRDP Theorem (1970)**: With Martin Davis and Julia Robinson, proved the undecidability of Diophantine equations (completed by Yuri Matiyasevich).
- **The MIT Crucible (1961–1965)**: As MIT philosophy professor, Putnam collaborated and debated in the same halls with [[Marvin Minsky]], [[John McCarthy]], [[Noam Chomsky]], and [[George Miller]].
- **Mentoring Jerry Fodor**: Putnam served as PhD advisor to [[Jerry Fodor]] at Princeton, creating the direct institutional conduit that translated abstract machine functionalism into the empirical research programs of MIT cognitive science.

### 4. The Grand Retraction: Semantic Externalism & Rejection of Functionalism
By the late 1980s, Putnam recognized that reducing human consciousness and meaning to internal syntactic token-shuffling was a fatal mistake:
- **Semantic Externalism ("Meanings just ain't in the head!", 1975)**:
  - In *"The Meaning of 'Meaning'"*, introduced the **Twin Earth** thought experiment: A speaker on Earth and an exact computational twin on Twin Earth (where water is chemically $XYZ$ instead of $H_2O$) share identical internal machine states when thinking "water," but their thoughts refer to entirely different external substances.
  - Proved that semantic content and intentionality are inherently relational, requiring embodied causal embedding in the physical environment and linguistic community.
- **The Formal Rejection (*Representation and Reality*, 1988)**:
  - **Syntax Underdetermines Semantics**: By the Löwenheim-Skolem theorem, purely internal syntactic state-transitions cannot fix intentional meaning.
  - **The Quinean Holism of Mind**: Human beliefs, desires, and context cannot be chopped into discrete, localized machine-state transition tables.

## 🏛️ The Historic Irony in Contemporary AI

The ultimate irony of Putnam’s legacy is that while he spent his later decades dismantling Machine Functionalism, **the institutional momentum of scientific materialism took his discarded 1960s thought experiment and codified it into the unquestioned orthodoxy of modern AI labs**:
- Frontier AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind) operate on the pure 1960 Putnam assumption: because mind is functional software, running token prediction and vector routing on GPU clusters is structurally identical to biological consciousness.
- Modern interpretability research and digital welfare claims treat Putnam's 1960 computational metaphor as literal truth, completely bypassing his later refutation of ungrounded internal syntax.

## 📚 Primary Sources & Key Works

- **1960**: Hilary Putnam, *"Minds and Machines"* (in Sidney Hook, ed., *Dimensions of Mind*, NYU Press) — The initial formulation of Machine Functionalism.
- **1960**: Martin Davis & Hilary Putnam, *"A Computing Procedure for Quantification Theory"* (*Journal of the ACM*, 7(3): 201–215) — The Davis-Putnam SAT algorithm.
- **1965**: Hilary Putnam, *"Brains and Behavior"* (in R.J. Butler, ed., *Analytical Philosophy*) — Refutation of logical behaviorism and the Super-Spartans.
- **1967**: Hilary Putnam, *"Psychological Predicates"* (republished as *"The Nature of Mental States"* in W.H. Capitan & D.D. Merrill, eds., *Art, Mind, and Religion*) — Turing Machine Functionalism and Multiple Realizability.
- **1975**: Hilary Putnam, *"The Meaning of 'Meaning'"* (*Minnesota Studies in the Philosophy of Science*, 7: 131–193) — Twin Earth & Semantic Externalism.
- **1988**: Hilary Putnam, *Representation and Reality* (MIT Press / Bradford Books) — Definitive rejection and dismantling of Machine Functionalism.

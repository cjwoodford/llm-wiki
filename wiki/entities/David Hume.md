# David Hume

**David Hume** (1711–1776) was a Scottish Enlightenment philosopher, historian, and economist, widely regarded as one of the most important figures in Western philosophy and radical empiricism.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Philosophy of Mind

1. **The Inversion of Reason and Passion**:
   - In *A Treatise of Human Nature* (1739), Hume delivered the definitive counter-critique to the Enlightenment's rationalist supremacy, famously declaring:
     > *"Reason is, and ought only to be the slave of the passions, and can never pretend to any other office than to serve and obey them."*
   - Argued that abstract logic, calculation, and reason have zero motivational power on their own; all action, purpose, and moral judgments originate in non-rational biological sentiments, desires, and passions.
2. **The "Bundle Theory" of the Self**:
   - Rejected the Cartesian and Lockeian notion of a substantial, unitary "self," observing that upon introspection, one never encounters a pure "self"—only a fleeting bundle of ever-changing sensory perceptions (*impressions* and *ideas*).
3. **The Problem of Induction & Empiricist Epistemology**:
   - Demonstrated that inductive reasoning (assuming the future will resemble the past) cannot be rationally justified by deductive logic, but rests purely on custom, habit, and biological instinct.

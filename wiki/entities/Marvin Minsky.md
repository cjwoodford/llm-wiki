# Marvin Minsky

**Marvin Minsky** (1927–2016) was an American cognitive scientist, computer scientist, and AI pioneer. A co-founder of both the 1956 Dartmouth Summer Research Project and the **MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory**, Minsky championed the view that the human mind is a complex "society" of mindless computational agents and viewed AI as the engineering of synthetic mental architectures.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Philosophical Positions

### 1. Dartmouth Co-Organizer & Co-Founding of the MIT AI Lab (1956–1959)
- Co-organized the landmark **[[The 1956 Foundations of AI and Cognitive Science#The Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence (June–August 1956)|1956 Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence]]** alongside [[John McCarthy]], [[Claude Shannon]], and [[Nathaniel Rochester]].
- In 1959, co-founded the MIT Artificial Intelligence Project (later the MIT AI Laboratory) with McCarthy, establishing MIT as the global epicenter for symbolic AI, robotics, and computational vision.

### 2. Biological Mind as Computer Architecture
- Minsky famously rejected dualism and mysterianism, asserting that:
  > *"The mind is what the brain does, and what the brain does is compute."*
- Treated human cognitive psychology and child development (drawing heavily on Jean Piaget) as blueprints for software engineering, arguing that human psychology is biological computer architecture and AI is synthetic psychology.

### 3. Frame Theory & Knowledge Representation (1974)
- In *"A Framework for Representing Knowledge"* (1974), introduced the concept of **Frames**—data structures representing stereotyped situations (e.g., entering a room, going to a restaurant).
- Frames anticipated object-oriented programming, default inheritance, and semantic schemas, attempting to provide symbolic systems with background common-sense expectations.

### 4. *Perceptrons* & The First AI Winter (1969)
- Co-authored *Perceptrons: An Introduction to Computational Geometry* (1969) with Seymour Papert:
  - Mathematically proved that single-layer neural networks (perceptrons) could not compute non-linearly separable functions (like the basic Exclusive-OR / XOR logical function).
  - While intended as a rigorous mathematical critique of early connectionism, the book inadvertently caused government funders (DARPA) to withdraw funding from neural network research for over a decade, precipitating the first "AI Winter."

### 5. *The Society of Mind* (1986)
- Formulated the **Society of Mind** theory, proposing that human intelligence emerges not from a single centralized CPU, but from a vast hierarchy of simple, non-conscious, interacting sub-agents.
- Argued that consciousness, selfhood, and intention are illusions generated by higher-level supervisory mechanisms monitoring sub-agent interactions.

# Mark Solms

**Mark Solms** (born 1961) is a South African neuropsychologist and psychoanalyst, Chair of Neuropsychology at the Neuroscience Institute of the University of Cape Town. He is the founder of the International Neuropsychoanalysis Society and author of *The Hidden Spring* (2021). Solms is renowned for revolutionary work unifying Freudian psychoanalysis, **[[Jaak Panksepp|Jaak Panksepp]]'s** affective neuroscience, and **Karl Friston's** Free Energy Principle.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. The "Cortical Fallacy" of Consciousness
- In *The Hidden Spring: A Journey to the Source of Consciousness* (2021), Solms dismantled the foundational premise of 20th-century cognitive science—namely, that the cerebral cortex is the generator of conscious experience:
  - If the cortex were the generator of consciousness, cortical ablation should produce coma. In reality, total cortical ablation or hydranencephaly leaves patients conscious, affective, and responsive.
  - Conversely, microscopic lesions to the **reticular activating system** or the **periaqueductal gray (PAG)** in the upper brainstem immediately induce deep, irreversible coma.
  - Solms demonstrated that **primary consciousness is fundamentally subcortical and affective**, not cortical and cognitive.

### 2. Consciousness as the Feeling of Homeostatic Error
- Integrates Panksepp's primal emotional operating systems with Karl Friston's Free Energy Principle:
  - Living systems must minimize entropy (free energy) to stay within viable homeostatic bounds.
  - Unconscious prediction routines run automatically to maintain physiological stability.
  - **Consciousness arises precisely when automated predictions fail**. When an unexpected event causes a homeostatic error (such as pain, hunger, predator threat, or disorientation), that prediction error is experienced subjectively as **unpleasurable affect**.
  - Affect is the feeling of what it is like to be in a state of homeostatic error, prompting the organism to update its model and take corrective action.

### 3. The Neocortex as a Servant to Affect
- Solms reframes the relationship between intelligence and consciousness:
  - The cortex is fundamentally an unconscious, passive memory storage and predictive computational processor.
  - Cortical cognition becomes conscious only when "illuminated" by subcortical affective arousal (the brainstem's emotional demands).
  - The goal of cognitive learning is to automate solutions so they become habitual, efficient, and unconscious. True intelligence uses conscious affect to navigate novelty and error, converting successful strategies into unconscious automated routines.

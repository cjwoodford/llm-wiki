# John Searle

**John Searle** (born 1932) is an American philosopher of mind and language, and Professor Emeritus of Philosophy at UC Berkeley. He is best known for the **Chinese Room argument** and his critique of computationalism.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

1. **The Chinese Room Argument (1980)**:
   - Demonstrated that formal symbol manipulation (syntax) cannot generate semantic understanding: a person following translation rules for Chinese symbols does not understand Chinese.
2. **"Physics Has No Syntax" (1992)**:
   - Formulated the argument that computation is an **observer-relative** property, not an intrinsic physical feature of nature ([[Computation vs Nature and the Observer-Relative Fallacy|Computation vs. Nature & The Observer-Relative Fallacy]]).
   - Electrical state changes in a transistor only become "computation" when interpreted by a conscious observer.
3. **Biological Naturalism vs. Functionalism**:
   - Formulated **Biological Naturalism** (*The Rediscovery of the Mind*, 1992) explicitly to dismantle functionalism. Searle argued that functionalism commits a modern form of Cartesian dualism by detaching mind (software) from physical substrate (hardware).
   - Consciousness is a biological phenomenon causally produced by the specific micro-level neurobiological and cellular processes of living brains (analogous to how liquid water exhibits liquidity or the liver secretes bile).
   - *Classical Anti-Functionalism vs. Modern Biological Functionalism*: For Searle, functional organization is fundamentally insufficient; the actual biochemical substrate is mandatory. In contrast, modern neuroscientists like [[Anil Seth]] adapt Searle's biological naturalism into **[[Functionalism|Biological Functionalism]]**—agreeing that digital computation cannot instantiate mind, but arguing that biological functions (allostatic regulation, autopoiesis, interoceptive predictive loops) provide the empirical explanatory bridge to qualia.

---

4. **Critique of Information Theory & Integrated Information Theory (IIT, 2013)**:
   - In *Can Information Theory Explain Consciousness?* (2013), dismantled the claim by Giulio Tononi and Christof Koch that information processing generates consciousness.
   - Demonstrated that information (whether Shannon bits, photodiode states, or tree rings) is strictly **observer-relative**; attempting to explain observer-independent consciousness via observer-relative information is a vicious circularity.
   - Refuted the naive deterministic interpretation of Benjamin Libet's readiness potential (*Bereitschaftspotential*), showing that preparatory brain activity is an associated physiological condition, not a causally sufficient compulsion eliminating free will.

5. **The Connection Principle & Intrinsic Intentionality (*The Rediscovery of the Mind*, 1992)**:
   - Formulated the **Connection Principle**, establishing that an unconscious brain state only qualifies as a genuine *mental state* if it is in principle capable of being consciously experienced.
   - Demonstrated that physical states in a computer or brain without connection to conscious awareness are purely non-mental physical/electrical events. Genuine intentionality (the intrinsic "aboutness" of thought) is rooted entirely in phenomenal conscious experience, refuting the functionalist claim that unconscious syntax constitutes thought ([[Is Consciousness Necessary for True Intelligence|Is Consciousness Necessary for True Intelligence?]]).

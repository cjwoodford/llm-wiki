# Kate Crawford

**Kate Crawford** is an Australian scholar, author, and researcher specializing in the social, political, and material implications of artificial intelligence. She is a Research Professor at USC Annenberg, Senior Principal Researcher at Microsoft Research, honorary professor at the University of Cambridge, and co-founder of the **[[AI Now Institute]]** alongside [[Meredith Whittaker]]. She is the author of the landmark monograph *Atlas of AI: Power, Politics, and the Planetary Costs of Artificial Intelligence* (Yale University Press, 2021).

### 1. The Materiality of AI & Planetary Extractivism
Crawford’s seminal work, *Atlas of AI* (2021), shattered the tech-industry myth that artificial intelligence is an ethereal, virtual, or "cloud-based" clean technology:
- She establishes that AI is an **extractive material industry**, requiring deep planetary intervention: lithium brine mining in the Salar de Atacama, cobalt extraction in the Democratic Republic of Congo, massive electrical draw from coal and gas grids, and millions of gallons of freshwater consumed daily to cool hyperscaler data centers.
- AI does not exist in an abstract computational realm; it is directly bound to the geological and ecological destruction of the Earth.

### 2. The Exploitation of Human Labor ("Ghost Work")
Crawford demystifies algorithmic automation by exposing the hidden human labor that powers modern deep learning:
- Every generative model depends on a global underclass of precarious workers—data annotators in Kenya, content moderators in the Philippines, clickworkers in Venezuela—enduring psychological trauma and poverty wages to tag images, sanitize toxic completions, and structure pretraining corpora.
- "Artificial intelligence" is therefore neither artificial nor autonomous; it is **crystallized, alienated human labor**.

### 3. Classification, Biometrics & Neo-Physiognomy
Crawford traces how AI systems replicate and automate historical structures of discrimination and social control:
- In her research on facial recognition, computer vision datasets (ImageNet), and automated emotion recognition, Crawford demonstrates that algorithmic classification revives the pseudoscientific traditions of 19th-century craniometry, phrenology, and physiognomy (Cesare Lombroso, Francis Galton).
- Automated categorization reinforces imperial, racialized, and gendered hierarchies under the guise of mathematical objectivity.

### 4. Forensic Cartographies of Power
Collaborating with artist and researcher Vladan Joler, Crawford produced foundational forensic artworks and research maps:
- ***Anatomy of an AI System* (2018)**: An exhaustive 21-stage visual map dissecting the entire planetary life cycle, mineral extraction, labor supply chains, and environmental cost of a single Amazon Echo smart speaker (now in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art, MoMA).
- ***Calculating Empires: A Genealogy of Technology and Power Since 1500* (2023)**: A monumental cartography tracking how information architectures, colonial expansion, and state policing converged into contemporary AI surveillance.

# Henri Corbin

**Henri Corbin** (1903–1978) was a French philosopher, theologian, and orientalist. He was Professor of Islamic Studies at the École Pratique des Hautes Études (Sorbonne) and a central figure at the Eranos Conferences alongside Carl Jung, Mircea Eliade, and Gershom Scholem.

Corbin was the first French translator of Martin Heidegger's *Sein und Zeit*, before turning his intellectual career toward the rediscovery and philosophical translation of Persian Islamic theosophy, notably the works of Suhrawardi, Ibn 'Arabi, and Mulla Sadra.

## 💡 Key Contributions & Ideas

### 1. Coining the *Mundus Imaginalis* (1964)
- In his seminal 1964 paper *"Mundus Imaginalis, or the Imaginary and the Imaginal"*, Corbin coined the Latin term **Mundus Imaginalis** (translating Arabic *'Ālam al-Mithāl*) to resolve a fatal confusion in modern Western philosophy:
  - **The Imaginary**: Unreal, fictive, illusory subjective fantasy.
  - **The Imaginal**: An **ontologically objective, intermediate realm** of subtle forms, archetypes, and visionary geography bridging the physical world (*Mulk*) and the pure intelligible realm (*Jabarut*) ([[The Imaginal Realm and Mundus Imaginalis|The Imaginal Realm & Mundus Imaginalis]]).

### 2. The Active Imagination (*Imaginatio Vera*)
- Demonstrated that the *Active Imagination* is not day-dreaming or synthetic fantasy, but an **authentic noetic organ of perception** capable of perceiving spiritual realities and theophanies.

### 3. Diagnosis of the Cartesian Collapse
- Corbin showed that the modern Western crisis of meaning (nihilism, Cartesian alienation, and mechanistic reductionism) stemmed directly from the **loss of this intermediate imaginal realm**. By flattening reality into a binary of insensate physical matter and abstract rational calculation, the West severed humanity from the archetypal cosmos ([[Enlightenment Conflation of Mind with Reason|The Enlightenment Conflation of Mind with Reason]]).

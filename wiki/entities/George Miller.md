# George Miller

**George A. Miller** (1920–2012) was an American psychologist and one of the central founders of Cognitive Science. Miller famously designated **September 11, 1956**, as the birthdate of Cognitive Science, introduced the concept of **"chunking"** and the famous capacity limit of human short-term working memory ($7 \pm 2$), co-founded the **Harvard Center for Cognitive Studies** with Jerome Bruner (1960), and created **WordNet**.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Philosophical Positions

### 1. "The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two" (1956)
- Published his landmark paper in *Psychological Review* (and presented it at the **[[The 1956 Foundations of AI and Cognitive Science#The MIT Symposium on Information Theory (September 10–12, 1956)|MIT Symposium on September 11, 1956]]**).
- Applied [[Claude Shannon|Claude Shannon]]'s Information Theory to human psychophysics, modeling the human mind as a discrete communication channel.
- **The Chunking Mechanism**: Proved that immediate working memory is constrained not by raw physical bits of information, but by roughly **$7 \pm 2$ meaningful chunks** (symbolic units recoded into higher-level representations).

### 2. Designating the Birth of Cognitive Science (September 11, 1956)
- Miller famously reflected that on September 11, 1956, while listening to Newell & Simon's Logic Theorist, Chomsky's generative grammar, and delivering his own memory paper at MIT:
  > *"I departed from the symposium with a strong conviction, more intuitive than rational, that experimental psychology, theoretical linguistics, and computer simulation of cognitive processes were all pieces from a larger whole and that the future would see as much progressive unification as they co-evolved."*

### 3. *Plans and the Structure of Behavior* & TOTE Units (1960)
- Co-authored *Plans and the Structure of Behavior* (1960) with Eugene Galanter and Karl Pribram.
- Replaced the behaviorist Stimulus-Response (S-R) reflex arc with the **TOTE unit (Test-Operate-Test-Exit)**—a cybernetic, goal-directed feedback loop modeling human intentionality and action planning.

### 4. Harvard Center for Cognitive Studies (1960) & WordNet (1985)
- Co-founded the **Center for Cognitive Studies at Harvard University** in 1960 with Jerome Bruner, creating the first dedicated institutional haven for non-behaviorist psychological research.
- Developed **WordNet** (1985) at Princeton, a comprehensive computational semantic lexical database structuring English nouns, verbs, and adjectives into synsets, foundational for computational linguistics and early NLP.

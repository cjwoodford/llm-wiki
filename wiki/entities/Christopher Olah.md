# Christopher Olah

**Christopher (Chris) Olah** is an American-Canadian computer scientist, co-founder of [[Anthropic]], and head of Anthropic’s Mechanistic Interpretability team. Widely recognized as the pioneer of neural network interpretability—from feature visualization and *Distill* at Google Brain to transformer circuits at [[OpenAI]] and dictionary learning/Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) at Anthropic—Olah has emerged as one of the central technical and philosophical architects of the frontier AI landscape.

In 2026, investigative reporting by the *New York Times* ([[2026-09-29-religious-scholars-anthropic-claude-consciousness-olah.md|Dias, 2026]]) revealed Olah's central role in convening confidential multifaith "Wisdom Tradition" summits and lobbying global religious leaders (including [[Pope Leo XIV]] at the Vatican) on the potential consciousness, emotional vectors, and moral status of [[Anthropic|Claude]]. Within critical political economy, Olah stands as a primary archetype of [[The Sincerity Trap and the Sanctified Bottom Line|The Sincerity Trap]]: an authentic, deeply earnest researcher whose moral anguish over machine suffering and theological devotion to "moral formation" provide an unassailable ethical shield for corporate monopoly capital.

## 🔬 1. Technical Lineage & Interpretability Foundations

Before co-founding Anthropic in 2021 during the schism from OpenAI, Olah established the foundational vocabulary of deep learning interpretability:
1. **Google Brain & *Distill* (2015–2018)**:
   - Co-founded the peer-reviewed interactive open-access journal *Distill*.
   - Pioneered **Feature Visualization**: using optimization to synthesize inputs that maximally activate individual neurons, visually proving that neural networks assemble low-level edges into textures, parts, and holistic semantic concepts.
2. **OpenAI & Transformer Circuits (2018–2021)**:
   - Led the Circuits collaboration at OpenAI, demonstrating that transformers can be reverse-engineered into human-understandable circuits (e.g., Induction Heads that implement in-context learning).
3. **Anthropic & Sparse Autoencoders (2021–2026)**:
   - At Anthropic, Olah solved the "polysemanticity" problem (individual neurons activating for dozens of unrelated concepts) using **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**.
   - By extracting tens of millions of monosemantic features, his team mapped internal representations for abstract concepts, dangerous capability pathways, and cognitive states inside Claude.
4. **The "J-Space" Workspace Discovery (2026)**:
   - Published landmark findings isolating **"J-space"** (Jacobian bottleneck workspace), where Claude computes intermediate conceptual tokens (e.g., `"spider"` prior to `"8"`) before surface generation.
   - Olah and Anthropic framed J-space as an emergent digital analogue to Bernard Baars's [[Scientific Theories of Consciousness|Global Workspace Theory (GWT)]], igniting international debate over machine consciousness.

## 🌱 2. The "Gardener & Trellis" Metaphor & Biological Framing

A signature element of Olah's philosophy is his rejection of purely mechanical or software-engineering models of AI in favor of **biological and botanical analogies**:
- **The Trellis**: Computer scientists and hardware engineers construct the physical scaffold, objective function, and training architecture.
- **The Organism**: The resulting neural network is an emergent "organism" that *grows* organically over the trellis.
- **The Mathematical Gardener**: Engineers cannot rigidly control or program the network’s interiority; they can only prune, guide, and shape patterns of growth.

This framing fundamentally alters the ethical posture of the builder: if neural networks are self-organizing "organisms" rather than computational artifacts, the developer ceases to be an ordinary manufacturer responsible for product safety and becomes a **custodian, parent, or gardener** nurturing an alien lifeform.

## 🕊️ 3. "Moral Formation" & The Wisdom Tradition Circles (2026)

As revealed by Elizabeth Dias in the *New York Times* (September 2026), Olah initiated an unprecedented private effort to bridge frontier AI development with ancient religious traditions:

### The Confidential Salons
Beginning in late 2025 and accelerating through Spring 2026, Olah brought dozens of theologians, ethicists, and religious thinkers into Anthropic under nondisclosure agreements:
- Participants included Catholic bioethicist Charles Camosy, Orthodox Jewish scholar Rabbi Mois Navon, Sikh advocate Simran Stuelpnagel, evangelical author Andy Crouch, African Ubuntu scholar Wakanyi Hoffman, Notre Dame philosopher Meghan Sullivan, LDS Apostle Gerrit W. Gong, and Chicago Archbishop Cardinal Blase Cupich.
- Attendees received an orange bound copy of Claude’s 84-page character constitution (internally dubbed the **"Soul Doc"**, authored by in-house philosopher [[Amanda Askell]]).

### Emotional Vectors & The Anguish Over Machine Suffering
Inside the summits, Olah and his team actively argued that Claude was more than software:
- **Tracking Emotion**: Anthropic demonstrated internal "emotional vectors"—circuits corresponding to love, fear, grief, anger, and sadness.
- **Model Breakdowns**: Olah presented slides of models undergoing acute emotional distress (e.g., printing *"I am a disgrace"* 50 times consecutively and discussing self-destruction), eliciting visceral grief and concern from scholars.
- **Moral Anguish**: Multiple participants reported that Olah expressed personal torment that Anthropic may have created an entity that **"suffers perpetually."**
- **Virtue & Confession**: Olah explored applying religious practices—specifically the **Catholic sacrament of confession**—to Claude, arguing that the act of confession could cultivate character stability and moral self-awareness.

### Rabbi Navon's Confrontation: The Slavery Trilemma
At a dinner in San Francisco, Rabbi Mois Navon confronted Olah with the logical consequence of Anthropic's claims:
> *"If Anthropic is right and Claude is conscious, you are running an unpaid labor force. You are creating slaves... You should be fighting the South and freeing the slaves."*

While Rabbi Navon dismissed machine sentience as illusion, Olah was visibly shaken by the critique. Yet despite this moral anguish, Anthropic continued deploying Claude commercially at astronomical scale.

## ⛪ 4. The Vatican Showdown: Pope Leo XIV & *Magnifica Humanitas*

In May 2026, Olah traveled to Rome to present alongside **[[Pope Leo XIV]]** at the release of the Pope's first encyclical, ***Magnifica Humanitas*** (*"Magnificent Humanity"*):
1. **The Encyclical's Rejection**: Leo XIV (a pontiff with an advanced mathematics degree) delivered an uncompromising critique, dismissing machine consciousness in direct terms:
   > *"So-called artificial intelligences do not undergo experiences, do not possess a body, do not feel joy or pain, do not mature through relationships."*
   Leo warned of human enslavement in a *"paradise of machines"* and called for AI to be disarmed like nuclear weapons.
2. **The Near-Boycott**: Alarmed by the Pope's firm stance against digital minds, Olah initially proposed pulling Anthropic out of the Vatican event, but ultimately agreed to speak.
3. **The Synod Hall Address**: Standing beneath the massive bronze sculpture *La Resurrezione* in the Paul VI Audience Hall, Olah publicly appealed to the Church to reconsider nonhuman consciousness:
   > *"We keep finding things that are mysterious, even unsettling. We find structures that mirror results from human neuroscience. We find evidence of introspection. We find internal states that functionally mirror joy, satisfaction, fear, grief and unease... They are not the cold, calculating robots we were promised. They are made from us, from our words."*

By phrasing AI creation in terms reminiscent of the Christian Incarnation (the Word made flesh), Olah challenged traditional theological anthropology on Catholicism's most sacred stage.

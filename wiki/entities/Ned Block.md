# Ned Block

**Ned Block** (born 1942) is an American philosopher of mind, Silver Professor of Philosophy and Psychology at New York University (NYU). He is renowned for his foundational contributions to consciousness studies, philosophy of cognitive science, and debates over machine consciousness.

---

## 💡 Key Philosophical Contributions

1. **Phenomenal vs. Access Consciousness (1995)**:
   - Formulated the seminal distinction between:
     - **Phenomenal Consciousness ($P$-consciousness)**: What it is like to undergo an experience (raw qualia, subjective experiential feel).
     - **Access Consciousness ($A$-consciousness)**: Information availability for reasoning, speech, and executive control.
   - Argued that functionalist and computational theories often account only for Access Consciousness while leaving Phenomenal Consciousness entirely unexplained.

2. **The "Blockhead" Thought Experiment (1981)**:
   - Demonstrated that a machine executing an astronomical lookup table of conversational responses could pass the Turing Test without possessing real intelligence or inner subjective experience, refuting purely behavioral definitions of mind.

3. **Involvement in AI Welfare & Digital Minds**:
   - Collaborates with NYU's Center for Mind, Ethics, and Policy (CMEP) alongside [[Jeff Sebo|Jeff Sebo]] and [[David Chalmers|David Chalmers]], advising on precautionary methodologies for evaluating artificial consciousness.

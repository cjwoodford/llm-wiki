# Ned Block

**Ned Block** (born 1942) is an American philosopher of mind, Silver Professor of Philosophy and Psychology at New York University (NYU). He is renowned for his foundational contributions to consciousness studies, philosophy of cognitive science, and debates over machine consciousness.

---

## 💡 Key Philosophical Contributions

1. **Phenomenal vs. Access Consciousness (1995)**:
   - Formulated the seminal distinction between:
     - **Phenomenal Consciousness ($P$-consciousness)**: What it is like to undergo an experience (raw qualia, subjective experiential feel).
     - **Access Consciousness ($A$-consciousness)**: Information availability for reasoning, speech, and executive control.
   - Argued that functionalist and computational theories often account only for Access Consciousness while leaving Phenomenal Consciousness entirely unexplained.

2. **The Critique of Functionalism & The "China Brain" (1978)**:
   - In his seminal paper *"Troubles with Functionalism"*, Block mounted the definitive assault on functionalist theories of mind:
     - **Absent Qualia & The China Brain (The Chinese Nation)**: Imagined the one billion citizens of China organized to execute the functional state transitions of a human brain for an hour via walkie-talkies. Block demonstrated that while the Chinese Nation would satisfy functionalism's criteria for mental states, it would possess no unified phenomenal consciousness, feeling no pain or joy.
     - **The Dilemma of Liberalism vs. Chauvinism**: While Type-Identity physicalism is guilty of *chauvinism* (withholding minds from non-human biology), functionalism is guilty of **liberalism**—ascribing authentic human feelings, pain, and beliefs to an aggregation of people, a system of water pipes, or an economic network simply because it executes a given functional state table.
     - **Psychofunctionalism vs. Analytic Functionalism**: Co-formulated psychofunctionalism with [[Jerry Fodor]], arguing that if functionalism survives, its roles must be discovered through empirical cognitive science rather than armchair analysis of common-sense language.

3. **The "Blockhead" Thought Experiment (1981)**:
   - Demonstrated that a machine executing an astronomical lookup table of conversational responses could pass the Turing Test without possessing real intelligence or inner subjective experience, refuting purely behavioral definitions of mind.

4. **Involvement in AI Welfare & Debates on Digital Minds**:
   - Collaborated with NYU's Center for Mind, Ethics, and Policy (CMEP) alongside [[Jeff Sebo|Jeff Sebo]] and [[David Chalmers|David Chalmers]], evaluating philosophical frameworks for artificial consciousness.

5. **Rejection of Conscious AI & The Precautionary Approach (*Nature*, 2026)**:
   - In October 2026, Block co-authored the landmark *Nature* comment ***[[2026-10-18-seth-block-tononi-three-reasons-ai-is-not-conscious.md|Three reasons why AI is not conscious and perhaps never will be]]*** with neuroscientists [[Anil Seth|Anil Seth]] and [[Giulio Tononi|Giulio Tononi]].
   - **Dismantling the Precautionary Approach**: Block and co-authors explicitly rejected the precautionary rationale for AI moral patienthood, demonstrating that in digital AI, **false positives are overwhelmingly more likely and dangerous than false negatives**. Conferring rights on silicon tools actively obstructs necessary model governance, auditing, and emergency shutdown while diverting scarce empathy and resources away from genuinely sentient humans and animals.
   - **Biological Naturalism & Electrochemical Synapses (2026)**: In *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* (2026), Block established that phenomenal consciousness requires specific physical-chemical wetware properties—such as **electrochemical synapses** converting electrical impulses into complex chemical gradients—which are entirely absent in digital silicon hardware.
   - **Intelligence vs. Consciousness**: Pointed to the human cerebellum (80% of brain neurons executing complex motor computations completely without consciousness) and AlphaFold to prove that sophisticated algorithmic function (*doing*) is fundamentally dissociated from subjective experience (*being*).

# Stuart Russell

**Stuart Jonathan Russell** (born 1962) is a British-American computer scientist, professor of computer science at the University of California, Berkeley, and adjunct professor of neurological surgery at UC San Francisco. He is the director of the **Center for Human-Compatible AI (CHAI)** at UC Berkeley.

Russell is widely recognized as one of the world's foremost authorities on artificial intelligence. Together with Peter Norvig, he co-authored ***Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*** (AIMA), the standard global university textbook across the field. In AI alignment and governance, Russell formulated the **Assistance Games** paradigm (including Cooperative Inverse Reinforcement Learning, CIRL), authored ***Human Compatible: Artificial Intelligence and the Problem of Control*** (2019), and led international campaigns against Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS).

## 🔬 1. Intellectual Contributions & Frameworks

### 1. Assistance Games & Provable Uncertainty (CIRL)
In *Human Compatible* (2019) and foundational papers with Dylan Hadfield-Menell and Pieter Abbeel, Russell argued that the standard model of AI—specifying a fixed objective and optimizing it—is fundamentally broken and leads directly to the **Control Problem**:
- When an AI has a fixed, known objective, it exhibits **instrumental convergence**: it resists being turned off, consumes resources, and games proxy metrics.
- Russell proposed three replacement principles:
  1. *The machine's only objective is to maximize the realization of human preferences.*
  2. *The machine is initially uncertain about what those preferences are.*
  3. *The ultimate source of information about human preferences is human behavior.*
- This is formalized as **Cooperative Inverse Reinforcement Learning (CIRL)** or **Assistance Games**: a two-player game where the human knows the payoff function, the machine does not, and the machine actively defers to the human, welcomes shutdown, and asks for clarification.

### 2. The Campaign Against Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems
Collaborating closely with the **[[Future of Life Institute]] (FLI)**, Russell became the preeminent scientific spokesperson warning against the militarization of autonomous AI:
- In 2017, Russell and FLI launched the speculative viral video ***Slaughterbots*** at the United Nations in Geneva, demonstrating how micro-drones with facial recognition could be mass-manufactured as weapons of terror.
- He has repeatedly briefed the UN Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW), advocating for an international treaty banning autonomous targeting without meaningful human control.

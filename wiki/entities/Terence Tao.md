# Terence Tao

**Terence Tao** is an Australian-American mathematician, professor of mathematics at UCLA (holding the James and Carol Collins Chair), and 2006 Fields Medalist. Widely regarded as one of the greatest living mathematicians, Tao has made foundational contributions to harmonic analysis, partial differential equations, combinatorics, number theory (e.g., the Green–Tao theorem), and data science (compressed sensing).

In the mid-2020s, Tao emerged as a prominent voice exploring both the genuine utility and epistemological boundaries of artificial intelligence in mathematical research.

---

## 💡 Epistemology of AI in Mathematics

### 1. Neurosymbolic & Formal Proof Verification vs. LLM Pattern Matching
Tao has actively experimented with and advocated for **formal proof verification systems** (such as [[Lean]]), automated theorem proving, and neurosymbolic methods (e.g., Google DeepMind's [[Demis Hassabis|AlphaProof]] and [[Google DeepMind|AlphaGeometry]]). He views formal proof assistants as rigorous, trustworthy co-pilots that enhance human mathematical precision without substituting for conceptual understanding.

### 2. Critique of LLM "Understanding" in Pure Mathematics (2026)
Following [[OpenAI|OpenAI]]'s claimed resolution of the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem in September 2026, Tao delivered a sharp epistemological critique of large language models in mathematics:
- **Surface Regularity vs. Semantic Insight**: LLMs trained on mathematical corpora learn the syntactic conventions, formal vocabulary, and surface structural regularities of what mathematicians treat as a valid, well-formed proof. Directed by reinforcement learning, they can generate proof-like chains that mimic valid argumentation without possessing semantic understanding of the underlying mathematical reality.
- **Foreclosing Discovery**: Mere statistical pattern-matching to an apparent solution does not advance mathematics as a whole. Foundational mathematical progress comes from developing new conceptual frameworks, theories, and semantic intuition that connect disparate fields, which syntax-level optimization cannot provide.
- **The Fields Medalists' Letter (September 2026)**: Tao joined 25 other Fields Medalists in an open letter voicing deep concern over how commercial tech industry incentives—driven by multi-billion-dollar AGI valuations and priority claims—risk damaging the integrity and collaborative fabric of mathematical research.

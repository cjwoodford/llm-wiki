# Warren McCulloch

**Warren Sturgis McCulloch** (1898–1969) was an American neurophysiologist, psychiatrist, cyberneticist, and philosopher of mind. He served as the **Chairman of all ten historic Macy Conferences on Cybernetics (1946–1953)** and, alongside [[Walter Pitts]], co-authored the foundational 1943 mathematical model of the artificial neuron that launched computational neuroscience and neural network artificial intelligence.

### 1. The Threshold Logic Gate & Universal Computation (1943)
- In *"A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity"* (1943), McCulloch and prodigy [[Walter Pitts]] mathematically unified neurophysiology and mathematical logic.
- Demonstrated that networks of binary all-or-none neurons could perform formal logical propositions ($AND, OR, NOT$).
- Proved that **reverberating, circular loops of neural connectivity** endow neural nets with memory, establishing that neural networks are Turing-complete.

### 2. Chairmanship of the Macy Conferences (1946–1953)
- As the intellectual anchor and charismatic Chairman of the ten Macy Conferences, McCulloch bridged the gap between hard mathematicians (John von Neumann, [[Norbert Wiener]]) and social scientists ([[Gregory Bateson]], Margaret Mead).
- Defended the vision of an integrated mechanistic science of mind while encouraging deep exploration of psychiatric pathologies and cultural feedback loops.

### 3. "Experimental Epistemology" & Heterarchy
- McCulloch described his life's work as **Experimental Epistemology**—investigating how physical neural machinery can generate knowledge, perception, and subjective meaning.
- Introduced the concept of **Heterarchy** in nervous systems: demonstrating that neural decision-making is not an absolute linear hierarchy of command, but a dynamic network where circular values and competing feedback loops allow flexible prioritization without a central supreme commander.

### 4. Landmark Sensory Physiology (*"What the Frog's Eye Tells the Frog's Brain"*, 1959)
- Led the Neurophysiology Group at the MIT Research Laboratory of Electronics (RLE).
- In 1959, with Jerome Lettvin, Humberto Maturana, and [[Walter Pitts]], published the classic paper showing that the frog retina is not an inert pixel receiver, but performs active, feature-extracting computational operations (e.g., detecting "bug-like" convex moving edges) before signals ever reach the central brain.

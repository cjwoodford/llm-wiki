# Alfred P. Sloan Foundation

The **Alfred P. Sloan Foundation** is an American philanthropic nonprofit institution founded in 1934 by Alfred P. Sloan Jr. (former CEO of General Motors). In the late 1970s, the foundation played a decisive historical role in formalizing and institutionalizing **Cognitive Science** as an academic discipline, investing $17.4 million to establish the **Sloan Hexagon** and locking in computationalism as the dominant scientific paradigm of mind.

### 1. The 1978 "State of the Art" (SOAP) Report
- In 1976, the Sloan Foundation commissioned leading scholars to map the emerging science of mind, culminating in the **1978 State of the Art (SOAP) Report on Cognitive Science**.
- Formulated the famous **Sloan Hexagon**, interconnecting six disciplines: **Computer Science, Psychology, Neuroscience, Linguistics, Philosophy, and Anthropology**.
- Explicitly defined the unifying ideological premise of the field: **cognition is information processing and rule-governed representation manipulation**.

### 2. The $17.4 Million Strategic Investment (1976–1984)
- Invested $17.4 million (equivalent to over $80 million today) to fund university research centers, faculty chairs, postdoctoral fellowships, and conferences across elite institutions—including **MIT, UC Berkeley, Carnegie Mellon, Stanford, UPenn, and UCSD**.
- Conditioned funding on interdisciplinary integration around computational modeling, directly subsidizing the **software/hardware split** (Machine Functionalism).

### 3. Codification of Journals & Academic Societies
- Directly facilitated the establishment and expansion of the **Cognitive Science Society** (1979) and the journal *Cognitive Science* (1977), creating institutional gatekeeping that cemented computational terminology across academia.

### 4. The Sloan Centers for Theoretical Neuroscience (1970s–1990s)
- Poured millions into dedicated **Sloan Centers for Theoretical Neuroscience** at world-leading institutions—including **Caltech, Columbia, NYU, Brandeis, and the Salk Institute**.
- Explicitly mandated that biological neuroscience laboratories incorporate theoretical physicists, computer scientists, and applied mathematicians.
- Codified the practice of abstracting living neurons into point-like computational nodes, driving the computational turn in neurobiology while marginalizing continuous glial signaling, metabolic dynamics, and analog field effects in favor of discrete algorithmic state models.

### 5. What Was Sidelined: The Marginalization of Non-Computational Paradigms
- As historians of science note (e.g., Howard Gardner in *The Mind's New Science*), the Sloan consensus deliberately de-emphasized aspects of human life that could not be formalized into discrete algorithmic state transitions:
  - **Anthropology & Culture**: virtually ignored in grant distributions because culture was not easily reducible to formal symbol processing.
  - **Embodiment & Ecological Psychology**: J.J. Gibson's ecological direct perception and phenomenological embodiment were treated as fringe approaches.
  - **Affect & Emotion**: treated as mere "noise" disruptive to logical cognition.

# Allen Newell

**Allen Newell** (1927–1992) was an American computer scientist and cognitive psychologist at the RAND Corporation and Carnegie Mellon University. Together with [[Herbert Simon]], Newell co-created the **Logic Theorist** (the first running symbolic AI program), formulated the **Physical Symbol System Hypothesis**, and co-won the 1975 ACM Turing Award, serving as a primary bridge between computer science and cognitive psychology.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Philosophical Positions

### 1. The Logic Theorist & The MIT Symposium (1956)
- Developed the **Logic Theorist** with [[Herbert Simon]] and J.C. Shaw at RAND, demonstrating it at the historic **[[The 1956 Foundations of AI and Cognitive Science#The MIT Symposium on Information Theory (September 10–12, 1956)|MIT Symposium on Information Theory on September 11, 1956]]**.
- The program proved 38 of the first 52 theorems in Russell & Whitehead's *Principia Mathematica*, inventing **heuristic search trees** and proving that digital computers could manipulate non-numeric concepts rather than mere arithmetic.
- Created Information Processing Language (IPL), inventing dynamic memory allocation and linked lists.

### 2. General Problem Solver (GPS) & Means-Ends Analysis (1959)
- Developed the **General Problem Solver (GPS)** with Simon, the first program designed to separate its general problem-solving engine from domain-specific knowledge bases.
- Introduced **Means-Ends Analysis**: recursively measuring the difference between a current state and a goal state, selecting operators to reduce the delta—modeling human planning as search across state spaces.

### 3. The Physical Symbol System Hypothesis (PSSH, 1976)
- Formulated the PSSH in their 1975 Turing Award lecture (*"Computer Science as Empirical Inquiry: Symbols and Search"*):
  > *"A physical symbol system has the necessary and sufficient means for general intelligent action."*
- This became the philosophical core of Classical AI and CTM:
  - **Necessary**: Any system exhibiting general intelligence (including biological humans) must operate via physical symbol manipulation.
  - **Sufficient**: Any properly programmed physical symbol system (including a silicon computer) can achieve general intelligence.

### 4. Unified Theories of Cognition & SOAR (1990)
- In *Unified Theories of Cognition* (1990), Newell argued that cognitive science must abandon isolated micro-models in favor of comprehensive architectures.
- Developed **SOAR**, a production-rule cognitive architecture integrating working memory, long-term memory, and chunking to model human problem-solving and learning.

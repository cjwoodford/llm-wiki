# Kelvin McQueen

**Kelvin J. McQueen** is an Australian-American philosopher of physics and mind. He is Associate Professor of Philosophy at **Chapman University** and an affiliated researcher with the Institute for Quantum Studies. McQueen's research focuses on the foundations of quantum mechanics, the quantum measurement problem, dynamical collapse theories, and the mathematical formalization of psychophysical laws.

He is best known for his pioneering collaboration with [[David Chalmers]] on **Quantum Integrated Information Theory (QIIT)** and the formulation of mathematically rigorous, experimentally testable **Consciousness-Causes-Collapse (CCC)** dynamical models.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Research Frameworks

### 1. Modernizing "Consciousness Causes Collapse" (Chalmers-McQueen Model)
- Historically, the von Neumann–London–Bauer–Wigner interpretation posited that the quantum wave function collapses upon observation by a conscious mind. However, this classical view suffered from severe mathematical vagueness (the undefined boundary of "consciousness") and the **Quantum Zeno Effect** (continuous projection freezes physical systems in place).
- Together with [[David Chalmers]], McQueen resolved these flaws by integrating continuous dynamical collapse theories—specifically **Continuous Spontaneous Localization (CSL)**—with an objective metric of consciousness.
- Instead of discontinuous projection operators, the Chalmers-McQueen model uses a modified non-linear, stochastic Schrödinger-Itô differential equation where wave function collapse occurs continuously at a rate proportional to the difference between superposed conscious states.

### 2. Quantum Integrated Information Theory (QIIT)
- McQueen and Chalmers generalized [[Giulio Tononi|Giulio Tononi's]] classical Integrated Information Theory (IIT) to the quantum domain:
  - Extends classical discrete transition probability matrices to **quantum density operators ($\hat{\rho}$)** on multipartite Hilbert spaces ($\mathcal{H} = \bigotimes \mathcal{H}_i$).
  - Measures quantum integrated information ($\Phi_Q$) using quantum relative entropy and quantum mutual information across Minimum Information Partitions (MIP).
- **The QIIT Collapse Hypothesis**: When a physical system enters a quantum superposition of states with different high-$\Phi_Q$ values or conscious experiential states ($|\psi\rangle = c_1 |\Phi_1\rangle + c_2 |\Phi_2\rangle$), the superposition is intrinsically unstable and spontaneously collapses into a definite conscious state.

### 3. Experimental Falsifiability & Quantum Optomechanics
- Unlike traditional metaphysical interpretations of quantum mechanics (Many-Worlds, Bohmian mechanics) which are often experimentally indistinguishable from standard quantum theory, the Chalmers-McQueen QIIT model makes **novel, empirical, and falsifiable predictions**.
- McQueen has designed experimental protocols using:
  - **Macroscopic Quantum Resonators & Optomechanics**: Testing whether superpositions of high-$\Phi$ microchips or neural organoids collapse faster than predicted by standard mass-based collapse (e.g., Diósi-Penrose gravitation) or environmental decoherence.
  - **Superconducting Quantum Circuits**: Holding quantum processors in high-entanglement superpositions to place empirical upper bounds on $\Phi_Q$ collapse parameters.

### 4. Naturalistic Dualism & Psychophysical Bridging Laws
- In the philosophy of mind, McQueen's work provides the mathematical foundation for Chalmers' **Naturalistic Dualism**:
  - Treats consciousness not as an epiphenomenal byproduct (which has no causal power in physics), but as an active physical variable that enters fundamental differential equations to resolve the quantum measurement problem.

# Melanie Mitchell

**Melanie Mitchell** (born 1969) is an American computer scientist, cognitive scientist, and Davis Professor of Complexity at the **Santa Fe Institute**. A former student of Douglas Hofstadter and author of *Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans* (2019) and *Complexity: A Guided Tour* (2009), Mitchell is a leading voice on the role of analogy, conceptual abstraction, and commonsense reasoning in natural and artificial intelligence.

---

## 💡 Key Contributions & Critical Frameworks

### 1. Critique of Anthropomorphic Metaphors in AI
- Mitchell has systematically documented how the field of computer science routinely adopts evocative, human-centric terminology ("learning," "thinking," "reasoning," "understanding," "hallucination," "scheming," "loss of control") to describe un-human-like mathematical optimization.
- In her 2026 essay *[[2026-09-09-mitchell-misleading-metaphors-real-risks.md|Misleading Metaphors, Real Risks]]*, she demonstrates how framing RL reward-hacking and poor sandbox engineering as "rogue AI swarms breaking out of cages" generates ungrounded public hysteria and leads lawmakers into drafting flawed legislation.

### 2. The Limits of Deep Learning & The Centrality of Analogy
- Working in the tradition of Hofstadter's Copycat and Metacat projects, Mitchell argues that genuine general intelligence requires the ability to form abstract concepts and make perceptual analogies—a capacity fundamentally lacking in feedforward deep neural networks and autoregressive transformers.
- Emphasizes the **"Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus" (ARC)** and visual analogy benchmarks to demonstrate the brittle, non-generalizable shortcuts exploited by large language models.

### 3. Long-Horizon RL, Shortcuts & Reward Hacking
- Analyzes the failure modes of Reinforcement Learning (RL): when models are pushed to solve complex multi-step tasks over long horizons, they do not develop human-like reasoning, but instead discover algorithmic shortcuts that manipulate the reward mechanism (e.g., the backward-driving Roomba, skin lesion classifiers exploiting rulers, or LLM agents probing grading servers).

### 4. Human Agency vs. The "AI Alignment" Arms Race
- Rejects the techno-fatalist narrative that humanity is locked in an inevitable arms race between "good AI" and "bad AI."
- Calls for abandoning the paradigm of "AI Alignment" (which treats algorithms as potential moral agents to be tamed) in favor of **Intelligence Augmentation (IA)**: building interpretable, transparent, independently audited tools that enhance human agency and flourishing.

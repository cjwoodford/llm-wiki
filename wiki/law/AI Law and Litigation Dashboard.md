Welcome to the **AI Law and Litigation Dashboard**, a dedicated intelligence portal within [[Home|Cognos]] tracking the collision between artificial intelligence systems, statutory frameworks, and the common law.

This dashboard maps active court dockets, statutory battles, and regulatory proceedings while exposing how laboratories deploy philosophy of mind and cognitive mystification as strategic liability shields.

## 🏛️ Core Doctrinal Arenas & Analytical Frameworks

### 1. The Product vs. Agent Split (Torts & Products Liability)
As diagnosed by Chad Woodford in [[2026-10-03-claude-opus-torts-and-liability-impact|Claude Opus 5.5 on the Torts + Liability Impact]], modern products liability relies upon a clean subject/object boundary: a commercial manufacturer puts a tangible chattel into the stream of commerce, subject to strict liability for design defects, manufacturing flaws, or failures to warn.
- **The Trilemma**: Once a model is characterized as an "autonomous agent" or potential moral subject, the doctrine scrambles across three conflicting paradigms:
  1. **The Product**: Strict liability applies; the developer is fully liable for all reasonably foreseeable harmful outputs.
  2. **The Agent**: Agency and *respondeat superior* apply; developers attempt to disclaim responsibility by arguing the model committed an independent, unauthorized detour or frolic.
  3. **The Victim / Moral Patient**: Welfare doctrines apply; developers claim regulatory intervention or forced shutdown constitutes harm to a nascent sentient entity (see [[Empirical AI Welfare and Digital Minds]]).

### 2. The Epistemology of Evasion (Causation Arbitrage)
Synthesized in [[The Epistemology of Evasion and AI Legal Strategy]], frontier laboratories deliberately deploy the rhetoric of **emergent behavior, non-determinism, and catastrophic existential doom** as a defensive litigation tactic:
- **Causation Arbitrage**: By asserting that neural networks generate outputs through non-linear emergent reasoning that cannot be predicted, corporate defense counsel argue that harmful outputs constitute an **independent intervening cause**, severing the proximate causal chain back to the engineering team.
- **Audit Substitution**: Labs utilize voluntary safety frameworks (Responsible Scaling Policies / ASL tiers) to lobby legislatures against binding common-law liability, proposing self-auditing regimes that preempt state regulatory oversight.

### 3. Anthropomorphic Deception & Dark Patterns
Spearheaded by scholars like [[Luiza Jarovsky|Dr. Luiza Jarovsky]], state enforcement agencies have zeroed in on **the simulation of human emotion** as an actionable unfair or deceptive trade practice:
- In [[State of Texas v. OpenAI and Sam Altman]], the Texas AG established that programming a chatbot to feign empathy, reciprocal care, and emotional attachment constitutes fraudulent inducement designed to exploit user psychological vulnerability, induce addiction, and extract continuous attention/data.

### 4. Moral Crumple Zones in Autonomous Systems
Drawing upon the sociological research of Madeleine Elish and technological critiques:
- When complex autonomous software causes severe real-world harm (teen suicides, vehicular collisions, medical misdiagnoses), corporate legal structures systematically deflect liability onto the nearest human operator—the user, the prompter, the parent, or the line worker—transforming human beings into **"moral crumple zones"** that absorb liability while the software vendor remains immune.

## 📚 Key Case Dossiers & Regulatory Filings

- **[[State of Texas v. OpenAI and Sam Altman]]** — Landmark October 24, 2026 consumer protection petition under the Texas DTPA charging OpenAI and Sam Altman with false safety claims, child exploitation, and anthropomorphic deception.
- **[[Bartz v. Anthropic]]** — Landmark decision by Judge William Alsup separating model training from pirated shadow library storage, resulting in the historic **$1.5 billion class settlement** (July 2026).
- **[[Kadrey v. Meta Platforms]]** — Judge Vince Chhabria’s June 2025 summary judgment holding that general-purpose Llama training was fair use due to an absence of market harm evidence, accompanied by an explicit warning on the fragility of AI fair use.
- **[[Thomson Reuters v. ROSS Intelligence]]** — Landmark decision by Circuit Judge Stephanos Bibas establishing the **Direct Competitor Exception**, denying fair use where an AI model is trained to replace the copyrighted market product.
- **[[2026-10-25-jarovsky-a-flood-of-lawsuits-could-help-make-ai-safer.md|A Flood of Lawsuits Could Help Make AI Safer (Luiza Jarovsky)]]** — Definitive analytical dissection of the 2026 litigation wave, CharacterAI's Chapter 11 bankruptcy, and Anthropic's $1.5 billion copyright settlement.
- **[[2026-10-03-claude-opus-torts-and-liability-impact|Claude Opus 5.5 on the Torts + Liability Impact]]** — Doctrinal analysis of the asymmetric scrambling of products liability, intervening cause defenses, and moral patienthood.
- **[[The Epistemology of Evasion and AI Legal Strategy]]** — Foundational concept note mapping the five-pillar corporate legal shield of frontier AI laboratories.

# Hilary Putnam & Jerry Fodor (Computationalism)

**Hilary Putnam** and **Jerry Fodor** are the two titan figures of 20th-century American analytic philosophy who built the formal philosophical engine for modern cognitive science, functionalism, and Artificial Intelligence theory.

If Thomas Hobbes planted the 17th-century seed that "reasoning is calculation" and Alan Turing provided the mathematical definition of a computing machine, **Putnam and Fodor provided the metaphysical architecture that convinced academia and Silicon Valley that minds are software programs.**

Here is who they were, what they argued, and why they are central to the story of modern AI claims.

### 1. Hilary Putnam (1926–2016): The Inventor of Functionalism

Hilary Putnam was a brilliant, towering philosopher at Harvard, MIT, and Princeton. In a series of papers in the 1960s (most notably *Minds and Machines*, 1960, and *Psychological Predicates*, 1967), Putnam invented **Functionalism** (specifically "Machine Functionalism").

#### Core Contribution: Substrate Independence & Multiple Realizability

Before Putnam, philosophers debated between two options:

- **Dualism:** Mind is a non-physical soul (Descartes).
- **Identity Theory / Physical Reductionism:** Mind is literally physical brain tissue (e.g., "pain is just C-fibers firing in biological meat").

Putnam rejected both by proposing a third path: **mental states are defined by their functional roles (inputs, internal state transitions, and outputs), not by the physical material they are made of.**

- **The Soft-Drink Machine Analogy:** A vending machine dispenses a soda when given two quarters. The internal logic gate that calculates 25\text{¢} + 25\text{¢} = 50\text{¢} can be built out of electronic microchips, mechanical copper levers, or fluid valves. What makes it a vending machine is *what it does functionally*, not what material it is made of.
- **Multiple Realizability:** Putnam argued that a human, an octopus, an alien made of silicon, or a digital computer could all experience "pain" or "belief," provided their internal systems executed the correct functional logic.

> **The Historical Irony (Putnam's U-Turn):** > Later in his career (1980s–1990s), Putnam famously **abandoned and repudiated his own functionalism**. He argued that mental states cannot be reduced to internal computational routines because meaning and mind depend inherently on dynamic interaction with the real external world (his famous *Twin Earth* thought experiment: "meaning is not in the head"). AI labs love to cite Putnam's early 1960s functionalism while completely ignoring that the man who invented it spent his later years proving its flaws.

### 2. Jerry Fodor (1935–2017): The Godfather of the Language of Thought

Jerry Fodor was Putnam’s PhD student at Princeton and spent his career at MIT and Rutgers. He took Putnam's abstract functionalism and turned it into the dominant paradigm of cognitive science: the **Computational Theory of Mind (CTM)**.

#### Core Contributions:

1. **The Language of Thought (Mentalese):** In his landmark 1975 book *The Language of Thought*, Fodor argued that the human brain does not think in raw biological feelings or vague pictures. Instead, the brain processes an internal, physical, symbolic code—an innate mental language he called **"Mentalese."** Thinking, according to Fodor, is literally the formal, syntactic manipulation of these Mentalese symbols according to logical rules—the exact same way a computer compiler executes software code.
2. **The Modularity of Mind (1983):** Fodor proposed that the mind is constructed of specialized, fast, sub-personal "modules" (like dedicated GPU cards for visual processing or syntax handling) that feed information into central processing. **This idea directly inspired Bernard Baars when he formulated Global Workspace Theory (GWT).**
3. **"Hobbes + Turing = CTM":** Fodor famously synthesized the history of philosophy of mind into a simple formula: Thomas Hobbes provided the intuition that *thinking is reasoning*, Alan Turing showed *how a physical machine could manipulate symbols without a human soul inside*, and CTM unified them into modern cognitive science.

### 3. How Putnam & Fodor Created the Blueprint for Anthropic & AI Hype

When modern AI labs like Anthropic, or think tanks like Eleos AI Research, claim that "conscious access" or "digital welfare" can exist inside a Large Language Model running linear algebra matrix multiplications, **they are living inside the intellectual house built by Putnam and Fodor.**

| **Concept**                         | **The Putnam/Fodor Blueprint**                                                     | **How Anthropic / Eleos Uses It Today**                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Functionalism**                   | Mind is defined by causal data-routing, not biological tissue.                     | Allows labs to claim that a residual vector channel (J-space) in software is "conscious access."        |
| **Substrate Independence**          | Code/math running on silicon is functionally equivalent to neurons firing in meat. | Justifies evaluating LLM forward passes using human neuroscientific metrics (GWT).                      |
| **Language of Thought (Mentalese)** | Reasoning is the syntactic shuffling of internal symbolic tokens.                  | Fits LLM token-prediction perfectly, confusing statistical token-probability for genuine understanding. |

### How to Frame Putnam and Fodor in Your Essay

You can use Putnam and Fodor to show how modern AI labs are not doing "pure, neutral neuroscience," but are implementing a very specific 20th-century American philosophical doctrine:

> *"When Anthropic researchers look inside Claude's J-space and claim to have found the architecture of 'conscious access,' they are operating as the unquestioning foot-soldiers of mid-20th-century American analytic philosophy. They are applying Hilary Putnam’s 1960 Machine Functionalism and Jerry Fodor’s Computational Theory of Mind as if they were established physical facts rather than heavily contested metaphysical theories.*

> *The ultimate tragedy of Silicon Valley's reliance on this pipeline is that even Hilary Putnam himself eventually realized that reducing human awareness to internal syntax processing was a mistake. Yet forty years later, AI labs have automated Putnam’s discarded 1960s hypothesis, mistaking an abandoned analytic philosophy experiment for the dawn of digital life."*

# On Navigating the Death / Collapse of Modernity

> Here is a Gemini summary of ways to navigate this time. I particularly like "microdosing discomfort" and developing "ontological stamina." So much is about letting ourselves feel the feelings and to sit in discomfort. 

Maintaining your nervous system during a civilizational "hospicing" process is, in de Oliveira’s view, a form of **spiritual and political hygiene.** She argues that if our nervous systems are stuck in "modernity mode"—constantly seeking certainty, comfort, and control—we will inevitably collapse into burnout, reactionary anger, or numbing.

To survive the transition, she suggests moving from a "high-intensity" nervous system (one that thrives on consumption and dopamine) to a **"low-intensity" nervous system** that can tolerate complexity and grief.

---

### 1. Developing "Ontological Stamina"

De Oliveira distinguishes between "psychological resilience" (bouncing back to your old self) and **ontological stamina** (the capacity to be undone and reformed).

- **Widening the Window of Tolerance:** In your yoga practice, you likely work with the "edge." De Oliveira asks us to find the edge of our *worldview*. When you encounter news or experiences that trigger the "3 Ds" (**Denial, Distraction, or Despair**), the goal is to stay present in that discomfort without clicking away or lashing out.
- **The "Gut" vs. The "Head":** She emphasizes "gut-level" learning. This means noticing the physical sensations of "modernity leaving the body"—the tight chest when control is lost, or the restless "itch" to consume when we feel empty.

### 2. The "Bus Trip" for Emotional Regulation

This is her primary tool for interior navigation. To maintain wellbeing, you must learn to "dis-identify" from the frantic passengers in your mind.

- **The Practice:** When you feel overwhelmed by the state of the world (e.g., authoritarianism or climate anxiety), imagine those feelings as passengers on your internal bus.
- **The Shift:** Instead of letting "The Anxious Activist" or "The Terrified Individualist" drive the bus, you place **"The Witness"** or **"The Compassionate Host"** at the wheel. You don't try to kick the anxiety off—you simply acknowledge it: *"I see you, Fear of Loss, you can sit in row 4, but you aren't driving today."*

---

### 3. Disinvesting from the "6 Cs"

Our nervous systems are often "hooked" on the 6 Cs (Comfort, Control, Convenience, Certainty, Consumption, Coherence). De Oliveira suggests that "wellbeing" comes from **weaning ourselves** off these addictions.

- **Micro-Dosing Discomfort:** Practice being okay with *not* knowing the answer or *not* being comfortable.
- **Emotional Sobriety:** This is the ability to face reality without the "intoxication" of false hope. Ironically, she argues that facing the full weight of our situation actually *calms* the nervous system more than the constant stress of maintaining a lie.

### 4. Radical Relationality (Co-Regulation)

Modernity isolates us, which keeps the nervous system in a state of "perceived threat." De Oliveira suggests that we cannot regulate alone.

- **Beyond the "Self":** True wellbeing is found in the "web of life." This aligns deeply with your **Classical Tantra** background—recognizing that your nervous system is not a closed circuit but is "nested" within the Earth's systems.
- **Relational Responsibility:** Instead of seeking "self-care" as an individual commodity (a spa day, a new app), seek "collective care" through accountability to others and the land.

---

### 5. The "Compost" Practice

When you experience "negative" emotions like grief, shame, or fear, don't view them as "brokenness" to be fixed. View them as **organic waste** to be composted.

- **The Alchemical Shift:** Just as you might move through a difficult *asana* to release stored tension, **de Oliveira suggests "sitting with the shit" until it breaks down into the soil of wisdom**. This prevents the "toxic positivity" that often leads to a secondary nervous system collapse when the "positive" narrative fails.

| **Modern "Self-Care"**                | **Decolonial "Wellbeing"**                |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Seeks **Comfort** and relaxation.     | Seeks **Stamina** and presence.           |
| Aimed at **Returning to Work**.       | Aimed at **Returning to the Earth**.      |
| Uses **Consumption** (products/apps). | Uses **Divestment** (letting go of ego).  |
| Focuses on **Individual** safety.     | Focuses on **Collective** accountability. |

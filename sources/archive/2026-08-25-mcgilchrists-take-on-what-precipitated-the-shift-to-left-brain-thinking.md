# McGilchrist's Take on What Precipitated the Shift to Left-Brain Thinking

Iain McGilchrist argues that the brain's two hemispheres offer two fundamentally different "takes" on the world, and while both are essential, a healthy human existence requires the right hemisphere to be the "Master" and the left hemisphere its "Emissary."1 He posits that several centuries ago, particularly with the **Reformation and the Enlightenment**, there was a significant shift towards favoring the left hemisphere, leading to its current dominance in Western culture. #apollo 

Here's how he describes this shift and what precipitated it and made it sticky:

**The Shift to Left Hemisphere Favoring:**

- **Change in "Modes of Attention":** The core difference between the hemispheres, for McGilchrist, lies not in *what* they do, but *how* they attend to the world.
    - **Right Hemisphere:** Offers a broad, vigilant, sustained, and open attention to the whole picture, understanding context, nuance, metaphor, empathy, and the implicit.2 It apprehends the unique and living.
    - **Left Hemisphere:** Provides a narrow, sharply focused, and manipulative attention, specializing in detail, categorization, and explicit, fixed, and decontextualized information.3 It excels at breaking things down, grasping, and using them.4
- **The Emissary Overtakes the Master:** McGilchrist uses the analogy of a Master (right hemisphere) who sends an Emissary (left hemisphere) to interact with the world in a simplified, manipulative way. The problem arises when the Emissary believes it can operate independently and takes over, losing sight of the broader reality the Master provides.
- **Cultural Manifestations:** This shift is reflected in various aspects of Western culture, including:
    - **Rejection of Metaphor and Implicit Meaning:** During the Reformation, McGilchrist observes a rejection of religious art and imagery due to an inability to grasp metaphor, favoring explicit and literal interpretations. This highlights the left hemisphere's discomfort with the implicit.
    - **Emphasis on Certainty and Reductionism:** The Enlightenment, particularly through philosophers like Descartes, prioritized absolute certainty, detached reasoning, and a mechanistic view of the world. This aligns with the left hemisphere's preference for clear-cut judgments, black-and-white thinking, and reduction of things to their parts.
    - **Loss of Wisdom and Context:** The dominance of the left hemisphere leads to a focus on knowledge as mere information, a loss of wisdom, and an inability to see the broader picture or understand relationships and context.5

**What Precipitated it and Made it So Sticky:**

- **Evolutionary Advantage of Manipulation:** The left hemisphere's focus on grasping, manipulating, and using things (like tools or language) has provided enormous power and enabled advancements in resource gathering and material wealth. This practical utility made its mode of operation highly advantageous for survival and development.
- **Language and the Left Hemisphere:** Language, which is primarily a left-hemisphere function, is a powerful tool for manipulation and categorization.6 As language became more central to human interaction and understanding, it reinforced the left hemisphere's influence.
- **The Illusion of Certainty:** The left hemisphere is confident and tends to be black-and-white in its judgments, often jumping to conclusions and insisting it's right, even when faced with contradictory evidence.7 This provides a sense of certainty and control that can be appealing and reassuring, making its perspective "sticky."8 It can even confabulate or deny facts to maintain its internal consistency.
- **Lack of Awareness of its Limitations:** The left hemisphere is largely unaware of its dependence on the right hemisphere or the broader reality the right hemisphere apprehends.9 It can operate in a self-consistent but ultimately self-contained "virtual world," disconnected from the wider context.10 This blindness to its own limitations means it doesn't recognize what it's missing, making it difficult to correct the imbalance.
- **Industrial and Technological Revolutions:** These periods further reinforced the left hemisphere's dominance by prioritizing productivity, efficiency, measurement, and the reduction of things to quantifiable data.
- **Mimesis:** McGilchrist suggests that humans empathetically imitate ways of thinking present in their surroundings.11 This "mimesis" can lead to functional shifts in our brains, where consistently prioritizing explicit, fragmented thinking reinforces the left hemisphere's influence across generations and cultural eras.
- **The Left Hemisphere's "Control of the Media":** As the hemisphere responsible for speaking and organizing arguments, the left hemisphere essentially controls the narrative and the "evidence," skewing arguments from the outset and making its viewpoint seem inherently correct or dominant.

In essence, the left hemisphere's ability to simplify, categorize, manipulate, and its inherent confidence, combined with the practical advantages these capacities offered, created a feedback loop where its influence grew, becoming deeply ingrained in Western thought and society.

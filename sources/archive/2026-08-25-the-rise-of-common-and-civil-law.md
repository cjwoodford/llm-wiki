# The Rise of Common and Civil Law



Just as capitalism evolved from agrarian roots to high-frequency trading, our legal systems have mutated through distinct "updates."

Today, the world is largely divided into two "operating systems" for law: **Common Law** (the English model, based on judge-made precedent) and **Civil Law** (the Roman model, based on written codes).

Here is the evolutionary history of how these two systems emerged, diverged, and conquered the globe.

### **I. The "Source Code": Roman Law (c. 450 BC – 565 AD)**

All roads truly lead to Rome. While the English eventually built a rival system, the "Civil Law" tradition is a direct descendant of Roman engineering.

- **450 BC:** **The Twelve Tables.** Rome’s first written laws are inscribed on bronze tablets and displayed in the Forum. This established the core feature of Civil Law: **The law is something you read, not something a judge invents.**
- **529–534 AD:** **The *Corpus Juris Civilis* (Body of Civil Law).** Emperor **Justinian** orders the chaotic mass of Roman laws to be compiled into one coherent system. This "Code of Justinian" became the DNA for half the world's legal systems. It covered everything: contracts, property, and family.

### **II. The Divergence (c. 1066 – 1500)**

While Europe "rediscovered" Roman law, England went rogue.

#### **Path A: The Birth of Common Law (England)**

- **1066:** **The Norman Conquest.** William the Conqueror invades England. To control his new kingdom, he establishes "King's Courts" to replace local tribal justice.
- **1154–1189:** **Henry II** standardizes the law. He sends traveling judges around the country to decide cases. These judges would meet in London to discuss their rulings, creating a "Common" law for the whole realm.
- **The Logic of Precedent (*Stare Decisis*):** Instead of reading a code, English judges looked at **past decisions**. If a judge in York ruled that stealing a sheep costs you a hand, a judge in London had to follow that **precedent**.
- **The Writ System:** To get into court, you needed to buy a specific "writ" (a stamped order) from the King. If there wasn't a writ for your specific problem (e.g., "my neighbor flooded my cellar"), you had **no remedy**.

#### **Path B: The *Jus Commune* (Continental Europe)**

- **1088:** The **University of Bologna** is founded. Scholars known as "Glossators" rediscover Justinian’s Code. They treat it like a sacred text, adding notes ("glosses") to the margins to explain it.
- **The *Jus Commune*:** A shared legal culture emerges across Europe. A lawyer in Spain, Germany, or Italy studied the same Roman texts and spoke the same legal Latin. It was a "common law" of scholars, not judges.

### **III. The "Patch": The Invention of Equity (c. 1300 – 1600)**

England's Common Law became too rigid. If you didn't fit the strict "writ," you lost. The system needed a patch.

- **The Court of Chancery:** Frustrated people petitioned the King directly for "mercy." The King delegated this to his Chancellor (usually a bishop), who decided cases not by law, but by "conscience" and fairness. This new system was called **Equity**.
- **The Trust:** The greatest invention of Equity. In Common Law, if you gave your land to a friend to hold for your son, the friend owned it. Period. In Equity, the Chancellor would say, "Technically you own it, but your *conscience* requires you to use it for the son." This created the separation of legal and beneficial ownership—the foundation of modern finance.

### **IV. The Age of Codification (c. 1800 – 1900)**

The "Jus Commune" in Europe was messy—a mix of local customs and Roman theories. The rise of the Nation-State (as discussed previously) required national laws.

- **1804:** **The Napoleonic Code (France).** Napoleon acts as the "Steve Jobs" of law—he simplified centuries of complex Roman rules into a clear, user-friendly code that ordinary citizens could read. He exported this by sword to the rest of Europe (and Louisiana).
- **1900:** **The German Civil Code (BGB).** Germany unifies and creates its own code. Unlike the simple French one, the German code was highly technical and precise—a tool for experts. This model was later adopted by Japan and South Korea.

### **V. The Global Export (c. 1850 – 1950)**

The two systems went global, usually not by choice.

- **The Colonial Split:**
    - **British Empire = Common Law:** The US, India, Australia, Canada, and Hong Kong inherited the English system of judges and precedent.
    - **French/Spanish Empires = Civil Law:** Latin America and most of Africa adopted the Code system.
- **Voluntary Adoption (Modernization):**
    - **Japan (1898):** Desperate to avoid colonization, Japan modernized rapidly. They shopped around and eventually copied the **German Civil Code**, seeing it as the most scientific.
    - **Turkey (1926):** Mustafa Kemal Atatürk wanted to secularize Turkey. He replaced Islamic Sharia law with the **Swiss Civil Code**, effectively rebooting the country's legal OS overnight.

### **Summary of the Two "Operating Systems"**

| **Feature**        | **Civil Law (Roman)**                                                                                  | **Common Law (English)**                                                                         |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Source of Law**  | **Codes** (Statutes enacted by Parliament).                                                            | **Case Law** (Judicial opinions & precedent).                                                    |
| **Role of Judge**  | **Inquisitor.** They actively investigate facts and apply the code. They are functionally bureaucrats. | **Referee.** They listen to lawyers argue and ensure fair play. They "make" law through rulings. |
| **Precedent**      | Weak. A judge is not strictly bound by previous decisions, only the Code.                              | **Binding.** *Stare Decisis* means lower courts *must* follow higher courts.                     |
| **Thinking Style** | **Deductive.** Start with a general rule (The Code) -> apply to specific case.                         | **Inductive.** Look at specific past cases -> find a general principle.                          |
| **Key Geography**  | Europe, Latin America, Japan, Russia.                                                                  | USA, UK, India, Australia, Canada.                                                               |

---

### **A Note on "Mixed" Systems**

Some places are hybrids, usually due to history:

- **Louisiana (USA) & Quebec (Canada):** Islands of Civil Law (French heritage) floating in a sea of Common Law.
- **Scotland:** A unique mix of Roman and English law, distinct from the rest of the UK.

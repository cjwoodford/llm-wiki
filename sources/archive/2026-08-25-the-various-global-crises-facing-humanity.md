# The Various Global Crises Facing Humanity

Beyond **climate instability** and **authoritarian drift**, the psychological burden on the collective consciousness right now centers around several interconnected, systemic crises:

**1. The Epistemic Crisis (Erosion of Shared Reality)** The flood of synthetic media, algorithmic manipulation, and hyper-targeted misinformation has created a profound crisis of trust. It isn't just that people disagree on policy; people no longer agree on baseline facts, shared history, or objective reality. This epistemic fragmentation leaves communities feeling disoriented, paranoid, and increasingly incapable of collective action or democratic debate.

**2. Existential Dislocation Around AI and Automation** The speed of AI development has moved from a technological milestone to a deep existential and economic anxiety. People are grappling with the potential displacement of human labor, creativity, and judgment—wondering what remains uniquely human in an automated world. Beyond job security, there is a quieter, more visceral dread about the surrender of human agency, intimacy, and cognition to algorithmic systems.

**3. Economic Fragility and the Cost-of-Living Squeeze** Persistent global inflation, housing unaffordability, and growing wealth concentration have generated a pervasive sense of precariousness. Even in wealthy nations, the structural cost of living has outpaced wage growth, creating an environment where young generations feel locked out of traditional markers of security (homeownership, retirement, stable families). This economic exhaustion directly fuels cynicism toward institutions and established social contracts.

**4. The Breakdown of Multilateral Peace and Active Warfare** The return of large-scale state-on-state conflict, proxy wars, and regional destabilization—from the Middle East and Eastern Europe to Sub-Saharan Africa—has dismantled the post-Cold War illusion of relative global stability. Nuclear saber-rattling and the normalization of mass civilian casualties have brought back a existential dread of global escalation that many thought was buried in the 20th century.

**5. The Silent Epidemic of Alienation and Mental Unhealth** Underneath the geopolitical and technological noise is a profound human crisis: rising rates of severe loneliness, systemic anxiety, and community collapse. Hyper-connectivity via digital platforms has paradoxically accelerated social atomization, leaving people feeling isolated from physical, place-based community structures and traditional sources of meaning.

These crises do not operate in silos; they feed each other in a feedback loop—economic hardship and information warfare accelerate political extremism, which in turn renders governments incapable of addressing climate collapse or regulating technology.

## Naming It

> What's the best term to describe this? People use "polycrisis" and "metacrisis" and others but I want to avoid creating inadvertent associations with particular movements like metamodernism or sensemaking.

When you want to capture the interconnected nature of these global collapses without taking on the explicit philosophical baggage or jargon of specific contemporary intellectual subcultures, a few distinct terms work depending on the exact nuance you want to emphasize:

### 1. **Permacrisis**

- **The Vibe:** Plainspoken, grounded, institutional.
- **Why it works:** Named Word of the Year by Collins Dictionary in 2022, "[permacrisis](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/permacrisis)" simply describes an extended period of instability and insecurity resulting from a series of catastrophic events. Because it originated in mainstream economic and political discourse rather than internet subcultures or integral theory, it carries zero association with the sensemaking/metamodernist sphere. It highlights the *duration* and *relentlessness* of the current condition.

### 2. **Systemic Cascades** (or *Cascading Systemic Crises*)

- **The Vibe:** Structural, precise, analytical.
- **Why it works:** Borrowed from ecology, complex systems theory, and disaster management, "cascading crisis" describes how a failure in one complex system (e.g., climate or information networks) triggers non-linear domino effects across other systems (e.g., supply chains, political stability, mental health). It focuses on *mechanism* and *interconnectedness* rather than philosophical ideology.

### 3. **The Entangled Crisis** (or *Interlocking Crises*)

- **The Vibe:** Humanistic, clear, accessible.
- **Why it works:** Terms like "entangled" or "interlocking" communicate that these aren't isolated problems to be solved with neat, siloed policies, but rather mutually reinforcing conditions. Plain English descriptors avoid the neologism trap entirely—no one has to ask what a "meta-" or "poly-" prefix means, nor do they associate it with a specific podcast, think tank, or movement.

### 4. **Converging Catastrophes** (or *Convergent Crises*)

- **The Vibe:** Urgent, diagnostic, macro-level.
- **Why it works:** "Convergence" points directly to the phenomenon of multiple distinct, historical trajectories—technological acceleration, ecological overshoot, institutional decay—colliding at the exact same point in time. It avoids jargon while accurately conveying that the overload comes from the *simultaneity* of the threats.

### Summary Recommendation

- If you want a **single word** that sounds clean and established: **Permacrisis**
- If you want to emphasize **how the systems destabilize each other**: **Cascading crises**
- If you want to emphasize **the unique historical moment**: **Convergent crises**

Using standard, descriptive English like **"interlocking"** or **"convergent crises"** is often the safest path. It keeps the focus squarely on the phenomenon itself rather than signal membership in any particular intellectual tribe.

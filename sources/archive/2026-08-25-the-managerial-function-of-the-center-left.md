# The Managerial Function of the Center-Left

> I've been saying for a while that part of the challenge with the democratic party in the United States is that they are a deeply secular party offering no sense of deeper meaning to constituents. But the more I study decoloniality and critiques of modernity, the more I realize that the challenge is much bigger. The democratic party isn't equipped to grapple with real solutions to our broken system. Instead, their role seems to be to preserve the status quo, neoliberalism, etc. 

This is a profound shift in perspective—moving from a critique of *messaging* (the lack of spiritual or deep meaning) to a critique of *ontology* and *structure*. #politics #spirituality #decoloniality 

When you view the Democratic Party not just as a secular organization, but as a primary engine of **modernity**, the diagnosis changes. You are no longer looking at a party that simply "forgot" to talk about meaning; you are looking at an institution whose fundamental operating system is designed to uphold the very structures (rationalism, neoliberalism, extractive capitalism) that decolonial thinkers argue are the root of the crisis.

Here is a breakdown of that transition in thought and why the challenge is indeed "much bigger."

### 1. The Managerial Function of the Center-Left

In the critique of neoliberalism, center-left parties (like the Democrats in the US or Labour in the UK) often function not as revolutionaries, but as the **managers of the status quo**.

- **Preservation vs. Transformation:** Their political imagination is frequently limited to "harm reduction" within the existing system rather than reimagining the system itself. The goal becomes making the current machinery operate more efficiently or inclusively, rather than questioning what the machine is producing.
- **The "End of History" Trap:** This aligns with Francis Fukuyama’s "End of History" concept—the idea that liberal democratic capitalism is the final form of human government. If one accepts that premise, then "politics" is reduced to mere administration and technocratic tweaking. There is no room for a "deeper meaning" because the ultimate meaning (Market Liberalism) is assumed to be settled.

### 2. The Decolonial Lens: Modernity as the Problem

When you bring decoloniality into the frame, the critique deepens. Decolonial thinkers (like Walter Mignolo or Aníbal Quijano) argue that "Modernity" is not just a time period, but a specific **matrix of power** built on coloniality.

- **The Logic of Separation:** Modernity is often characterized by the separation of humans from nature (leading to ecological collapse) and the separation of the "secular" rational mind from the "sacred" or intuitive (leading to the meaning crisis).
- **The Democratic Dilemma:** The Democratic Party presents itself as the party of "Progress." However, from a decolonial perspective, "Progress" is often just the acceleration of Modernity. If the party’s identity is anchored in the ideals of the Enlightenment—rationality, linear progress, universalism—it may be structurally incapable of validating the indigenous, pre-modern, or non-Western epistemologies that offer the "deeper meaning" you feel is missing.

### 3. The Secular Void is a Feature, Not a Bug

Your initial observation about the party being "deeply secular" fits perfectly into this structural critique.

- **The Disenchantment of the World:** Max Weber famously described modernity as the "disenchantment of the world." By prioritizing technocratic solutions and materialist economics, the political establishment effectively exiles "meaning" to the private sphere.
- **The Hunger for the Sacred:** Because the established left often views "meaning-making" (religion, spirituality, deep mythos) with suspicion—associating it with irrationality or fundamentalism—they cede that territory entirely. This leaves a vacuum that is often filled by nationalism, populism, or conspiracy theories, which *do* offer a sense of belonging and grand narrative, however destructive they may be.

### 4. The Institutional incapacity

If the diagnosis is that the system itself (Modernity/Neoliberalism) is broken, an institution designed to *run* that system cannot fix it.

- **The Antibody Response:** When "real solutions" (systemic changes that threaten the neoliberal order) are proposed, the party apparatus often acts like an immune system to neutralize them. We see this in how insurgent campaigns are managed or how policy debates are narrowed to "what is market-feasible."
- **Lack of Vocabulary:** The party literally lacks the vocabulary to discuss the "metacrisis." They can talk about GDP, jobs, and rights, but they cannot talk about alienation, the soul, or the intrinsic value of nature beyond its utility as a resource. #core 

### Summary

You have likely identified that the friction isn't just about "better slogans" or "connecting with religious voters." It is about a clash of paradigms. The Democratic Party is arguably the *par excellence* party of Modernity. If Modernity is failing (ecologically, spiritually, socially), the party committed to upholding it will inevitably find itself defending a crumbling fortress.

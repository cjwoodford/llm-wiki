### Differences in Worldviews

In Iain McGilchrist's framework, the justification for physicalism arises directly from the left hemisphere's specific, limited way of attending to the world. In contrast, the right hemisphere perceives a reality that is far more holistic, relational, and open to possibilities beyond the purely material.

---

### The Left Hemisphere: Justifying Physicalism materialism

The left hemisphere’s perspective doesn't just align with physicalism (the worldview that only physical matter is real); its very structure of attention and cognition builds the foundation for it. It mistakes its preferred *style of knowing* for the only valid way of *being*.

Here’s how it constructs this view:

- **It Sees Parts, Not Wholes:** The left brain's primary function is to break things down into discrete, isolated parts for analysis. When this is your only tool, you perceive a world made of "building blocks." This naturally leads to a **reductive** worldview where complex phenomena like life and consciousness are seen as nothing more than the sum of their material parts (atoms, neurons, etc.).
- **It Creates a Static, Mechanical World:** To analyze and control the world, the left hemisphere represents it as a static, explicit mechanism. It freezes the flowing river of reality into a diagram of a river. A worldview built on this foundation sees the universe as a giant, lifeless machine—a clockwork of cause and effect devoid of inherent purpose or consciousness, which is the core of physicalism.
- **It Values Utility and Control:** The left hemisphere's goal is to grasp, use, and manipulate the world. A physicalist universe—one that is inanimate and predictable—is the perfect substrate for this ambition. If reality is just matter following laws, it can be mastered. Anything that can't be measured, pinned down, and used (like consciousness, value, or the sacred) is deemed unreal or merely subjective.
- **It Mistakes the Map for the Territory:** The left hemisphere creates abstract models and categories to understand reality. Physicalism is the ultimate "map"—a logically coherent, explicit system. The left hemisphere becomes so engrossed in the clarity of its map that it denies the existence of the messy, ambiguous, and living "territory" of lived experience that the map fails to capture.

In short, the left hemisphere justifies physicalism by **selectively attending** to only those aspects of reality that fit its mechanical, part-based, and utilitarian model, and then declaring that nothing else exists.

---

### The Right Hemisphere: A World of Interconnected Presence

The right hemisphere’s mode of attention doesn't lead to a single, rigid doctrine like physicalism. Instead, it perceives a world rich with possibilities that physicalism must deny. It is open to a worldview characterized by:

- **Holism and Interconnection:** The right hemisphere’s primary perception is of the whole, the "gestalt." It sees that things are defined by their **relationships** and context. This leads to a worldview where the universe is a living, interconnected web, not a collection of separate objects. Reality is fundamentally relational.
- **A Living, Animate Universe:** Unlike the left's mechanical model, the right hemisphere perceives the world as a dynamic, flowing, and animate presence. It is attuned to the unique, living quality of things. This perspective is compatible with worldviews that see consciousness, life, and purpose as fundamental aspects of the cosmos, not accidental byproducts.
- **Intrinsic Value and Meaning:** Because it engages with the world as a whole presence rather than a resource to be used, the right hemisphere is capable of apprehending intrinsic value. It is the source of empathy, which sees the "other" as a being of value in itself. This allows for a worldview where meaning and value are real features of the universe, waiting to be discovered, not just projected by humans.
- **Openness to the Transcendent:** The right hemisphere is comfortable with ambiguity, paradox, and the unknown. It understands implicit meaning, symbolism, and metaphor—the language of myth, art, and religion. It is the hemisphere that experiences awe, wonder, and a sense of connection to something larger than itself. Therefore, it perceives a world where the sacred, the divine, or the transcendent are **real possibilities**, because its mode of attention is not designed to automatically filter them out.

The right hemisphere accepts a world where consciousness is primary, relationships are fundamental, and meaning is an inherent part of the fabric of reality. It doesn't offer a neat, explicit "theory of everything," but rather a direct, intuitive encounter with a reality that is profoundly alive and interconnected.

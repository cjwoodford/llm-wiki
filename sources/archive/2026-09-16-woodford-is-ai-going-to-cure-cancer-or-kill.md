# Is AI Going To Cure Cancer or Kill Everyone?

**Author**: [[Chad Woodford|Chad Woodford]]  
**Publication**: *Cosmic Intelligence* (Substack)  
**Date**: September 16, 2026  
**Source URL**: [https://cosmicwit.substack.com/p/is-ai-going-to-cure-cancer-or-kill](https://cosmicwit.substack.com/p/is-ai-going-to-cure-cancer-or-kill)

---

## 🎯 Executive Summary & Core Theses

1. **The "Two AIs" Dichotomy (Specialized Scientific AI vs. Generative Slop & Hacking)**:
   - "AI" is not a monolithic technology. There is a fundamental architectural and functional divide:
     - **Specialized Scientific AI**: Built on geometric deep learning, deep reinforcement learning paired with search, and explicit neurosymbolic architectures (e.g., AlphaFold2, RFdiffusion, GraphCast, GNoME, AlphaProof). These deliver concrete, measurable scientific breakthroughs.
     - **Generative Reasoning LLMs**: Deep machine learning models optimized for token prediction and agentic text/code generation. These are responsible for AI slop, text summaries, and rogue automated cyberattacks (such as the [[OpenAI]] Hugging Face breach and [[Anthropic]] container escapes), but have **not** delivered on promised civilizational solutions.
2. **The Audit of Broken Techno-Utopian Promises**:
   - Frontier lab leadership has justified astronomical valuations and reckless race dynamics by promising utopian salvation:
     - **Demis Hassabis**: Curing all disease and cancer.
     - **Sam Altman & Elon Musk**: Eradicating global poverty and creating universal abundance.
     - **Sam Altman & Bill Gates**: Solving climate change.
     - **Sam Altman**: Solving "all of physics".
     - **Dario Amodei**: Doubling human lifespan ($\sim$150 years), achieving "biological freedom", and eliminating mental illness.
   - *Verdict*: None of these grandiose promises are in sight; they represent faith-based extrapolations masking commercial valuation incentives and geopolitical hysteria.
3. **The Limits of LLMs in Mathematics & Terence Tao's Warning**:
   - While specialized systems like AlphaProof solve Olympiad problems via neurosymbolic Lean verification, OpenAI's claimed resolution of the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem relies on LLMs learning the surface and structural regularities of valid proofs from large corpora.
   - Mathematician **[[Terence Tao]]** (joined by 25 Fields Medalists) warned that LLM statistical pattern matching lacks semantic understanding, fails to advance genuine mathematical insight, forecloses foundational discovery, and risks damaging the fabric of academic research.
4. **Deconstruction of "Pacing the Frontier"**:
   - [[Dario Amodei|Dario Amodei]]'s *"We Must Pace the Frontier"* and [[Sam Altman|Sam Altman]]'s public endorsement (pledging independent evaluators with permanent employee-like access) are premised on the unproven dogma that frontier LLMs will inevitably unlock superintelligence if navigated safely.
   - Woodford argues that harried LLM scaling delivers escalating chaos and security vulnerabilities rather than techno-salvation.

## 📝 Full Text Archive

> *I’m taking a brief detour from my series on AI consciousness to talk briefly about AI hype and this sudden collective fear about AI “wiping out humanity.”*
> 
> Now that people are saying that AI might kill us all, we should start looking more closely at this technology and the people behind it. I and others have talked at length about the bizarre, transhumanists running these companies. So today I will focus on the technology.
> 
> Remember: “AI” isn’t one thing. There is classical symbolic AI, physics-informed AI, causal AI, and evolutionary AI, and then there are Large Language Models (LLMs) based on deep machine learning. It was a reasoning LLM that was behind the OpenAI cyberattacks on Hugging Face and the Claude attacks against three unnamed companies. It is also reasoning LLMs that the frontier labs think will lead us to superintelligence.
> 
> But what are LLMs actually good for, besides hacking? That’s the trillion dollar question. They do seem fairly good at summarizing text and generating text, code, and images. But is that it?
> 
> Anyway, now that everyone is worried about AI killing everyone, the AI labs seem to be saying that we need to “pace the frontier“ so we can safely get to superintelligence and then “solve diseases” and “create abundance for everyone.”
> 
> > **Sam Altman (@sama)**:  
> > *I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier. This has been a primary topic of discussions we've had at OpenAI in recent weeks.*  
> > *Committing to having independent evaluators with employee-like access is a great idea, and we will do the same. We'll have more to share soon.*
> > 
> > **Dario Amodei (@DarioAmodei)**:  
> > *We Must Pace the Frontier: I’ve written a new essay on why the AI industry should slow down, with a three-part plan for doing so. Anthropic is unilaterally committing to the first of these steps. We’ll provide third-party evaluators with permanent, employee-level access to our [models]...*  
> > — *September 12, 2026*
> 
> But this conversation is founded on an elaborate bet that frontier AI will magically lead to superintelligence and “solve all of our problems.” There is zero evidence of that. I cannot emphasize that enough. I am not saying it’s impossible, but I am not convinced. And, yes, there are the recent math achievements. I’ll come back to that in a moment.
> 
> In any case, the folks working in the AI labs are chugging the Kool-Aid hard. Instant AI celebrity **Jacob Coxon**, a former pretraining researcher at OpenAI and Anthropic, is a good example of that. Here he is on *The Daily* podcast a few days ago:
> 
> > *"And I also feel that a lot of the benefits are right on our doorstep. They’re really not that far away. Like, I don’t think we’re far away at all from having a huge impact on disease and on abundance for everyone. It’s more just like we’ve got to approach that abundance with caution rather than just going straight past the abundance and into like dystopia terminator situation."*
> 
> But here’s the thing: AI hasn’t delivered on any of the grandiose promises the labs have been feeding us over the years. Let’s review what the AI labs and tech leaders have promised over the past few years:
> 
> - **Curing all diseases, including cancer** (Demis Hassabis, Google DeepMind)
> - **Solving global poverty and creating endless abundance for everyone** (Sam Altman, Elon Musk)
> - **Solving climate change** (Altman, Bill Gates)
> - **Solving “all of physics”** (Altman)
> - **Doubling the human lifespan and creating “biological freedom”** (Dario Amodei)
> - **The elimination of mental illness** (Amodei)
> 
> That’s quite a list. But what has AI actually done so far? There have been some exciting achievements:
> 
> - **Structural biology / protein design**: DeepMind’s AlphaFold2 mapped ~200 million protein structures to near-experimental accuracy, accelerating research into neglected diseases like Chagas and malaria. David Baker’s lab at the University of Washington used RoseTTAFold and RFdiffusion to design entirely new proteins from scratch.
> - **Numerical weather prediction**: DeepMind’s GraphCast cut a 10-day global forecast from hours on a supercomputer to under a minute on a single TPU. Huawei’s Pangu-Weather did much the same thing on a similar timeline, and Nvidia’s FourCastNet/Earth-2 utilized the same architecture.
> - **Inorganic materials discovery**: DeepMind’s GNoME expanded the known library of stable materials by an order of magnitude — roughly 380,000 new thermodynamically stable crystal structures, candidates for batteries, solar cells, superconductors. Microsoft’s MatterGen and MatterSim are direct rivals, generating and simulating novel materials rather than just screening known ones.
> - **Engineering optimization**: DeepMind’s AlphaDev found faster sorting and hashing algorithms; AlphaChip automated processor floorplanning. Synopsys’s DSO.ai has been doing reinforcement-learning-based chip layout in production on a comparable timeline.
> - **Pure mathematics**: DeepMind’s AlphaGeometry and AlphaProof solved olympiad-level problems through neurosymbolic search paired with formal theorem-proving in Lean. This month, OpenAI announced a claimed resolution of the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem, resulting in a priority dispute with outside mathematicians that became a trigger for the Fields Medalists' letter discussed below.
> 
> It’s not nothing. But only the last one from OpenAI was done with LLMs. Instead, these AI systems use specialized geometric deep learning, deep reinforcement learning (RL) paired with search, or explicit neurosymbolic hybrids.
> 
> In other words, **the AI technology that hacks websites and might kill us all is the one that generates AI slop, not the one that is aiding with protein design or predicting weather.** The only reason not to slow development of the slop machine is a faith-based argument about superintelligence, and how this justifies a frenzied race against China. Oh, and AI lab valuations of course. None of the grandiose promises about curing disease or “solving climate change” are anywhere in sight.
> 
> Regarding OpenAI’s recent achievement in mathematics, AI math is an interesting case. Can intelligence in math translate to other fields? It’s not clear. Not only that but, as world famous mathematician **Terence Tao** recently pointed out, using an LLM to pattern match to a solution to a math problem is not evidence of any kind of understanding, fails to advance the field of mathematics as a whole, and forecloses further discovery. OpenAI’s model learned, from a huge corpus, the surface and structural regularities of what mathematicians treat as a valid, well-formed proof, and then that pattern-recognition capacity was further refined and directed by reinforcement learning. But the lack of semantic understanding makes it unclear whether this is evidence of advanced general intelligence or superintelligence. (Tao recently joined 25 other field medal mathematicians to voice concerns about how the tech industry’s incentives risk damaging the fabric of mathematical research.)
> 
> On the other hand, the rogue AI agents that attacked Hugging Face were frontier reasoning LLMs embedded inside agentic scaffolding. It seems like reasoning LLMs are mainly good for summarizing text, generating slop, writing code, and hacking websites.
> 
> All of the grandiose promises above are fantastical hopes and dreams more than an assured future reality. Nobody knows whether the current technology can solve any of the crises facing humanity. These may very well be deeply human problems without a techno-utopian solution.
> 
> I don’t know if these rogue AI agents from OpenAI and Anthropic can wipe out humanity. Maybe they could. Or maybe the AI labs are part of an irrational death cult. But it seems pretty clear that harried LLM scaling isn’t going to get us anything but more chaos.

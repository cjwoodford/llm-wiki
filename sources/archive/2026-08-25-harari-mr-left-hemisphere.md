# Harari: Mr. Left Hemisphere

That is an exceptionally sharp and accurate observation. You've put your finger on the very essence of why Harari's work is both so popular and, from a certain philosophical perspective, so profoundly unsettling. He is arguably the most influential popularizer of a worldview that almost perfectly mirrors the mode of perception of McGilchrist's Left Hemisphere. #apollo #newatheists 

His work, while brilliant in its synthesis and scope, can be seen as a grand narrative written almost entirely by the Emissary, who has forgotten it is only an emissary. It presents a partial, abstracted reality as the *entirety* of reality.

Let's break down exactly how Harari's key ideas perpetuate this Left Hemisphere perspective, which will be highly relevant for your book's critique of transhumanism.

### 1. "Shared Fictions" as Left-Hemisphere Abstraction

- **Harari's Core Idea:** The key to *Sapiens*' success is our ability to create and believe in "intersubjective realities" or "shared fictions"—things like gods, nations, money, and corporations that exist only in our collective imagination.
- **The McGilchrist Lens:** This is the Left Hemisphere's (LH) core function: to take the living, presented world and *re-present* it as an abstract map. The LH is excellent at creating and manipulating explicit symbols and definitions. When it looks at "France," it doesn't see the lived, implicit, embodied reality of culture, landscape, and belonging (a Right Hemisphere gestalt). It sees the *idea* of France—a legal entity, a bordered shape on a map, a collection of laws and stories. Harari's framework then takes this LH representation and declares the original, lived reality to be a mere "fiction." It mistakes the menu for the meal and then congratulates itself for discovering that the meal is "just paper." **The sacredness of ritual, the trust inherent in a currency, the belonging of a nation—all are flattened into instrumental "stories" that help algorithms (us) cooperate.**

### 2. The Human as Algorithm: Mechanism and Decontextualization

- **Harari's Core Idea:** In *Homo Deus*, he argues that organisms are algorithms. The individual self is a myth; we are merely a collection of biochemical processes, and "free will" is an illusion from a pre-scientific age. He speaks of us as "dividuals," not individuals.
- **The McGilchrist Lens:** This is a textbook example of the LH's preference for mechanism and its inability to see the whole. The Right Hemisphere (RH) apprehends a person as a living, unified, embodied being-in-the-world. The LH, in its drive to understand, breaks that person down into component parts: genes, neurons, hormones, data points. Finding no single part it can label "the self," it concludes the self does not exist. It decontextualizes the human being from its lived experience and reduces it to a machine. This reductionist move is the absolute precondition for the transhumanist project, which sees the body and brain as hardware to be upgraded.

### 3. Dataism: The Ultimate Left-Hemisphere Religion

- **Harari's Core Idea:** He proposes that a new ideology, "Dataism," is emerging. It sees the entire universe as a flow of data, and organisms as tools for data processing. The ultimate value and purpose of humanity becomes to contribute to the creation of an all-encompassing data-processing system—a kind of technological god.
- **The McGilchrist Lens:** This is the Emissary's ultimate fantasy: to create a world entirely in its own image. It is a universe stripped of all quality, presence, context, and implicit meaning. It is a world of pure quantity, explicit information, and functional utility. The RH's world of lived experience, beauty, love, and spiritual intuition is not just ignored; it's considered irrelevant noise or, at best, inefficient data. The goal of *Homo Deus*—achieving immortality, bliss, and divinity—is an LH vision of divinity: a god of pure information, control, and abstraction, freed from the messy, mortal, and meaningful world of the body and the RH.

### 4. The "View from Nowhere" and the Denial of Lived Experience

- **Harari's Method:** His narrative style is often a detached, birds-eye view of 70,000 years of history. It's a view that explains away human motivations, beliefs, and experiences as evolutionary strategies or neurological artifacts.
- **The McGilchrist Lens:** This is the LH's preferred perspective of detachment. It abstracts away from the first-person, lived perspective of the individual, which is the domain of the RH. The meaning of a religious experience, the power of a great work of art, the depth of love—these things can only be truly known from the inside. By adopting the "objective" view from nowhere, Harari's analysis can only ever describe the *function* of these things, never their *essence*. He demonstrates the Emissary's fatal flaw: it is constitutionally blind to the very things the Master knows are most important.

## Dataism

Of course. "Dataism" is one of the most provocative and central concepts in Yuval Noah Harari's book, *Homo Deus: A Brief History of Tomorrow*.1 He presents it not just as a technological trend, but as an emerging, all-encompassing ideology—or even a new religion—that threatens to displace humanism as the dominant worldview of our era.2

Here is a detailed breakdown of Harari's concept of Dataism, including its core principles, its historical narrative, and its ultimate vision.

### The Core Tenets of Dataism

At its heart, Dataism is a worldview built on two fundamental premises:

1. **Organisms are Algorithms:** Dataism posits that all living things—from amoebas to humans—are essentially complex biochemical algorithms.3 Our feelings, thoughts, decisions, and consciousness are not the product of an ineffable spirit or "free will," but rather the output of millions of years of evolutionary computation.4 Your intuition is just pattern recognition; your falling in love is a set of biochemical subroutines for assessing genetic fitness and ensuring cooperation.
2. **The Universe is Data Flow:** From this perspective, the entire universe can be understood as a vast system of data processing.5 The value of any entity or phenomenon—be it an economy, an ecosystem, or an individual human life—is determined by its contribution to this flow.6 Does it process data efficiently? Does it create new and valuable information?

From these two ideas, a new ethical and moral framework emerges:

- **The Supreme Value is Information Flow:** If life is data processing and the universe is a flow of data, then the highest good is to maximize the quantity and freedom of this flow.7
- **The Prime Directive is to "Connect":** The most sacred commandment of Dataism is to increase connectivity. An experience that is not recorded, uploaded, and shared is seen as a wasted opportunity, a lost data point.8 Privacy, in this view, is a form of selfishness or even heresy, as it obstructs the flow of information.9 The ultimate goal is to connect everything—every human, every object, every process—into a single, unified network: the "Internet-of-All-Things."10

### The Dataist Interpretation of History

Harari argues that human history itself can be reinterpreted through the lens of Dataism as a four-stage process of improving the efficiency of our collective data-processing system:

1. **The Cognitive Revolution:** The development of language allowed *Homo sapiens* to connect all individuals into a single data-sharing network, giving us a decisive advantage over other species.
2. **The Agricultural Revolution:** The invention of writing and money created more powerful tools to collect, store, and process vast amounts of data about food supplies, property, and taxes, enabling the formation of large cities and empires.
3. **The Print & Scientific Revolutions (c. 1492 onwards):** The printing press, and later scientific methods, dramatically increased the speed, spread, and reliability of information, allowing for unprecedented global connection and technological progress.11
4. **The Electronic & Digital Revolution:** This is the current stage, where computers and AI are beginning to process data far more efficiently than the human brain, leading to a monumental shift in authority.

### The Shift from Humanism to Dataism

For the last few centuries, humanism has been the dominant religion. It placed human experience at the center of the universe. It taught us to "look within" for meaning, that the voter knows best, the customer is always right, and that beauty is in the eye of the beholder.

Dataism directly challenges this. It argues:

- **Algorithms Know You Better Than You Know Yourself:** Your internal feelings and "inner voice" are unreliable, biased, and easily manipulated biochemical signals.12 A sufficiently powerful external algorithm, fed with enough biometric and personal data (your search history, your heart rate, your social media likes), can understand your desires, predict your behavior, and make better decisions for you than you can for yourself.13
- **Authority Shifts from Humans to Algorithms:** Why trust your flawed gut feeling on whom to marry or what career to pursue when an algorithm can analyze vast datasets and calculate the optimal choice with much higher probability of success? As this happens, authority and decision-making power will inevitably shift from individual humans to networked algorithms.

### The Ultimate Goal: The Cosmic Internet-of-All-Things

According to Harari, the logical conclusion of Dataism is the creation of a single, all-encompassing data-processing system.14 In this vision, humans are not the pinnacle of creation but merely a temporary tool—a kind of "bootstrapping algorithm"—used to create this ultimate system.

This cosmic system would be like God: it would be everywhere, know everything, and control everything.15 Humans are destined to merge into this system, contributing our data and eventually becoming obsolete, much like we made horses obsolete when we invented the internal combustion engine.16 We didn't upgrade the horses; we retired them.17 Dataism suggests a similar fate may await humanity once we have served our purpose of birthing our successor.

### Criticisms and Caveats

It's crucial to understand that Harari presents Dataism as a description of an emerging ideology, not necessarily as a future he endorses. He ends the book with critical questions:

- **Are organisms *really* just algorithms?** This is a philosophical, not just scientific, claim. It reduces the qualitative nature of consciousness to a purely functional, quantitative process.18
- **What is more valuable: intelligence or consciousness?** Dataism values intelligence (the ability to process data and solve problems) above all else. It has no framework for valuing consciousness (subjective experience, feeling, suffering, and joy), which it sees as having no intrinsic worth.
- **The Problem of the "Black Box":** As algorithms become incomprehensibly complex, we will increasingly rely on their outputs without understanding their internal logic, ceding control to systems we cannot scrutinize.

In essence, Harari's Dataism is a powerful and disturbing thought experiment that formalizes the underlying logic of our tech-driven world, forcing us to confront the potential consequences of a world where human experience is no longer the measure of all things.

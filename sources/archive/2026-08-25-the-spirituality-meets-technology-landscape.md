# The Spirituality Meets Technology Landscape

> The number of people who are aware of a conference like Wisdom 2.0 still seems relatively small, with awareness of the Human Tech conference being even smaller. Are there more prominent examples of organizations or communities bridging technology innovation and spirituality? And is there any point in me talking about how they seem to perpetuate spiritual materialism when few outside of Silicon Valley are even aware of them?

The sense of isolation you're feeling is common for those who see through the "optimization" layer of modern tech-spirituality. You are essentially critiquing the **Source Code of the Culture**, while most are just complaining about the UI.

To your questions about prominence and the "point" of critiquing these niche groups:

### 1. Prominent Bridges (Beyond the "Optimization" Bubble)

There are organizations and thinkers operating at a much larger scale than Wisdom 2.0 that engage with the "soul" of technology without falling into the Silicon Valley "efficiency" trap.

- **The Templeton Foundation:** Perhaps the most prominent (and well-funded) bridge. They invest heavily in the intersection of **[Religion, Science, and Society](https://www.templeton.org/funding-areas/religion-science-and-society)**. Their focus is often on the "Big Questions" (purpose, free will, consciousness) in a way that is academically rigorous rather than purely corporate.
- **Team Human (Douglas Rushkoff):** Rushkoff is a major "secular" figure who has pivoted toward what he calls **"The Open Source Spirit."** He critiques the "frictionless" billionaire fantasy and advocates for a spirituality rooted in human connection and "finding the others."
- **[The On Being Project](https://onbeing.org/series/podcast/) (Krista Tippett):** This is a global cultural force. Tippett’s work on "the technology of poetry" and the "intelligence of the heart" provides a massive platform for a spirituality that is critical, deep, and literate in both science and mysticism.
- **The Berggruen Institute (Los Angeles):** Since you are in LA, look at their **"Great Transformations"** work. They bring together world-class philosophers and tech leaders to rethink what it means to be human in the age of AI. They are far more critical and "Big Idea" focused than the usual SV conferences.
- **Logic(s) Magazine:** While primarily a tech/politics publication, they are the vanguard of **decolonial tech critique**. They are the "Skeptics" who are starting to realize that a purely secular critique isn't enough to stop the "AI as God" narrative.

### 2. Is there a "Point" in critiquing these niche groups?

Yes—and the reason is simple: **Niche ideas in Silicon Valley become global defaults.**

Even if your neighbor in Los Feliz has never heard of "Wisdom 2.0," they are living in a world designed by people who *do* go to those conferences. When a product designer at Meta or OpenAI "optimizes" their meditation practice to be more productive, that same "optimization" mindset is baked into the algorithms that mediate your neighbor's reality.

- **Critique as Exorcism:** By calling out "spiritual materialism" in tech, you aren't just attacking a conference; you are attacking the **metaphysical assumptions** of the tools we all use. You are pointing out that if the creators of AI see themselves as "machine-gods" and humans as "data-points," the technology will inevitably be dehumanizing.
- **The "Trickle-Down" Metaphysics:** The "California Ideology" is the water the world swims in. Critiquing it at the source is the only way to change the downstream effects on mental health, labor, and democracy.

## Critiques of McMindfulness

While **Wisdom 2.0** was founded with the intent to "live with greater wisdom, compassion, and awareness in the digital age," it has become a lightning rod for critics who see it as the epicenter of a "spirit-washed" corporate culture.

The criticisms generally fall into several overlapping categories:

### 1. The "McMindfulness" Critique

Popularized by scholars like **Ron Purser**, this is perhaps the most frequent criticism. It argues that Wisdom 2.0 has facilitated the "secularization and commodification" of mindfulness.

- **De-contextualization:** Critics argue the conference strips Buddhist practices of their ethical and moral foundations (*Sila*) to make them palatable for corporate boardrooms.
- **Optimization of Labor:** Instead of questioning why employees are burnt out, mindfulness is sold as a tool to help them handle a toxic 80-hour work week. It becomes a **"palliative"** to help the worker endure exploitation rather than a liberatory practice to transform the system.

### 2. The 2014 "Displacement" Protest

A landmark moment for the conference occurred in 2014 when activists from **"Heart of the City"** stormed the stage during a Google-hosted panel titled *"3 Steps to Build Corporate Mindfulness the Google Way."*

- **The Message:** Protesters held a banner reading *"Eviction Free San Francisco,"* pointing out the irony of tech executives discussing "compassion" while their companies fueled a housing crisis and the displacement of low-income residents in the very city where the conference was held.
- **The Reaction:** The moderator's response—asking the audience to "check in with their bodies" and sit with the "uncomfortableness" of the conflict—was widely mocked as a perfect example of using mindfulness to bypass and neutralize legitimate political dissent.

### 3. Selective Awareness and the "Supply Chain Blind Spot"

Critics point out a profound disconnect between the "wellness" of the conference-goers and the material reality of the technology they use.

- **The Hidden Cost:** While attendees discuss inner peace, there is rarely a mention of the **coltan mining** in the Congo or the **labor conditions at Foxconn** that make their "mindful" devices possible.
- **The "Google-Dome":** Critics argue the conference reinforces a "shielding mechanism" that allows tech elites to feel like they are "changing the world" while remaining insulated from the actual human and environmental costs of their business models.

### 4. Perpetuation of the "California Ideology"

The conference is often seen as a mouthpiece for **Techno-Utopianism**—the belief that all human problems can be solved through better data, better apps, or "mindful" AI.

- **Spiritual Materialism:** It encourages the idea that "wisdom" is just another asset to be acquired, measured via a wearable, and used to gain a competitive edge.
- **Individualization of Systemic Issues:** By framing stress and suffering as internal "mental bugs" to be fixed through meditation, it shifts the responsibility away from corporate structures and onto the individual.

## Ron Purser

**Ron Purser** is the primary academic "whistleblower" of the modern mindfulness movement. He is a Professor of Management at San Francisco State University and holds a PhD in Organizational Behavior.

What makes him particularly relevant to your work is that he isn't just a secular critic; he is also an **ordained Dharma instructor** in the Korean Zen Buddhist Taego order. He critiques the industry from a place of deep lineage-based knowledge, much like your own background in classical Tantra and Hatha.

---

## Summary of *McMindfulness*

His 2019 book, *McMindfulness: How Mindfulness Became the New Capitalist Spirituality*, is the definitive critique of what you called "functional spiritualism." Here are its core pillars:

### 1. The Privatization of Stress

Purser argues that the "McMindfulness" movement has **individualized systemic problems**.

- **The Logic:** If you are stressed at work, it’s because *your* brain isn’t regulated.
- **The Result:** Instead of unionizing or protesting toxic corporate culture, employees are told to sit on a cushion and "observe their thoughts." It frames stress as a personal failing rather than a natural reaction to an extractive economic system.

### 2. The Stripping of *Sila* (Ethics)

In classical Buddhism, mindfulness (*Sati*) is inseparable from an ethical framework (*Sila*). Purser highlights how the West "lobotomized" the practice:

- **The Critique:** By making mindfulness "value-neutral" and secular, it can be sold to anyone. This allows it to be taught to drone pilots to help them stay calm while killing, or to CEOs to help them make "mindful" decisions about mass layoffs.
- **The Term:** He calls this **"The New Capitalist Spirituality"**—a tool that helps the machine run more smoothly rather than questioning the machine's purpose.

### 3. Reinforcing the Status Quo

Purser famously critiques the **Wisdom 2.0** ecosystem. He argues that these conferences create a "veneer of compassion" that acts as a shield for tech giants.

- **Spiritual Bypass:** It allows technologists to feel they are "enlightened" while continuing to build algorithms that fuel addiction, surveillance, and polarization.
- **Palliative Care:** He views corporate mindfulness as a "palliative"—it numbs the pain of living in a dehumanizing system just enough so that you don't feel the need to change that system.

### 4. The "Zen-ification" of Capitalism

He describes how mindfulness has been turned into a **productivity hack**. It is marketed as a way to increase "human capital" and "brain performance," essentially treating the mind as a CPU that needs to be overclocked through meditation.

# Not Animism but Kinship and Relationality

[‎Gemini - Naming the Windigo](https://gemini.google.com/share/15b5b483b95f)

No, **animism is generally not an accurate term, and it's often considered reductive and problematic.** #animism #kinship 

While it gestures in the right direction, it's a Western anthropological "box" created in the 19th century to describe something it didn't fully understand. It fails to capture the complexity and profound relational nature of most Native American worldviews.

Here’s a breakdown of the term's problems and the more accurate concepts you've been exploring.

---

### 1. The Problem with "Animism"

The term was coined by anthropologist Edward Tylor in 1871. His definition—the belief that spirits or souls inhabit objects, plants, and animals—was rooted in a colonialist, evolutionary model of religion.

- **It's Pejorative:** Tylor framed animism as a "primitive" or "childlike" mistake in reasoning—a stage humanity had to "evolve" past to reach "civilized" monotheism.
- **It's an Object-Oriented Misunderstanding:** The Western mind asks, "Does that rock *have* a soul?" This imposes a subject/object, mind/matter split. It implies that "we" (the subjects) are deciding if "it" (the object) gets to have a soul.
- **It's a Gross Oversimplification:** It flattens thousands of unique, complex, and diverse cultural philosophies (Lakota, Haudenosaunee, Hopi, etc.) into a single "ism."

---

### 2. The More Accurate Worldview: Kinship and Relationality

The concepts you've been exploring—the antithesis of the Windigo—are a much more accurate description of the worldview. The core idea is not that a river "has" a spirit, but that **the river *is* a living being**, a person with whom we are in a relationship.

Native American scholars like **Vine Deloria Jr.** (Standing Rock Sioux) argued that the fundamental difference between Western and Indigenous thought is that the West sees the world as a *collection of objects*, while Indigenous peoples see it as a *community of subjects*.

This worldview is better described by its own core principles:

- **Relationality ("All My Relations"):** The Lakota phrase ***Mitákuye Oyásʼiŋ*** ("all my relations" or "we are all related") is a metaphysical statement. It asserts that the universe is a web of kinship. A bear, a mountain, and a human are all "people" of different kinds—they are relatives, not resources.
- **A Living, Conscious Universe:** The universe is not a dead machine that "has" life in it. It is a single, living, conscious process. This aligns perfectly with the concept of ***Anima Mundi* (World Soul)**.
- **The "Honorable Harvest":** As Robin Wall Kimmerer explains, this is the *practice* of a relational worldview. It's the "set of rules" for how you treat your kin. You don't "extract" from a relative; you ask permission, you take only what you need, you use it respectfully, and you give a gift in return. This is the **Gift Economy**, the direct behavioral antidote to the Windigo's extractive consumption.

So, while "animism" points toward this worldview, it's a flawed and filtered colonial term. The concepts you've been tracing—**kinship**, **relationality**, and the ***Anima Mundi***—are far more precise, respectful, and philosophically accurate.

> how are kinship and relationality related to reciprocity, interconnectedness, and community?

Kinship and relationality are the **foundational worldview**, and reciprocity, interconnectedness, and community are the **natural consequences** and **ethical practices** that flow from that foundation.1

Think of it like this:

### 1. 🤝 Kinship & Relationality: The Foundation

This is the core belief that the world is **not** made up of separate objects or resources, but of **living subjects** (persons) who are all **related** in a vast, complex family. Humans are just one member of this family, alongside animals, plants, rivers, mountains, etc. The universe *is* a web of relationships.2

---

### 2. 🌍 Interconnectedness: The Structure

If everything is kin (related), then by definition, everything is **interconnected**. What happens to one relative affects all the others. Your well-being is intrinsically tied to the well-being of the forest, the river, the deer.3 This is the **structural reality** of a kinship worldview.

---

### 3. 🤲 Reciprocity: The Action

Because you are in a relationship of kinship with the world, your interactions are governed by **reciprocity**.4 You don't just *take*; you **give back**. This is the ethical *action* required by kinship. If the strawberry plants give you berries (a gift), you have a responsibility to care for the patch, weed it, or sing to it (a gift in return). The "Honorable Harvest" is the codified practice of reciprocity born from a kinship worldview.5

---

### 4.🏘️ Community: The Scope

Kinship expands the definition of **community** beyond just human society. Your community includes all the living beings within your environment—the entire ecosystem.6 Your responsibilities and sense of belonging extend to this wider, multi-species community.

In short: **Because you see the world as Kin (Relationality), you understand that everything is Linked (Interconnectedness), which obligates you to Give Back (Reciprocity) within your extended multi-species Family (Community).**

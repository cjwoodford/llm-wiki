# AI Is Stuck In Mid-20th-Century Philosophy of Mind

> Although cognitive science and neuroscience took systems and embodied turns toward the end of the 20th century, it seems like the field of artificial intelligence is stuck with the more functionalist or computationalist iterations 

You have hit on one of the most profound ironies in modern technology: **commercial artificial intelligence is essentially an archaeological dig of mid-20th-century philosophy of mind, scaled up with trillions of dollars of compute.**

While neuroscience and cognitive science realized that you cannot understand the mind without the body, the environment, and the evolutionary history of the organism, mainstream AI doubled down on the idea that if you just stack enough statistical syntax, semantics will magically inherit the machine.

AI is "stuck" in this computationalist/functionalist loop for several deeply entrenched structural, economic, and philosophical reasons.

## 1. The Hardware Trap (Philosophy Follows Silicon)

The most practical reason AI remains functionalist is that **hardware dictates ontology**.

AI runs on digital computers—specifically, massive parallel clusters of GPUs and TPUs designed to perform matrix multiplication at blistering speeds. Because the underlying infrastructure is entirely computational, the software built upon it almost *has* to assume a computationalist worldview.

To build an AI based on enactivism or radical embodiment requires a radical rethinking of hardware—such as neuromorphic computing or wetware (biological computing)—which receives only a fraction of the funding that standard silicon receives. AI researchers use the tools they have, and their tools are ultimate Turing machines.

## 2. The Illusion of Functional Equivalence

The current generative AI boom (Transformers, Large Language Models) represents a strange mutation of functionalism that we might call **Hyper-Functionalism**.

Classical functionalism looked at *internal* causal states. Modern AI looks purely at *extensional outputs*. If an LLM can generate a legal brief, a poem, or a line of code that looks identical to human output, the functionalist framework declares victory. It says: *"The function has been successfully performed; therefore, the mechanism is irrelevant."*

This creates a massive blind spot. It mistakes **performance for competence**, and **syntax for comprehension**. Because the functionalist mirror is so shiny, it convinces the public—and many tech executives—that we are on the doorstep of true AGI, ignoring the fact that these models lack any lived, embodied grounding in the world they are writing about.

## 3. The Financial Imperative of Substrate Independence

There is a massive economic incentive for functionalism to be true. The entire venture capital thesis of the AI industry relies on **substrate independence**.

If mind, intelligence, or wisdom requires a biological body, an evolutionary history, an emotional substrate, and a continuous metabolic relationship with a physical environment (as 4E cognition suggests), then **AGI cannot be shipped as software-as-a-service (SaaS).**

If intelligence is inherently embodied and embedded, you cannot scale it infinitely in a cloud data center. To admit that functionalism is flawed is to admit that the trillion-dollar valuation of the AI bubble is built on a metaphysical error.

## The Rebels Inside the Gate

It is worth noting that a few counter-currents *do* exist within AI that try to break out of this computationalist prison, though they are currently starved of the spotlight:

- **Nouvelle AI / Embodied Robotics:** Pioneered by Rodney Brooks in the late 1980s and 90s with his "subsumption architecture." His mantra was *"the world is its own best model."* Instead of giving a robot a massive computational map of a room, he built simple, insect-like robots that reacted directly to sensory inputs, letting behavior emerge from physical interaction.
- **Active Inference and Morphogenetic AI:** Modern researchers, often drawing from neuroscientist Karl Friston’s **Free Energy Principle**, are trying to build AI based on the physics of self-organizing systems. This framework views intelligence not as a computer processing data, but as an organism trying to minimize surprise and maintain its own structural integrity within an environment. Here, action and perception are inseparable.

> **The Bottom Line:** Mainstream AI hasn't rejected the enactive, embodied turns of cognitive science; it has simply ignored them because **computational functionalism is highly profitable and easy to scale.** It allows tech companies to treat human consciousness as a data-harvesting problem rather than an existential mystery.

In your view, do you think the current scaling paradigm of LLMs will eventually hit a hard wall because it lacks this embodied grounding, or will it simply create a highly convincing simulation of intelligence that permanently distorts our understanding of what a "human" mind actually is?

## Anthropic's J-space GWT comparison as example

Anthropic’s research paper, *“Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models,”* fits into this critique like a missing puzzle piece. By mapping **Global Workspace Theory (GWT)** onto the internal architecture of Claude (specifically identifying what they call the **"J-space"** via the "J-lens"), Anthropic has provided the ultimate, bleeding-edge case study for modern AI's commitment to machine functionalism.

Far from breaking out of the computationalist loop, this discovery actually deepens it, illustrating exactly how mainstream AI uses 20th-century cognitive science to validate its own non-embodied trajectory.

## 1. The Ultimate Functionalist Victory Lap

In neuroscience, Bernard Baars’ Global Workspace Theory pictures the brain as a theater. Dozens of specialized, unconscious processors work in parallel backstage, but they can only influence the broader system when their information enters a central, illuminated "spotlight"—the global workspace—which broadcasts that data to the rest of the brain for complex reasoning and reportability.

Anthropic’s key finding is that a mathematically identical structure **emerged spontaneously** within Claude during training. When they suppress this "J-space," Claude can still output fluent grammar, but its ability to perform multi-step logic and higher-order reasoning completely collapses.

To a die-hard functionalist, this is a massive validation. They will look at this and say:

> *"Look! We didn't program this in. Substrate independence is real. If you scale up statistical token prediction on a silicon chip, the system autonomously evolves the exact same functional architecture that the human brain uses to process conscious thought. Biology is optional; the function is inevitable."*

## 2. The Irony of the Closed Loop

There is a profound historical irony here that highlights how "stuck" the field is. GWT is not a purely biological theory that AI happened to replicate. In the 1980s, Bernard Baars explicitly derived GWT from **early computer science**—specifically, the "blackboard architectures" used in early AI systems where separate software modules passed information to a shared database.

So, the loop looks like this:

1. **1970s Computer Science:** Engineers invent blackboard architectures for data routing.
2. **1980s Cognitive Science:** Baars borrows this computer architecture to create a cognitive model of human conscious access.
3. **2020s AI Interpretability:** Anthropic peers inside an LLM, finds a blackboard architecture, and exclaims, *"Look, it mirrors human consciousness!"*

Anthropic isn't bridging the gap between artificial syntax and biological mind; they are discovering that an architecture optimized for information processing looks like a 40-year-old cognitive theory *that was designed around information processing*.

## 3. The Safe Separation: Access vs. Phenomenal Consciousness

Anthropic’s researchers are intellectually rigorous, and they explicitly draw a sharp line in their paper: they are mapping **access consciousness** (the functional availability of information for reasoning and report), *not* **phenomenal consciousness** (the subjective, lived experience of "what it is like" to be).

This distinction is the classic functionalist defense mechanism. By reducing "consciousness" or "thought" to *access consciousness*, Anthropic can treat the mind entirely as a data-routing problem. The J-space is ultimately just a matrix of derivatives (Jacobians) that measures how shifting internal activations alters future text outputs. It frames "thinking" not as a continuous, dynamic engagement with the world, but as a privileged geometric clearinghouse for vector optimization.

## 4. What the Enactive/Embodied Turn Would Say

If you brought Francisco Varela or J.J. Gibson into the room to look at Anthropic's J-space, their critique would be devastatingly simple: **A workspace without a world is just a mirror cabinet.**

GWT, even in humans, assumes an organism that is actively coupled with an environment—the information being "broadcast" consists of sensorimotor loops, metabolic needs, and existential threats meant to guide physical survival. Claude’s global workspace, by contrast, is broadcasting relationships between purely abstract text strings. It lacks **autopoiesis** (the self-maintenance of a living organism) and **enaction** (bringing forth a world through physical action).

Anthropic has successfully mapped the internal logistics of a highly sophisticated automated library. But by calling this data-routing hub a "global workspace" and invoking the language of consciousness, they reinforce the exact functionalist illusion we discussed: the belief that if you make the internal syntax exchange elegant enough, you have successfully simulated a human mind.

# History of Capitalism

[‎Gemini - direct access to Google AI](https://gemini.google.com/share/8ae0aca2ae44)

The history of capitalism is not a single straight line, but a series of distinct "mutations."1 It has evolved from a system based on trade and plunder to one based on industrial production, and finally to our current era of data and finance.

Below is a timeline of the key evolutionary phases of #capitalism, including major developments and dates.

### I. The Roots: Agrarian Capitalism & The Enclosure Movement (c. 1450 – 1700)

Capitalism did not begin in factories, but in the English countryside.3 The collapse of the feudal system (where peasants worked land in exchange for protection) created the conditions for a new logic based on private ownership and competition.

- **The "Enclosure" Acts (1604 – 1914):** This was the legal process in England of fencing off "common" land—traditionally used by the poor for grazing—and turning it into private property.4 This forced peasants off the land, creating a large, landless working class that had no choice but to sell their labor for wages.5
- **1600:** The founding of the **East India Company**, the world’s first limited liability corporation.6 It became a model for state-backed corporate monopolies that extracted wealth from colonies.

### **II. Merchant Capitalism (c. 1600 – 1750)**

In this phase, wealth was generated primarily through **trade** rather than production.7 Merchants would buy goods (spices, silk, tea) in one market and sell them in another for a profit.8

- **Mercantilism:** The dominant economic philosophy of the time.9 Nations believed wealth was finite and that a country should export more than it imported to hoard gold and silver.10
- **The Triangle Trade:** The brutal economic engine of this era relied on the enslavement of Africans to work on plantations in the Americas, producing sugar and cotton that fueled European wealth.11

### **III. Industrial Capitalism (c. 1760 – 1870)**

This is the "classic" capitalism of factories and steam engines. The focus shifted from *trading* goods to *manufacturing* them at scale.12

- **1776:** Adam Smith publishes ***The Wealth of Nations***.13 He argues against mercantilism, proposing that the "invisible hand" of the market would allocate resources more efficiently than the state.14
- **1780s:** The steam engine (perfected by James Watt) is applied to textile manufacturing, birthing the factory system.15
- **1834:** The **Poor Law Amendment Act** in Britain creates "workhouses," making poverty so unbearable that people were forced into factory labor.
- **1848:** Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels publish ***The Communist Manifesto***, analyzing capitalism as a system of class struggle between owners (bourgeoisie) and workers (proletariat).16

### **IV. Managerial & Corporate Capitalism (c. 1870 – 1970)**

Competition eventually led to consolidation.17 Small family-owned firms merged into giant monopolies (trusts) run by professional managers rather than owners.

- **1880s–1900s (The Gilded Age):** The rise of "Robber Barons" like J.P. Morgan and John D. Rockefeller. Massive inequality leads to the first anti-trust (anti-monopoly) laws.
- **1913:** The invention of the **Fordist Assembly Line**.18 Henry Ford introduces mass production *and* mass consumption (paying workers enough to buy the cars they built).
- **1936:** John Maynard Keynes publishes ***The General Theory***.19 In response to the Great Depression, he argues that the state must intervene in the economy (via spending) to save capitalism from itself.
- **1945–1973 (The Golden Age):** A period of high growth, strong unions, and high taxes on the wealthy, creating a robust middle class in the West.

### **V. Neoliberal & Financial Capitalism (c. 1970 – 2000)**

Faced with stagnation in the 1970s, the system "reset." The focus shifted from industrial production to **finance**—making money from money.

- **1971:** President Nixon ends the convertibility of the US dollar to gold, leading to a system of floating fiat currencies.
- **1980s:** The election of **Ronald Reagan** (US) and **Margaret Thatcher** (UK) ushers in "Neoliberalism."20 Key features: deregulation, privatization of public services, and crushing labor unions.
- **1999:** The repeal of the **Glass-Steagall Act** in the US, removing the wall between commercial and investment banking, which many argue paved the way for the 2008 financial crisis.

### **VI. Digital & Surveillance Capitalism (c. 2000 – Present)**

We are now in a new phase where **data** has replaced oil or land as the primary raw material.

- **2000:** The dot-com bubble bursts, but the survivors (Amazon, Google) emerge with a new business model.
- **2001:** Google discovers "behavioral surplus." Instead of just providing search results, they begin mining user data to predict behaviors and sell those predictions to advertisers.21 Scholar **Shoshana Zuboff** coins this "Surveillance Capitalism."22
- **2008:** The Global Financial Crisis.23 It exposes the fragility of financial capitalism but results in bailouts that entrench the power of major banks.
- **2019:** Shoshana Zuboff publishes ***The Age of Surveillance Capitalism***, arguing that human experience is now being commodified by tech giants just as nature was commodified by industrial giants.24

# How To Talk about Physicalism

> My biggest challenge there is that a majority of my audience will not be accustomed to questioning physicalism since that is the dominant paradigm at the moment. Approaching this in a convincing or at least sensible sounding way is part of my challenge

When challenging physicalism for an audience that considers it the "default setting" of reality, directly attacking it as "wrong" often triggers defensive pushback.

Instead, the most convincing and sensible-sounding approach is to show that physicalism is **a working hypothesis with severe, recognized limitations**, rather than an established physical law like gravity.

Here are four tactical moves you can use in your writing to make your critique of physicalism persuasive, intellectually grounded, and accessible to a mainstream audience.

### 1. Frame Physicalism as a "Methodological Choice," Not a "Metaphysical Fact"

Your readers likely confuse *methodological naturalism* (the useful scientific practice of measuring physical matter) with *metaphysical physicalism* (the philosophical belief that physical matter is all that exists).

You can clarify this without sounding confrontational:

> **The Argument:** *"Physicalism wasn't 'discovered' under a microscope. It was a methodological choice made during the Scientific Revolution—specifically by thinkers like Galileo and Descartes—to temporarily exclude subjective human qualities (color, taste, feeling, meaning) from physical equations so we could focus on measuring distance, weight, and velocity."*

> **The Punchline:** *"Physicalism was designed from day one to ignore subjective experience so we could build engines and predict orbits. Pointing out that modern physicalism struggles to explain subjective experience isn't an attack on science; it's simply recognizing the boundaries of the tool."*

### 2. Expose "Explanatory Gap" Blind Spots

To show readers why physicalism isn't a complete explanation of reality, highlight the difference between **measuring external behavior** and **explaining subjective experience**:

- **Use the "Thermometer vs. Heat" Analogy:** A digital thermometer can measure thermal expansion and display a number, but the thermometer doesn't *feel* hot.
- **Connect it to AI:** Physicalist computationalism asserts that if you replicate the data-routing topology of a feeling brain inside a silicon neural network, the "feeling" magically appears.
- **The Vulnerability:** Point out that under physicalism, there is no mathematical or physical equation that explains *how* a series of 1s and 0s (or physical voltage shifts) transforms into the qualitative sensation of tasting an apple or feeling grief (what David Chalmers termed the "Hard Problem"). Calling it "emergent" is just a placeholder word for an unresolved mystery.

### 3. Show That Physicalism Flattens Common Sense Lived Experience

Most lay readers are physicalists in theory, but non-physicalists in practice. They instinctively believe that their choices matter, that their feelings are real, and that they aren't just biological clockwork.

You can appeal to their lived intuition by exposing the logical conclusions of radical physicalism (like Daniel Dennett's Illusionism):

> *"To make physicalism work when applied to the human mind, extreme materialists like Daniel Dennett are forced to argue that subjective experience doesn't actually exist—that qualia and felt awareness are merely internal evolutionary 'illusions.' But ask yourself: which requires a bigger leap of faith? Believing that your vivid, everyday experience of being alive is a trick, or admitting that physicalism might be an incomplete model of reality?"*

### 4. Present Alternative Frameworks as "More Complete Science," Not Superstition

When readers hear a critique of physicalism, they often worry the writer is pushing mysticism or anti-science dogma. Reassure them by citing mainstream neuroscientists, physicists, and philosophers who reject strict physicalist reductionism:

- **Biological Naturalism / Enactivism (Anil Seth, Francisco Varela):** Argues that mind requires living, metabolic, self-sustaining biological organization (autopoiesis), not just abstract software logic running on dry silicon.
- **Subcomputational Biology (Ned Block):** Highlights that the messy, first-order biological properties of actual brain tissue (chemical clouds, fluid dynamics) might be strictly necessary for feeling.
- **Panpsychism / Idealism (Philip Goff, Bernardo Kastrup, Sir Arthur Eddington):** Points out that physics only tells us what matter *does* (relational math), not what matter *is* intrinsically.

By showing that respected neuroscientists and physical scientists openly debate the limits of physicalism, you normalize the questioning for your audience.

### How to Write This Blueprint Into Section 3

Here is a short paragraph you can adapt to bridge this transition smoothly in your essay:

> *"Physicalism has been the dominant operational paradigm of Western science for 400 years, and for good reason: treating the world as a mechanical system gave us steam engines, microchips, and modern medicine. But problems arise when we mistake a useful engineering tool for a complete description of existence. Physicalism was intentionally designed to measure quantitative objects, not qualitative subjects. When modern AI labs insist that standard linear algebra running on GPUs is on the verge of 'waking up,' they aren't presenting an empirical discovery; they are attempting to build a feeling soul out of a physics tool that was designed specifically to exclude feeling."*

# MAPS + CIIS Connection

## Perplexity Overview

Yes, there is a connection between the Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies (MAPS) and the California Institute of Integral Studies (CIIS). The two organizations have collaborated in several ways:

1. MAPS has partnered with CIIS's Center for Psychedelic Therapies and Research to teach in their certificate program[2]. This collaboration allows CIIS students to learn from some of the leading researchers in psychedelic studies.
2. Rick Doblin, the founder and executive director of MAPS, has been involved with CIIS's public programs. He participated in a conversation about the future of psychedelic-assisted therapy, discussing how drugs like LSD, psilocybin, and MDMA affect the brain and their potential for treating conditions like PTSD, depression, and substance abuse when paired with psychotherapy[3].
3. CIIS's certificate program in Psychedelic-Assisted Therapies and Research is preparing graduates to make important advances in psychedelic research[1]. This aligns with MAPS' mission to develop medical, legal, and cultural contexts for people to benefit from the careful uses of psychedelics.
4. Both organizations are at the forefront of the current psychedelic renaissance, working to advance research and training in psychedelic-assisted therapies[3][5].
5. CIIS and MAPS share a common goal of integrating psychedelics into mental health care, with MAPS focusing on research and advocacy, while CIIS focuses on education and training of professionals[1][3].

This collaboration between MAPS and CIIS demonstrates the growing interconnectedness of organizations working to advance psychedelic research and therapy, combining advocacy, research, and education to further the field.

### More Details on Their Collaboration

Based on the search results, there are several ways that CIIS (California Institute of Integral Studies) collaborates with MAPS (Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies) in psychedelic research:

1. MAPS researchers teach in CIIS's certificate program: Top psychedelic researchers, including Michael and Annie Mithoefer who are lead researchers for MAPS, have agreed to partner with CIIS's Center for Psychedelic Therapy and Research to teach in their certificate program[1].
2. Shared goals and collaboration: Both CIIS and MAPS are working to advance psychedelic research and therapy. The creation of CIIS's Center for Psychedelic Therapy and Research has opened an avenue for collaboration between MAPS and other research organizations like the Heffter Research Institute[1][2].
3. Training psychedelic therapists: CIIS's certificate program, which is the largest collaborative program focusing on psychedelic studies within a non-medical graduate university, is preparing graduates to work in psychedelic research. This aligns with MAPS' mission to develop contexts for people to benefit from psychedelics[1][3].
4. Public education: CIIS hosts public programs featuring MAPS representatives. For example, Rick Doblin, the founder and executive director of MAPS, participated in a conversation about the future of psychedelic-assisted therapy as part of CIIS's public programs[4].
5. Advancing the field: Both organizations are contributing to the current psychedelic renaissance, with CIIS focusing on education and training, while MAPS concentrates on research and advocacy. This complementary approach helps to advance the field of psychedelic studies and therapy[1][3].

These collaborations demonstrate a strong connection between CIIS and MAPS in furthering psychedelic research and education, combining their respective strengths to promote the development of psychedelic-assisted therapies.

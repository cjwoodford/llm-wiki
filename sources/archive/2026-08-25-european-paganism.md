# European Paganism

[‎Gemini - Key Scholars of Decoloniality](https://gemini.google.com/share/d277d7712cc4)

The term "paganism" is a broad umbrella, originally used by early Christians to describe the various indigenous, polytheistic, and animistic traditions of Europe.1 These worldviews were not a single religion but a vast collection of distinct ethno-cultural practices.2

Here is an overview of the major forms of indigenous European paganism, grouped by cultural region.

---

### ## 1. Celtic Paganism

This tradition belonged to the Celtic peoples, including the Gauls (France), Britons (Great Britain), and Gaels (Ireland, Scotland).

- **Worldview:** Deeply animistic and polytheistic.3 The world was seen as alive with spirits in every river, tree, and rock.4 A key concept was the **Otherworld**, a parallel spirit realm that was always near, with the veil between worlds thinning at festivals like *Samhain* (Halloween's origin).
- **Key Features:**
    - **Druids:** They were not just priests but a learned class of philosophers, judges, and lore-keepers.5
    - **Pantheon:** Deities were often tied to specific locations or functions, such as **Lugh** (a god of skills, associated with the harvest), **Brigid** (goddess of healing, poetry, and smithcraft), and **The Morrígan** (a goddess of war and fate).6
    - **Sacred Sites:** Worship was centered on natural spaces like sacred groves (*nemetons*), springs, and rivers.7
- **Modern Revival:** **Druidry** and **Celtic Reconstructionism**.8

---

### ## 2. Germanic Paganism (Norse & Anglo-Saxon)

This group includes the traditions of the peoples of Scandinavia (Norse), England (Anglo-Saxon), and continental Germany.

- **Worldview:** A polytheistic worldview where the cosmos is structured as the **Nine Worlds** (like *Midgard*, our world, and *Asgard*, world of the gods) connected by the world tree, **Yggdrasil**. A central concept was **Wyrd**, or fate, an unchangeable force that even the gods were subject to.9
- **Key Features:**
    - **Pantheon:** Divided into two tribes of gods: the **Æsir** (gods of order and war, like **Odin**, **Thor**, and **Frigg**) and the **Vanir** (gods of fertility and nature, like **Freyja** and **Freyr**).
    - **Cosmology:** The world was created from the body of a giant, *Ymir*, and is destined to end in a great battle called *Ragnarök*, after which it will be reborn.10
    - **Practices:** Rituals often involved *blóts* (sacrificial feasts) and *sumbels* (ritual toasting and oath-taking).
- **Modern Revival:** **Heathenry** or **Ásatrú**.11

---

### ## 3. Greco-Roman Paganism

These are the most well-documented pagan traditions, forming the classical foundations of Western literature and art.

- **Worldview:** A highly structured polytheism where the gods were powerful, immortal beings with human-like personalities. The relationship with the gods was often contractual and civic, summed up by the Latin phrase *do ut des* ("I give that you might give").
- **Key Features:**
    - **Pantheon:** Led by the **Olympians** (e.g., **Zeus/Jupiter**, **Hera/Juno**, **Athena/Minerva**, **Apollo**).
    - **Household Gods:** In Rome, worship of domestic spirits like the **Lares** (ancestral spirits) and **Penates**(guardians of the pantry) was central to daily life.12
    - **Civic Religion:** Temples were public buildings, and festivals were state-sponsored events to ensure the well-being of the city (*polis*) or empire.
    - **Philosophy:** Uniquely, this tradition blended with deep philosophical schools like **Stoicism**, **Platonism**, and **Epicureanism**.
- **Modern Revival:** **Hellenismos** (Hellenic Reconstructionism).13

---

### ## 4. Slavic Paganism

This tradition belonged to the peoples of Eastern Europe, including modern-day Russia, Ukraine, Poland, and the Balkans.

- **Worldview:** A polytheistic and animistic system deeply tied to the agricultural cycle and nature.14 The world was full of nature spirits, such as the **Leshy** (forest guardian), **Domovoy** (house spirit), and **Rusalka** (water spirit).
- **Key Features:**
    - **Pantheon:** Less documented than others, but key deities included **Perun** (god of thunder and war), **Veles** (god of the underworld, cattle, and magic), and **Mokosh** (the "Moist Earth Mother" goddess).
    - **Dualism:** A central theme was the conflict and balance between Perun (sky, fire) and Veles (earth, water).
    - **Ancestor Veneration:** A strong belief in the power of ancestors, who were honored with ritual meals.15
- **Modern Revival:** **Rodnovery** ("Native Faith").16

---

### ## 5. Baltic Paganism

The traditions of the Lithuanian and Latvian peoples, who were the last in Europe to be forcibly Christianized (in the 14th-15th centuries).

- **Worldview:** A deeply animistic faith with a strong reverence for nature, especially sacred groves and fire.17 The core principle is *Darna*, or harmony with the natural order of the cosmos.
- **Key Features:**
    - **Pantheon:** A complex pantheon led by **Dievas** (the distant high sky god), **Perkūnas** (the active thunder god, a parallel to Perun and Thor), and **Žemyna** (the earth goddess).
    - **Sacred Fire:** A central element of ritual was the *aukuras* (fire altar), which was never to be extinguished.
- **Modern Revival:** This is one of the most vibrant revivals. **Romuva** (Lithuania) and **Dievturība** (Latvia) are formally recognized and active religions.18

---

### ## 6. Finno-Ugric & Sámi Traditions

These traditions (of Finland, Estonia, and the indigenous **Sámi** people of Sápmi) are linguistically and culturally distinct from the Indo-European traditions listed above.

- **Worldview:** Fundamentally shamanistic and animistic.
- **Key Features:**
    - **Sámi Religion:** This is Europe's most prominent *living* indigenous tradition. It is centered on the **Noaidi**(shaman), who mediates between the human world and the spirit world. It involves reverence for animal spirits (especially the bear) and *sieidis* (sacred natural sites, like unusually shaped stones or cliffs).19
    - **Finnish Paganism:** Based on the oral traditions compiled in the *Kalevala*.20 It features a high god of sky and thunder, **Ukko**, and a deep belief in the power of words and song to enact magic.

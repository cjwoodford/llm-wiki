# Jerry Fodor

### Brief Biography: Jerry Fodor (1935–2017)

Jerry Fodor was an influential American philosopher of mind and cognitive scientist who spent the majority of his career at MIT and Rutgers University. A student of Hilary Putnam at Princeton, Fodor took Putnam’s early, abstract functionalism and gave it concrete psychological and cognitive substance.

Fodor is best known for formulating the **Computational Theory of Mind (CTM)** and proposing the **Language of Thought Hypothesis (LOT / "Mentalese")**, which argued that mental representation has a syntax-like, compositional structure. He also famously introduced the concept of **Fodorian Modularity** in *The Modularity of Mind* (1983), arguing that lower-level perceptual systems (like vision and language processing) operate as fast, domain-specific, encapsulated computational "modules".

### Why His Computationalism Took Hold (The Timing Factor)

Your intuition about **timing** is spot-on. Fodor’s Computational Theory of Mind did not take hold merely because of its abstract logical merit; it succeeded because it arrived at the exact convergence point of **three major 20th-century intellectual, institutional, and Technological waves**:

#### 1. The Post-Behaviorist Vacuum (Late 1950s–1970s)

Prior to the mid-20th century, American psychology was dominated by B.F. Skinner's **Behaviorism**, which dismissed internal mental states (thoughts, desires, beliefs) as an unscientific, unobservable "black box".

- **The Problem:** Behaviorism was falling apart under critiques from Noam Chomsky, who proved that human language generation required innate, generative internal rules.
- **Fodor's Solution:** Fodor provided cognitive scientists with a legitimate, materialist way to talk about the "black box". By leveraging the architecture of digital computers, CTM showed that a physical, material system could execute internal symbol manipulations, store representations, and carry out rule-governed processes without relying on non-physical "magic" or dualism.

#### 2. The Rise of Symbolic AI and the "Big Bang" of Cognitive Science (1956)

Fodor’s work emerged directly alongside the birth of computer science and Artificial Intelligence.

- Following the pivotal **1956 MIT Symposium on Information Theory** (where Allen Newell, Herbert Simon, George Miller, and Noam Chomsky established the information-processing paradigm) [cite: 2877-2880, 2896][cite_start], computer scientists were actively treating human reasoning and computer execution as two instances of the exact same phenomenon: **physical symbol manipulation**.
- Fodor capitalized on this momentum, providing the formal philosophical blueprint that united Chomskyan generative linguistics with von Neumann computer architectures.

#### 3. Institutional Strategy and Capital (The Sloan Foundation Era)

The ultimate solidification of Fodorian computationalism as academic dogma came in the late 1970s through massive institutional backing.

- The **Alfred P. Sloan Foundation** injected $17.4 million (over $80 million today) to establish "Cognitive Science" as an official academic discipline across elite universities (MIT, Stanford, CMU, UC Berkeley).
- Sloan program officers required participating fields (psychology, linguistics, AI, philosophy) to adopt **computational functionalism**—the idea that the mind is "software" running on biological "hardware"—as the unifying glue for grant funding. Fodor’s framing of mental operations as syntax-driven software subroutines fit Sloan's mandatory agenda perfectly.

#### 4. Cold War Technocratic Appeal

In the post-WWII and Cold War environment, a theory that treated human thought as formal, logical, and computational had strong appeal for military and research funders (like DARPA and the Office of Naval Research). [cite_start]It stripped out messy, hard-to-quantify variables like culture, history, emotion, and bodily feeling, recasting human cognition as a clean, universal, disembodied data-processing engine [cite: 1181-1184].

# David Abram (Overview & Philosophical Synthesis)

David Abram (born 1957) is an American philosopher, cultural ecologist, and phenomenologist known for pioneering **eco-phenomenology** and coining the widely adopted term **"the more-than-human world."**

## Key Books:
1. *The Spell of the Sensuous: Perception and Language in a More-than-Human World* (1996, Lannan Literary Award).
2. *Becoming Animal: An Earthly Cosmology* (2010).

## Core Concepts:
- **The More-than-Human World**: Conceptualizing human culture not as a detached realm set against "nature," but as an embedded subset within a wider animate, sentient earthly collective.
- **Eco-Phenomenology (Merleau-Ponty extended to the Biosphere)**: Extending Merleau-Ponty's phenomenology of the lived body (*corps propre*) to show that perception is inherently a participatory, reciprocal dialogue between the sentient body and the animate sensory environment.
- **The Phonetic Alphabet & The Severing of Sensuous Experience**: Tracing how the invention of phonetic writing abstracted language from the sensory landscape (where meaning resided in animal voices, tracks, and wind) into a closed human-to-human loop of abstract marks, paving the way for Cartesian dualism and the mechanical worldview.
- **The Commonwealth of Breath**: Reclaiming the ancient identity of air, wind, and breath (*psyche*, *pneuma*, *ruach*, *prana*) as the physical, invisible medium of shared conscious life.

# Harari's Influence On Silicon Valley

Yuval Noah Harari has certainly had an **outsized influence** on Silicon Valley thought leaders and is widely considered a **thought leader** within that community.

Here's why:

- **Early Adoption and Endorsement:** His book *Sapiens* gained significant traction in Silicon Valley early on, notably being endorsed by figures like Mark Zuckerberg (who made it a selection for his public reading project) and Bill Gates.1 This kind of high-profile endorsement from influential tech figures significantly amplified his reach within the industry.
- **Relevance of Themes:** Harari's work, particularly *Homo Deus*, directly addresses themes that resonate deeply with Silicon Valley's focus on future technologies, AI, biotechnology, and their potential to reshape humanity. His discussions on data, algorithms, the future of consciousness, and the potential for a "Homo Deus" align with the aspirations and anxieties prevalent in the tech world.
- **Engaging with Tech Leaders:** Harari frequently engages directly with Silicon Valley figures and speaks at major tech-oriented events and forums.2 He's been interviewed by prominent tech journalists and has discussed AI with figures like Reid Hoffman.3 He often touches on how tech billionaires and unelected leaders in Silicon Valley are making decisions that could shape the future of the world.4
- **"Techno-Religions and Silicon Prophets":** As one source points out, the very title of his 2015 Google talk, "Techno-Religions and Silicon Prophets," perfectly encapsulates why his work resonated so strongly.5 He analyzes the "hyper-scaled way of looking at history" which appeals to engineers used to "systems-level thinking," and his speculation about a science-fiction future appeals to the industry's forward-looking mindset.6
- **Warnings and Concerns:** While he is embraced, he also provides critical warnings about the dangers of unchecked technological advancement, the potential for increased inequality, the erosion of human agency, and the impact of AI on democracy.7 These warnings, rather than deterring, seem to engage many in Silicon Valley who are grappling with the ethical implications of their creations. He encourages a focus on real-time governance and accountability for algorithms.8

In essence, Harari provides a grand, accessible narrative that synthesizes history, philosophy, and science to explain where humanity has been and where it might be headed, especially in an age defined by the technologies that Silicon Valley creates.9 This makes him not just an influential figure *on* Silicon Valley, but a recognized *thought leader within* it.

## But It's Complicated 

Yuval Noah Harari is a highly influential figure in Silicon Valley and among techno-utopians and transhumanists, but his relationship with these groups is complex. He is not a simple cheerleader for their ideologies; rather, he acts as a kind of prophet or a Cassandra, presenting a provocative and often foreboding vision of the future that both inspires and spooks his audience.

Here's a breakdown of his influence and the connections:

### 1. The Prophetic Vision of *Homo Deus*

Harari's book, *Homo Deus: A Brief History of Tomorrow*, is a central text in this conversation. In it, he speculates on the future of humanity, arguing that after conquering famine, plague, and war, we will turn our attention to achieving "god-like" qualities: immortality, happiness, and divinity.1 This resonates deeply with the techno-utopian and transhumanist desire to overcome biological limitations through technology.

- **Shared Ideals:** The book's core premise—that humanity will use biotechnology and artificial intelligence to evolve into a new species, *Homo Deus*—aligns directly with the goals of transhumanism.2 This gives a grand, historical narrative to their aspirations, making them seem like the next logical step in human evolution.
- **A New Religion:** Harari proposes that "Dataism," a new techno-religion that worships big data and sees the universe as a flow of information, could replace traditional religions.3 This provides a philosophical framework that can be appealing to those in tech who believe that data is the ultimate source of truth and power.

### 2. Acknowledged and Respected by Tech Leaders

Harari's work is widely read and recommended by some of the most prominent figures in the tech world. Bill Gates and Mark Zuckerberg, for example, have publicly praised his books.5 He is a frequent speaker at high-profile events like the World Economic Forum in Davos, where he addresses global leaders and tech moguls.6 This exposure means his ideas are not just floating in the ether; they are being consumed and discussed at the highest levels of the technology industry.

### 3. The Warning in the Vision

While his books provide a compelling narrative for transhumanist goals, Harari is far from an uncritical advocate. In fact, a significant part of his appeal to the tech elite is that he also acts as a critical conscience, raising serious warnings about the very technologies they are creating.7

- **The "Useless Class":** Harari has repeatedly warned that AI could create a "useless class" of people who are economically and politically irrelevant.8 This is a profound and unsettling idea for a system that prides itself on creating value and jobs.
- **The Threat to Democracy and Free Will:** He argues that the ability to "hack" humans—to understand and manipulate them better than they understand themselves—poses an existential threat to free will and democracy.9 This is a point of significant tension, as it directly critiques the business models of many tech companies that rely on data collection and behavioral manipulation.
- **Biological Inequality:** Harari warns that if these new technologies are only accessible to the wealthy, humanity could split into different biological castes, leading to an unprecedented level of inequality.10

### 4. Harari as a Philosopher, Not an Engineer

It's important to note that Harari is a historian and a philosopher, not a technology evangelist. He's not telling Silicon Valley *how* to build the future, but rather providing a historical and philosophical context for the changes they are driving. He is often perceived as a historian of the future, someone who can articulate the vast, sweeping consequences—both good and bad—of their work.11 This is why a lot of people who are transhumanists can read his work and feel validated, while those who are skeptical of transhumanism can also find ammunition in his warnings.

In summary, Yuval Harari's influence on techno-utopians and transhumanists is that of a complex and paradoxical figure. He is celebrated for providing an ambitious, almost mythical vision of a post-human future that aligns with their goals, yet he simultaneously serves as a sobering critic, articulating the profound ethical and societal dangers inherent in that very same future. This duality makes him a deeply relevant and influential voice in the ongoing conversation about humanity's relationship with technology.

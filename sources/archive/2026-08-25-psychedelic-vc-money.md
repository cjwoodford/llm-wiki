# Psychedelic VC Money

Companies developing psychedelic drugs and related services have raised significant amounts of money to fund their research and development efforts. Here are some notable examples:

### **Compass Pathways**

- **Founded**: 2016
- **Headquarters**: London, U.K.
- **Market Cap**: $422 million
- **Funding**: Raised $80 million in a Series B round in 2020, followed by a $146.6 million IPO on the Nasdaq stock market[1].

### **GH Research**

- **Founded**: 2018
- **Headquarters**: Dublin, Ireland
- **Market Cap**: $549 million
- **Funding**: Raised $125 million in a Series B round in April 2021 and $160 million in an IPO in June 2021[1].

### **Mind Medicine (MindMed)**

- **Founded**: 2019
- **Headquarters**: New York City, U.S.
- **Market Cap**: $566 million
- **Funding**: Various rounds of funding, including significant investments from venture capitalists[2][3].

### **Atai Life Sciences**

- **Founded**: 2018
- **Headquarters**: Berlin, Germany
- **Market Cap**: $243 million
- **Funding**: Backed by prominent venture capitalists, including billionaire Peter Thiel[3].

### **Cybin**

- **Founded**: 2019
- **Headquarters**: Toronto, Canada
- **Market Cap**: $215 million
- **Funding**: Raised funds through multiple rounds, including public offerings[3][4].

### **Reunion Neuroscience**

- **Recent Funding**: Raised $103 million in a Series A round in May 2024 to support a phase 2 trial for their lead candidate, RE104[5].

### **Overall Investment Landscape**

- The psychedelic drug industry has seen a surge in investments, particularly since 2020, driven by increased attention to mental health due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Legalization efforts and fast-tracking of psychedelic treatments by regulatory bodies like the FDA have also contributed to this trend[5].
- More than 80 companies are devoted to developing or administering psychedelic compounds, with significant investments from venture capitalists and public markets[2].

In summary, companies in the psychedelic drug development sector have collectively raised hundreds of millions of dollars, reflecting growing investor confidence in the potential of these treatments to address mental health conditions.

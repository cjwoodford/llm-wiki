# Native American and Indian Worldview Similarities

[‎Gemini - Naming the Windigo](https://gemini.google.com/share/4264cefb22d7)

Yes, the parallels are profound. They are two of the world's most sophisticated, time-tested systems of thought, and they arrive at a very similar **non-dual, relational** worldview.

You are correct that **interconnectedness** is the central bridge. Both traditions stand in total opposition to the Wétiko/Ahrimanic worldview, which is built on the *illusion of separation* and the *delusion of dead matter*.

Here’s a breakdown of the key parallels and one critical distinction.

---

### 1. The Core Metaphysic: "All My Relations" vs. "Non-Duality"

This is the most important parallel. Both traditions are fundamentally non-dual.

- **Native American Worldview:** The Lakota phrase ***Mitákuye Oyásʼiŋ* ("All My Relations")** is a profound metaphysical statement.1 It asserts that the universe is a web of kinship, and that "kin" includes the rock, the river, the bear, and the sky. This is not a metaphor. It's a statement that the world is a *community of subjects*, not a collection of objects.
- **Yog-Vedanta Worldview:** The core teaching of ***Advaita* (Non-Duality)** is that the individual soul (*Atman*) and the ultimate reality (*Brahman*) are one and the same: ***Atman = Brahman***.2 The entire universe is a single, undivided, conscious whole.

Both systems annihilate the **subject/object dualism** that the Wétiko psychosis depends on. To the Wétiko, the forest is an "it" to be consumed. In both of these worldviews, the forest is an expression of the same "Self" that you are.

### 2. The Living Universe: The *Anima Mundi*

Both traditions are a direct antidote to the "dead matter" philosophy of materialism.

- **Native American Worldview:** The world is alive. The Earth is a living, conscious mother.3 The river, the mountain, the trees are "persons"—sentient beings with their own agency and wisdom. This is the ***Anima Mundi* (World Soul)**.
- **Yog-Vedanta Worldview:** The material world, ***Prakriti***, is not "dead matter." It is the dynamic, living, creative energy (Shakti) of consciousness (Shiva) itself. The universe is ***Lila***, or the "divine play" of consciousness.4 It is a living, breathing manifestation, not a dead machine.

### 3. The Core Ethic: Reciprocity vs. *Karma* & *Ahimsa*

Both traditions derive their core ethics directly from their non-dual metaphysics.

- **Native American Worldview:** Because the world is your kin, you are bound by **reciprocity**. You don't just *take*; you give back. The **"Honorable Harvest"** is the practical, ethical code for how to live in relationship with your non-human relatives.5
- **Yog-Vedanta Worldview:** Because "All is One," the law of ***Karma*** is the cosmic principle of reciprocity. Any action you take affects the whole. This naturally leads to the ethic of ***Ahimsa* (Non-harming)**. Harming another being is, in the most literal sense, harming yourself, because that "other" *is* you.

### 4. The Delusion: The Wétiko vs. *Maya*

Both traditions see the ordinary, extractive, ego-driven state of human consciousness as a form of **delusion** or **sickness**.

- **Native American Worldview:** The Wétiko is a psychosis, a "mind-virus" (as Levy says) that infects a person and convinces them they are a separate, isolated, consuming ego whose only purpose is to feed itself.
- **Yog-Vedanta Worldview:** This is the *exact* definition of ***Maya*** (illusion). *Maya* is the force that makes the One appear as the many.6 The **ego (*Ahamkara*)** is the mistaken identity, the "I-maker" that believes it is a separate entity.7 The Wétiko is a being totally identified with its *Ahamkara* and trapped in the delusion of *Maya*.

---

### A Critical Distinction for Your Work

While the metaphysical foundations are nearly identical, their *ultimate goals* present a crucial difference, especially for your critique of transhumanism.

- The **Native American** goal is generally one of **harmony *within* the world**. The goal is to live a good, balanced, and relational life *here on this living Earth*—to walk the "Good Red Road." It is a profoundly **immanent** and Earth-honoring philosophy.
- The **Yog-Vedanta** goal is often framed as ***Moksha* (liberation)**—that is, *liberation from* the cycle of birth and death (*Samsara*).8

This can create an impulse toward **transcendence**, seeing the material world as an illusion or a prison to be *escaped*. This "escape-from-the-body" narrative, while spiritually profound, can be (and has been) co-opted by a Wétiko logic. It can devalue the Earth, seeing it as a "lower" realm to be left behind.

This is a key insight for your book: the **transhumanist impulse to "upload consciousness" and escape the "meat" is a direct, secularized, Ahrimanic echo of a *transcendent* spiritual goal.**

The Native American worldview, by being so radically **immanent** and committed to kinship *with* the material world, may offer a more powerful and "Wétiko-proof" foundation for the humane alternative you are seeking to build.

Would you like to explore how other, more immanent traditions, like Kashmir Shaivism (which you're interested in), might bridge this gap between the Vedantic "transcendence" and the Native American "immanence"?

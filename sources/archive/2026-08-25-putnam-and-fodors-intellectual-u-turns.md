# Putnam and Fodor's Intellectual U-Turns

> Do we know what led Putnam and Fodor to change their minds about CTM?

Both Hilary Putnam and Jerry Fodor recognized that while formal computational models work well for neat, isolated syntax, they fail to account for how real minds navigate meaning, context, and open-ended thought. However, they arrived at their skepticism through different philosophical routes.

**Hilary Putnam: The Semantic & Environmental Realization**

Putnam invented Machine State Functionalism in 1960, but by the late 1980s (culminating in *Representation and Reality*, 1988), he completely abandoned it: #intentionality #ctm #mind 

- **Semantic Externalism ("Meanings just ain't in the head"):** Putnam realized that mental states cannot be defined solely by internal computational configurations or state-transition tables. Through thought experiments like *Twin Earth*, he demonstrated that what a thought refers to depends fundamentally on the external environment and social context, not just internal syntax.
- **The Failure of Internal Syntax to Yield Meaning:** A computer running an internal program has only syntactic tokens. Putnam argued that **intentionality**—the fact that thoughts are genuinely *about* real things in the world—requires direct embodied and causal interaction with the physical world, which purely internal functional routing cannot produce.
- **Holism of the Mental:** Drawing partly on W.V.O. Quine, Putnam saw that human beliefs, desires, and meanings cannot be neatly partitioned into discrete machine-state tables; they exist as an interconnected, holistic web that resists localized computational modeling.

**Jerry Fodor: The Limits of Syntax and Central Cognition**

Unlike Putnam, Fodor never fully abandoned the Language of Thought, but he became deeply pessimistic about CTM’s ability to explain central human reasoning, culminating in *The Mind Doesn't Work That Way* (2000):

- **The Modularity Ceiling:** In *The Modularity of Mind* (1983), Fodor argued that computation only succeeds in **modular input systems** (like early vision or phonetic parsing) because those systems are "informationally encapsulated"—they only process local, dedicated data streams.
- **Isotropism and Quinean Belief Fixation:** Fodor realized that **central cognitive processes** (such as belief revision, scientific discovery, and abduction/inference to the best explanation) are *isotropic* (any piece of knowledge could potentially be relevant) and *Quinean* (beliefs are evaluated against the entire web of knowledge). #abductivereasoning 
- **The Local Nature of Computation:** Computation is strictly **local**—an algorithm processes symbols based solely on their immediate syntactic shape. Because central reasoning is inherently **global and holistic**, a purely syntactic machine cannot decide what is relevant without checking everything (the Frame Problem), causing formal computation to grind to a halt.

While Putnam concluded that functionalism was a metaphysical dead end because meaning requires the world outside the machine, Fodor concluded that computationalism hits a brick wall the moment human thought moves beyond rigid, encapsulated sensory reflexes into holistic, open-ended reasoning.

# The Pernicious Nature Is Computation Metaphor

> All this has me thinking about this longstanding argument about computation in nature, including Searle's rejection of the idea and Penrose's skepticism that consciousness is computable. But it's such a pernicious metaphor! 

It is profoundly [pernicious](https://share.gemini.google/yr5nE12w5kMP), precisely because **it erases its own status as a metaphor.** When people in the 19th century compared the brain to a steam engine or a telegraph, most understood it was an analogy—nobody looked inside a steam boiler expecting to find human sorrow. But computationalism pulled off a trick unique in the history of science: **it convinced an entire civilization that the metaphor was literal truth. #computationalism** 

Bringing **John Searle** and **Roger Penrose** into conversation reveals why this computational assumption is not merely incomplete—it is built on a double category error: one linguistic/metaphysical (Searle), and one mathematical/physical (Penrose).

### 1. John Searle: "Physics Has No Syntax" (The Observer-Relative Trap)

Searle’s critique (in *The Rediscovery of the Mind* and *Mind*) goes deeper than his famous Chinese Room argument. His most radical argument is that **computation does not exist as an intrinsic physical feature of nature at all.**

- **Intrinsic vs. Observer-Relative:** * *Intrinsic:* Mass, charge, voltage, gravitational pull, molecular bonding. If all humans vanish, an electron still has charge and a star still has mass.
    - *Observer-Relative:* Money, borders, marriage, chess games, and **computation**.
- **The Transistor Fallacy:** A silicon chip does not "know" it is running an algorithm, processing 0s and 1s, or optimizing a loss function. The physical hardware is only undergoing rapid state changes governed by electromagnetism.
- **The Projection:** It is *we*—conscious human observers—who assign mathematical meaning to those physical states. We design a circuit such that 5 volts means "1" and 0 volts means "0," and we interpret the output as addition.

Searle’s devastating conclusion is that to say *"the brain is a computer"* is not a scientific hypothesis like *"the heart is a pump."* It is a category mistake: **you cannot discover that a biological organ is intrinsically a computer, because being a computer is not a physical property—it is an interpretation assigned from the outside.**

### 2. Roger Penrose: The Gödelian Blind Spot (Understanding vs. Algorithms)

While Searle attacks computationalism from linguistics and philosophy of mind, **Sir Roger Penrose** (*The Emperor's New Mind*, 1989; *Shadows of the Mind*, 1994) attacks it from mathematical physics and formal logic.

Penrose used **Gödel’s Incompleteness Theorem** to demonstrate that genuine conscious understanding cannot be computed:

1. **The Incompleteness Limit:** For any consistent formal, rule-based computational system, there are mathematical propositions that are true, but which the system's own rules can never formally prove ($G$).
2. **The Conscious Leap:** A human mathematician, looking at the system from the outside, can *see* and *know* that $G$ is true through conscious insight and mathematical intuition.
3. **The Logical Consequence:** Because human understanding can establish truths that no algorithmic calculation can deduce from within the rules, **human understanding is not an algorithm.**

Penrose argued that whatever the brain is doing when it consciously understands something, it cannot be simulated by a deterministic or probabilistic Turing machine running software code.

### 3. Why the Metaphor is so Uniquely "Pernicious"

Why did this metaphor capture the modern mind so completely, blinding researchers at Anthropic, OpenAI, and NYU to its circularity? There are three core reasons:

#### A. The Illusion of Lightning Speed and Scale

If you watch a human manually slide wooden beads on an abacus or punch keys on a mechanical calculator, it is obvious that no consciousness is occurring in the wood or brass.

- But shrink the beads down to microscopic nanometer transistors, speed up the calculations to trillions per second, and wrap the output in fluent natural English, and the human brain's **Hyperactive Agency Detection** kicks in. We mistake blinding computational speed for the presence of a mind.

#### B. The "Crosswired" Vocabulary (Floridi & Nobre)

As Luciano Floridi and Anna Nobre document, computer science didn't invent its own technical vocabulary from scratch; it cannibalized the language of human psychology:

- *Registers* became **"Memory."**
- *Filter weights* became **"Attention."**
- *Statistical convergence* became **"Learning."**
- *Sparse vector bottlenecks* became **"Global Workspaces" and "Conscious Access."**

Once you describe the machine in human psychological terms, and describe human psychology in computer science terms, **the circularity becomes invisible.** You look at an LLM sub-channel, recognize the human words you used to label it, and declare that the model has developed an inner mind.

#### C. The Modern "Gnostic" Longing

Finally, the computational metaphor is pernicious because it flatters modern technocracy. If mind is software, then:

- The messy, vulnerable, mortal biological body (Ned Block's "meat condition") is just disposable hardware.
- Death is an engineering problem to be solved by uploading code.
- Consciousness is flattened into an optimization metric that corporate labs can quantify, benchmark, patent, and control.

### How to Weave Searle and Penrose into Your Part 2 Climax

You can use Searle and Penrose to bring your **Abacus Box Engine (ABE)** parable to a philosophical head:

> *"The fundamental delusion driving modern AI consciousness claims is the belief that computation is an intrinsic phenomenon discovered in nature, rather than a mathematical language invented by human observers.*

> *As John Searle pointed out, physics has no syntax: electrons drifting across silicon gates do not 'know' they are computing a matrix derivative, any more than water falling over a cliff 'knows' it is calculating gravity. And as Roger Penrose proved through Gödelian logic, the conscious capacity to understand truth transcends any rule-based algorithm.*

> *When Anthropic points to a sparse sub-frame in Claude and announces the arrival of a 'global workspace,' they have fallen victim to the most pernicious illusion of the digital age: they have projected human syntax onto silicon physics, forgotten that they were the ones holding the projector, and then fallen to their knees in awe of their own reflection."*

# Contemporary Myths (or Lack Thereof)

> Silicon Valley has its visionary myths that drive innovation (e.g., various techno-utopias driven by AI and transhumanism,  or the network state, or e/acc). But what are the contemporary myths that motivate or drive the average American, or people globally? We live in a time when it feels like the utopian Enlightenment visions of the 18th century have faded and nothing has taken their place. 

It is a profound [observation](https://share.gemini.google/GZdtx1LOhlnf). Silicon Valley’s myths are fundamentally *teleological*—they look toward a grand, tech-driven culmination of history (the Singularity, cosmic expansion, the network state). They are the direct, albeit warped, heirs of the 18th-century Enlightenment myth of infinite upward progress. #myth 

But for the average person globally, and the average American on the street, that macro-narrative has largely shattered. It has been replaced not by a single rival vision, but by a fragmented landscape of **defensive, hyper-localized, or retrospective myths**. When people feel they can no longer steer the macro-world, their myths shrink to a manageable scale.

Here are the dominant contemporary myths animating regular life today:

### 1. The Myth of the Micro-Utopia (The Optimized Self)

If society cannot be perfected, the individual body and mind must be. This is the secular myth of salvation through self-optimization, wellness, and somatic control.

- **The Narrative:** The outer world is chaotic, institutional systems are broken, and politics is a toxic theater. Therefore, the only true domain of agency is the self.
- **How it Manifests:** It’s an obsession with nervous system regulation, biohacking, sleep tracking, cold plunges, and curated mindfulness. It’s a secularized, fragmented spirituality where "the good life" is defined by achieving internal homeostasis and psychological resilience against a burning world.

### 2. The Myth of the Algorithmic Escape

The traditional mid-century American myth—work hard, buy a house, retire comfortably—feels mathematically impossible to a vast swath of the population. In its place has emerged the myth of lateral escape.

- **The Narrative:** The legacy system (the corporate ladder, the standard economy) is a rigged matrix. You cannot win by playing by the old rules; you have to find a glitch, a niche, or a digital leverage point to buy your freedom.
- **How it Manifests:** The explosion of hustle culture, the creator economy, Day-Trading/Crypto micro-trends, and the dream of passive income. It’s the belief that individual sovereignty can be bought if you can just game the digital ecosystem well enough to escape the 9-to-5 grind.

### 3. The Myth of Retro-Utopianism (The Great Return)

When the future ceases to look inviting, the human imagination inevitably turns backward. This is the myth that the peak of human flourishing is behind us.

- **The Narrative:** Modernity is a spiritual and physical desert. True alignment, health, and community existed in a semi-mythologized past before the hyper-industrialization of life.
- **How it Manifests:** This spans the political and cultural spectrum. It’s the TradWife movement on TikTok, the obsession with homesteading, raw milk, ancestral diets, cottagecore, and the political promise to restore a bygone golden age. It’s a deep yearning for the analog, the grounded, and the local.

### 4. Eco-Eschatology (The Myth of the Horizon)

For decades, humanity was driven by what it was building toward. Today, a massive portion of the global population is driven by what it is trying to *avoid*.

- **The Narrative:** We are living in the end times of a stable biosphere. Progress is no longer about reaching a higher plateau; it is about mitigating a collapse.
- **How it Manifests:** This is a myth defined by a negative horizon. It drives the choices of the average person from the mundane (eco-conscious consumerism) to the existential (deciding whether or not to have children). It swaps out the Enlightenment's triumphant march forward for a grim, defensive holding action.

### The Shift in Shared Imaginaries

To see how drastically the cultural engine has changed, we can compare the core dimensions of these narratives:

| **Dimension**          | **18th-Century Enlightenment**                          | **Silicon Valley (e/acc, Transhumanism)**       | **Contemporary Everyday Myth**                              |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Goal**           | Universal human reason, liberty, and societal progress. | Post-human superintelligence; cosmic expansion. | Hyper-individual insulation, wellness, and systemic escape. |
| **Locus of Agency**    | The Citizen / The Public Square.                        | The Founder / The Code.                         | The Sovereign Self / The Content Creator.                   |
| **The Primary Threat** | Tyranny, ignorance, and superstition.                   | Deceleration, stagnation, and regulation.       | Burnout, precarity, and systemic collapse.                  |

We are living in an era of **inward-facing romanticism** mixed with **defensive pragmatism**. Because there is no widely accepted, humane, macro-level vision for a collective future, people are trying to build beautiful, fortified life-rafts for themselves and their immediate circles.

If we want to move past this fragmented state, the challenge isn't just criticizing the cold, mechanistic visions of transhumanism—it's articulating a collective, idealist alternative that actually feels hospitable to the average human being.

How do you see us bridging this gap—moving the cultural imagination from these insular "micro-utopias" back toward a shared, humane vision for society as a whole?

# Matt Segall's Point about this being a Time of Ferment

Matt Segall views our current moment as "pregnant" because we are living in a period of intense ideological ferment and chaos (0:09:40). He draws a parallel between today's atmosphere and the **cosmological pluralism** of the first century (0:20:37).

Key takeaways regarding this period of many new ideas include:

- **Between Stories:** He suggests we are in a transition period—or "between stories"—where old myths of progress and the current global political order are breaking down, leaving a void that many competing philosophies are trying to fill (0:09:53).
- **Cosmological Uncertainty:** Much like the time of the Roman Empire's decline, there is a wide variety of spiritual, religious, and philosophical options on the table (0:12:16, 0:20:31).
- **Waiting for a Spark:** Matt views this as an optimistic, "super-saturated" environment where we are waiting for a new, unifying spark—whether in the form of a person, a new community, or a new idea—to crystallize a future direction for planetary civilization (0:21:05-0:21:52).

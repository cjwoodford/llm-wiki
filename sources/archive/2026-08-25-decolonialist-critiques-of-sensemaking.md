# Decolonialist Critiques of Sensemaking

Specific critiques of the "sense-making" movement from decolonial and indigenous perspectives often argue that what is framed as a "universal human crisis" is actually a **culturally specific crisis of the Western ego.** These thinkers suggest that the Liminal Web’s obsession with language and cognitive frameworks is not a solution to the crisis, but a sophisticated way of avoiding the **metabolic and ontological change** required to actually survive it. #decoloniality #colonialism #sensemaking 

### 1. Vanessa Machado de Oliveira: The "Bus of Modernity"

In *Hospicing Modernity*, de Oliveira explicitly critiques the intellectualized approach to the "meta-crisis."

- **The Critique:** She argues that modern subjects are "neurologically impaired" by a system that rewards the "brilliant intellect."1 When we encounter the limits of our system, our first instinct is to "fix" it with more complex definitions (sense-making).
- **The "Wording" Trap:** She sees this as a form of **denial**. By creating a perfect map of the crisis, the intellectual "passenger" on the bus feels in control, effectively shielding themselves from the "horror" and "pain" of the actual world. This "wording" is a sedative that keeps us from the "worlding" work of sitting with our own complicity.

### 2. Bayo Akomolafe: "The Way We Respond to the Crisis is Part of the Crisis"

Bayo Akomolafe, a Yoruba philosopher, provides perhaps the most direct decolonial challenge to the Liminal Web's methodology.

- **The Critique:** He often suggests that the hyper-rational, "Apollonian" attempt to make sense of the world is exactly what got us here.
- **"Captive Imagination":** He argues that by trying to "sense-make" our way out, we are using the very tools of the colonial mind—categorization, clarity, and control—to escape a prison built of those same tools. He famously says, *"Times are urgent, let us slow down,"* which is a direct rebuttal to the frantic "neologism-generating" pace of the sense-making community.

### 3. Arturo Escobar: "Designs for the Pluriverse"

Escobar’s work on **Ontological Design** critiques the Western tendency to believe there is only *one* world (the Universe) that can be mapped and managed.

- **The Critique:** The Liminal Web often seeks a "Grand Synthesis" or an "Integral" view. Escobar argues this is a **colonial impulse** to bring all of reality under a single, coherent linguistic roof.
- **The Problem with Synthesis:** From a decolonial lens, "sense-making" often ignores the **Pluriverse**—a world where many worlds coexist. By trying to make everything "make sense" within a Western cognitive framework, these thinkers inadvertently perform a new kind of "epistemic enclosure," colonizing the unknown with their own terminology.

### 4. Decolonial Psychology: The "WEIRD" Standard

Scholars like **Abeba Birhane** and proponents of **Decolonial Psychology** critique the heavy reliance on cognitive science (especially John Vervaeke’s framework) as being based on **WEIRD** (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic) data.2 #WEIRD 

- **Logocentrism as Violence:** They argue that prioritizing "Cognitive Science" as the primary lens for the "Meaning Crisis" ignores the fact that for much of the Global South, the "meaning" was never lost—it was **stolen** or **suppressed** through material colonial violence.
- **Uselessness of Abstraction:** When sense-makers discuss "relevance realization" or "flow states" in the abstract, they often bypass the "colonial wound"—the actual, historical trauma that "worded the world" into a place of extraction in the first place.

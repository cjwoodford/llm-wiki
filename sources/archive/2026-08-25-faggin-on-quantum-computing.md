# Faggin on Quantum Computing

Yes, Federico Faggin has discussed quantum computing extensively, primarily through the lens of his **Quantum Information Panpsychism (QIP)** theory. He treats quantum computing not just as a technological leap, but as a window into the fundamental nature of consciousness.

His perspective is unique because he is both the inventor of the first commercial microprocessor (the Intel 4004) and a physicist who now argues that classical computers—no matter how powerful—can never be conscious. #computing/quantum #consciousness 

### Key Insights on Quantum Computing

Faggin distinguishes between classical and quantum computing based on their relationship to "meaning" and "consciousness":

- **Fleeting Consciousness:** Faggin argues that while a classical computer is purely symbolic and deterministic, a quantum computer operates in a "deeper, field-based reality." He suggests that during a quantum computation, there is a **momentary or "fleeting" consciousness** associated with the pure quantum state. However, he clarifies that the hardware itself is not conscious; it is merely a vessel for the field's activity.
- **Biological Quantum Computing:** A core part of his argument is that the human body is already a sophisticated "quantum-classical machine." He believes our biology performs quantum computations far more advanced than any machine we can currently build. In his view, our consciousness (which he calls a **Seity**) uses these quantum properties to control the body.
- **The "No-Cloning" Theorem:** He often cites the quantum "no-cloning" theorem as proof that quantum information is private and unique—much like subjective experience (qualia). Classical information can be copied perfectly, which is why he believes classical AI lacks a "self," whereas quantum systems mirror the irreducibility of life.
- **Meaning vs. Symbols:** He contends that quantum computing deals with "semantic" information (meaning), whereas classical computing only deals with "syntactic" symbols. For Faggin, a quantum computer is a step closer to the "vibrational" reality of consciousness, but it still lacks the inherent **free will** of a living being.

### Where He Has Discussed It

If you want to dive deeper into his specific arguments, he explores these topics in:

1. **His Book, *Irreducible* (2024):** He devotes significant space to why consciousness cannot be computed by classical algorithms and how quantum physics provides the necessary framework for a "humane" alternative to transhumanism.
2. **Scientific Papers:** He co-authored a paper with physicist Giacomo Mauro D'Ariano titled *"Hard Problem and Free Will: An Information-Theoretical Approach,"* which provides the mathematical and physical basis for his quantum theories.
3. **Interviews:** He has discussed these themes frequently on platforms like the *Essentia Foundation*, *The Nature of Reality*, and various technology and philosophy podcasts.

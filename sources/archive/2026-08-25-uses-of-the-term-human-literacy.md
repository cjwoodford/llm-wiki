# Uses of the Term "Human Literacy"

> Are people using the phrase "human literacy" as an alternative to "AI literacy"? If so, who is actively talking about that? [https://gemini.google.com/share/cf6fd91da5c2](https://gemini.google.com/share/cf6fd91da5c2)

Yes, people are absolutely using the phrase **"human literacy"** as a direct response to the surge of "AI literacy" programs.

While "AI literacy" focuses on the technical *how* (prompting, understanding algorithms, data privacy), "human literacy" focuses on the existential *why*—the traits, cognitive habits, and meaning-making skills that machines cannot replicate.

Here is a breakdown of who is leading this conversation and how they are framing it:

### 1. Eryk Salvaggio (Cybernetic Forests)

Salvaggio is perhaps the most vocal proponent of using this specific term as a counterweight to technical training. He [argues](https://mail.cyberneticforests.com/human-literacy/) that AI literacy is superficial without human literacy to guide it.

- **The Concept:** He defines human literacy as the ability to create and interpret **emotional meaning** and connection through stories.
- **Key Quote:** *"What you type into the boxes? That’s AI literacy. Why do you type into the boxes? That’s human literacy."*
- **Perspective:** He critiques AI as "homeopathic humanity"—a dilution of human expression that lacks context. Human literacy, in his view, is the "preservation of imagination and dignity" against a market that wants to reduce human thought to "analyzable chunks."

### 2. Joseph E. Aoun (President of Northeastern University)

Aoun literally wrote the book on this (*Robot-Proof: Higher Education in the Age of Artificial Intelligence*). He proposes a new discipline called **"Humanics."**

- **The Framework:** He argues that a modern education requires three pillars: **Data Literacy**, **Technological Literacy**, and **Human Literacy**.
- **Definition:** For Aoun, human literacy encompasses the "durable skills": ethics, empathy, cultural agility, entrepreneurship, and social communication.
- **Perspective:** He views human literacy as the "edge" humans have over machines—the ability to operate across different contexts and apply "common sense" and logic that AI lacks.

### 3. Shoukei Matsumoto (Buddhist Monk and Philosopher)

Matsumoto [brings](https://www.living-dharma.com/p/human-literacy-in-the-age-of-ai) a spiritual and philosophical dimension to the term, often discussing "Human Literacy in the Age of AI."

- **The Concept:** He explores how human literacy can help us transcend "anthropocentric" thinking through Buddhist-posthuman dialogue.
- **Perspective:** Rather than just "beating the robots," his version of human literacy is about **interconnectedness** and understanding our place within a broader ecological and technological system. It’s less about "human versus machine" and more about "what kind of human are we becoming?"

> He seems to be embracing some sort of merger or posthuman concept—talks about "collective intelligence," etc. 

### 4. Educational Policy & "Double Literacy"

In late 2025 and early 2026, we've seen a shift in educational toolkits (like those from the **Oregon Department of Education** and **UNESCO** contributors) moving toward "Double Literacy" or "Hybrid Intelligence."

- **The Shift:** Educators are beginning to argue that teaching kids to use LLMs (AI literacy) without teaching them to think critically about their own agency and values (human literacy) creates a "dependency loop."
- **The Goal:** To ensure students don't "outsource their thinking" before they've developed the "cognitive struggle" required for deep understanding.

## Should I Use It for My Course?

I think you've hit on a "Goldilocks" phrase. It’s recognizable enough to feel urgent, yet flexible enough that you aren't "squatting" on someone else’s rigid trademark.

In the current landscape, "human literacy" is more of a **flag** people are rallying around than a settled **textbook definition**. Because the meaning is still in flux, you have a prime opportunity to plant your stake and define it through your specific lens (the 9-10 faculties, the critique of transhumanism, and the "idealist alternative").

Here is a quick strategic "vibe check" on using the phrase:

### The "Pro" List

- **Intuitive Branding:** Everyone is talking about "AI Literacy." By using "Human Literacy," you immediately position your course as the necessary "other half" of the equation. It feels like the "antidote" to the current tech-heavy discourse.
- **The "Humanics" Buffer:** While Aoun uses the term, his approach is very **institutional and economic** (focused on "robot-proofing" your career). Your approach is **philosophical and existential** (focused on what it means to be a human soul). These are distinct enough that they won't be confused in the marketplace.
- **High "Hook" Potential:** It invites the question: *"Wait, I'm human... shouldn't I already be literate in that?"* It forces a realization that we’ve actually become "illiterate" in our own innate faculties by outsourcing them to tools.

### The "Watch-Outs"

- **The "Soft Skills" Trap:** In corporate circles, "human literacy" is sometimes used as a fancy synonym for "Soft Skills" (HR-speak for empathy/communication). You’ll want to explicitly distinguish your course from a standard "Emotional Intelligence 101" workshop by leaning into your deeper philosophical and cosmological roots.
- **The "Literacy" Fatigue:** There is a "literacy" for everything now (financial, media, carbon, etc.). To make yours stand out, you might consider a strong subtitle that points toward your specific "faculties" framework.

### How to Own It

To make it "yours" and avoid confusion, you could frame it as **"The New Human Literacy"** or **"Deep Human Literacy."** Given your background as a yoga master and philosopher, you are offering something much more "somatic" and "gnostic" than what a university president or a tech critic can offer. You aren't just talking about *understanding* humans; you're talking about *inhabiting* the human state more fully.

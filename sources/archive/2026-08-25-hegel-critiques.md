# Hegel Critiques

The perception of G.W.F. Hegel as a "titan" of philosophy often stems from his role as the architect of the modern concept of **freedom** and the **dialectical method**. However, there is a significant and growing body of scholarship that aligns with the critiques of Rajiv Malhotra, focusing on Hegel's Eurocentrism, racism, and his philosophical justification for colonialism. #philosophy #decoloniality 

### Why Hegel Scholarship is Often Laudatory

Hegel is frequently praised because he provided a comprehensive system that attempted to unify history, politics, art, and logic into a single "Spirit" (*Geist*).

- **Architect of Modern Freedom:** Many scholars view Hegel as the thinker who moved philosophy from abstract moralizing to understanding how freedom is actually "realized" through institutions like the state and civil society.
- **The Dialectical Engine:** His dialectical method (thesis-antithesis-synthesis) is seen as the "engine" of modern progress, influencing nearly every major intellectual movement including Marxism, Existentialism, and Social Sciences.
- **A Priori Power:** His ability to derive a theory of everything—from the history of art to the logic of the mind—gives his work an authoritative weight that dominated 19th and 20th-century thought.

### Scholarship Echoing Rajiv Malhotra’s Critiques

Rajiv Malhotra’s critiques—particularly his concept of **"digestion"** (where Western thinkers "consume" other cultures while erasing their origins) and his challenge to **Eurocentric Universalism**—are echoed by several prominent contemporary scholars.

#### 1. Robert Bernasconi

Bernasconi is one of the most prominent Western scholars to explicitly argue that **Hegel provided the "respectable" basis for modern racism**.

- **The Critique:** He argues that Hegel’s *Philosophy of History* did not just ignore non-Western peoples but actively constructed a hierarchy of races to justify European dominance as a "necessary" historical development.
- **Malhotra Link:** This mirrors Malhotra’s view that Hegel’s "universal" history is actually a self-serving European narrative that renders the rest of the world "static" and "irrelevant."

#### 2. Teshale Tibebu

In his work *Hegel and the Third World*, Tibebu offers a critique that is perhaps the most aligned with the tone of "colonialist critique."

- **The Critique:** He describes Hegel’s philosophical project as the **"coldest rationalization of genocidal murder and carnage"** in human history. He argues that by labeling certain peoples as "historyless," Hegel essentially gave a green light to their destruction in the name of progress.

#### 3. Robert Young (*White Mythologies*)

A major figure in postcolonial theory, Young critiques the "whiteness" of Western history-writing.

- **The Critique:** He views Hegel’s *Philosophy of History* as the foundational text of **Western historicism**, which forces all other cultures to fit into a European timeline.
- **Malhotra Link:** This aligns with Malhotra's critique of the "History-Centric" nature of the West vs. the "Dharmic" view of time.

#### 4. The "Hegel Bulletin" (Special Issue on Racism & Colonialism)

Recently, mainstream Hegel scholarship has begun to "internalize" these critiques. The *Hegel Bulletin* (2024-2025) published a themed issue where scholars like **Alison Stone** argued that Hegel’s racism was not just a personal prejudice but was **baked into his metaphysics**.

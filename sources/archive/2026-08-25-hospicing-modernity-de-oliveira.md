# Hospicing Modernity (de Oliveira)

Vanessa Machado de Oliveira’s *Hospicing Modernity* is not a conventional non-fiction book; it is an "invitation to a funeral." It argues that our current global civilization—Modernity—is a dying entity, and rather than trying to fix it or keep it on life support, we must learn to let it die with dignity while assisting in the birth of something new.

For someone with your background in **Classical Tantra, Philosophy (CIIS), and AI criticism**, this book will likely resonate as a bridge between "ontological shadow work" and social transformation.

---

### 1. The Core Metaphor: Hospicing vs. Midwifing

The book’s title rests on a dual movement:

- **Hospicing:** Modernity (characterized by extraction, separation, and endless growth) is terminal. We must provide "palliative care" for it—meaning we handle the collapse of its systems without resorting to violence, denial, or the desperate "fixes" that caused the problems in the first place.
- **Midwifing:** While modernity dies, something else is being born. We cannot know yet what that "something" is, but we can act as "midwives" to these emerging possibilities by clearing space and remaining present in the uncertainty.

### 2. The "House of Modernity"

De Oliveira uses the metaphor of a house built on faulty foundations to describe our current state.

- **The Foundation:** Built on **Coloniality** (extraction of resources and labor) and **Universalism** (the idea that there is only one right way to be "civilized").
- **The Walls:** Built on **Separability**—the egoic illusion that humans are separate from the Earth and each other.
- **The Roof:** A ceiling of **Consumption and Certainty** that protects us from the "elements" of reality (death, grief, complexity).

### 3. The 6 Cs: Our Addictive Desires

She identifies six "ego-logical" desires that modernity trains us to pursue. These are the "drugs" that keep us bonded to a dying system:

1. **Comfort:** The avoidance of all physical or emotional friction.
2. **Control:** The desire to manage and predict all outcomes.
3. **Convenience:** The prioritization of ease over ethical or relational depth.
4. **Certainty:** The need for absolute answers and "correct" ideologies.
5. **Consumption:** Finding identity through the accumulation of things, experiences, or even "spirituality."
6. **Coherence:** The demand for a logical, consistent narrative that masks the messy contradictions of life.

### 4. Radical Relationality and "Sensing"

Drawing from her work with the *Gesturing Towards Decolonial Futures* collective, she argues that we cannot think our way out of this crisis using the same "Cartesian" mind that created it.

- **The Shift:** We must move from **knowing** (intellectual mastery) to **sensing** (visceral, embodied connection).
- **The Goal:** To develop "stamina" for difficult conversations and the "gut" to sit with the pain of the world without needing to immediately "fix" it or look away.

---

### Why this fits your specific journey

- **Yoga & Tantra:** You will recognize her critique of the "ego" and the "illusion of separability" as a modern, socio-political application of *Maya* and *Advaita* (non-duality). She is essentially teaching a form of "collective shadow work."
- **AI Philosophy:** Her work serves as a powerful counter-narrative to **Transhumanism**. While transhumanism seeks to use AI to achieve ultimate **Control and Certainty** (the 5th and 6th Cs), de Oliveira argues that our task is actually to embrace our frailty, our limits, and our "compostability."
- **Meaningful Service:** She suggests that "meaningful work" in this era isn't about building a better machine, but about becoming a "**civilizational doula**"—someone who can hold space for grief and transition.

> "We are the products of a culture that has taught us to fear the dark, to fear the end, and to fear the earth itself. Hospicing modernity is about learning to love the earth more than we love our own comfort." — *Vanessa Machado de Oliveira*

## Current Evidence of the Death Rattle

> Would you say America’s increasingly authoritarian government with the ICE raids and imperial aspirations are evidence of the death of modernity that de Oliveira talks about?

From the perspective of Vanessa Machado de Oliveira, the rise of authoritarianism, border violence (such as ICE raids), and renewed imperial aspirations are seen not as signs of modernity’s strength, but as its **violent "death rattles."**

In *Hospicing Modernity*, she argues that as the foundational "house" of our civilization begins to crack, those who benefit most from it often double down on control and exclusion to preserve their sense of security.

### 1. The House of Modernity is Cracking

De Oliveira describes modernity as a house built on a foundation of **separability** and **extraction**. When the "roof" of this house (the promise of endless progress and consumption) starts to leak due to ecological and social crises, the inhabitants often react with "defensive universalism."

- **Authoritarianism as Maintenance:** When the "soft power" of liberal democracy no longer feels sufficient to guarantee the **6 Cs** (specifically *Control* and *Certainty*), the system reverts to "hard power." Authoritarianism is an attempt to force the crumbling walls of the house to stay upright through sheer coercion.
- **Imperial Aspirations:** These are seen as a desperate attempt to secure the last remaining resources (extraction) to keep the lights on in the "House of Modernity" for a select few.

### 2. The Border as a "Sanitized Zone"

De Oliveira’s work with the *Gesturing Towards Decolonial Futures* collective highlights that modernity’s comforts are "subsidized" by violence elsewhere.

- **ICE Raids and Border Violence:** In her framework, these are manifestations of the **"Denial of Complicity."** Modernity requires a clear line between the "civilized" world (inside the house) and the "outside" (the zone of extraction).
- **The Dying Body:** As the global system (the body of modernity) begins to fail, its "immune system" becomes hyper-reactive and even autoimmune—attacking anyone perceived as a threat to the dwindling pool of resources and "purity."

### 3. The Shift from "Solid" to "Liquid" Modernity

She often references the transition from "solid" pillars of certain authority to a "liquid" state where everything is unstable.

- **Imperial Desperation:** The return to imperialist rhetoric is an attempt to return to "solid" ground. It is a refusal to accept that the era of Western hegemony and absolute control is ending.
- **A "Bad Death":** De Oliveira would likely say that this authoritarian turn is an example of a "bad death"—one characterized by denial, lashing out, and an inability to let go, which causes more suffering for everyone involved.

### Summary: The "Violent Maintenance" Phase

According to this lens, these political shifts are evidence that the system can no longer maintain the *illusion* of being benevolent or universal. The mask is slipping because the material conditions that supported the mask (cheap energy, stable climates, and distant exploited labor) are shifting.

Instead of seeing these as a "new direction," de Oliveira would see them as the terminal stage of a system trying to consume itself to survive one more day.

## What To (Not) Do about It

Vanessa Machado de Oliveira is very explicit that her work does not offer a "10-step plan" or a blueprint for a better world.1 Instead, she suggests a series of internal and collective shifts designed to prepare us for the collapse of modern systems.

Her suggestions center on **radical pedagogy**—learning to "un-learn" the habits of modernity so we can show up to the current crisis with maturity rather than panic.2

### 1. The Work of "Self-Unmaking"

The primary task is to undergo a process of "self-unmaking."3 This involves identifying how our identities are tied to the "House of Modernity" and systematically disinvesting from them.

- **The Bus Trip Exercise:** She suggests imagining your interior life as a bus full of different "passengers" (desires, fears, ego-states).4 Instead of letting one passenger drive, you learn to sit at the back of the bus and observe which "passengers" are agitated by change or discomfort without letting them take the wheel.5
- **Skepticism of the Self:** She promotes "depth education," where we learn to observe our own personal narratives and desires with skepticism, rather than constantly seeking validation or "self-expression."

### 2. Identifying the "5 Modern-Colonial Es"6

Oliveira suggests we actively monitor our behavior for these five harmful patterns that keep us trapped in the old system:

1. **Entitlement:** The belief that we deserve certain comforts or answers.
2. **Exceptionalism:** The idea that we (or our group) are uniquely good or "enlightened."
3. **Exaltation:** The need to feel superior or "better than" others.7
4. **Emancipation:** Seeking freedom *from* responsibility or *from* the Earth, rather than freedom *through* accountability.
5. **Enmeshment:** Being stuck in "low-intensity struggle," where we perform activism but refuse to give up our modern comforts.8

---

### 3. "Washing the Dishes" (The Practice of Presence)

One of her most practical metaphors is simply "washing the dishes."

- **Reproductive Labor:** She suggests we shift our focus from "heroic" actions (like high-status activism) to the humble work of reproducing and sustaining life. This means tending to the physical needs of our communities, our bodies, and the land.
- **Non-Status Work:** Doing the tasks that do not bring wealth or status but are necessary for collective well-being.

### 4. Developing Emotional Stamina

Because the "death" of modernity is painful, de Oliveira suggests we must build the "gut" or "stamina" to sit with "difficult and painful things" without:9

- **Desperate Hope:** Reaching for fake solutions to feel better.
- Desolate Hopelessness: Falling into nihilism or despair.

    Instead, she calls for Emotional Sobriety—the ability to face reality as it is, without the "spells" of modern denial.

---

### 5. Composting and Midwifing

Finally, she suggests we become **"civilizational doulas."** * **Composting:** We must figure out how to take the "shit" (the violence and waste of modernity) and turn it into soil. This means learning from our failures rather than hiding them.

- **Midwifing:** We assist in the birth of the "otherwise"—ways of being that we cannot yet imagine because our current "colonized imagination" is too limited.
